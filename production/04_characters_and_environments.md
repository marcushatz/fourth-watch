# 04 · Character and environment consistency

Every visual must look like one production. Consistency comes from approved references, reused every time.

## The order

1. Marcus approves a look (`README.md`, the look gate).
2. Design each recurring character, set and prop as a reference.
3. Marcus approves each reference.
4. Register it in `ledger/assets.csv` and as a Higgsfield Element where the model supports it.
5. Generate shots from those references.

## Reference pack for each recurring character

- A full-body reference on a plain background, in the approved look.
- A short written description, reused word for word in prompts.
- Robe colour and shape, head covering, beard, proportions, one identifying prop.

## Character rules

These apply in any approved look:

- **Adult proportions.** About 6.5–7.5 heads tall, with small heads and long lines.
- **Silhouette first.** Identity must read from shape, posture and gesture.
- **Faces:** restrained and shaped by light. Never dot eyes, big eyes or cartoon features.
- **Hands and limbs:** real structure, no tube arms or mitten hands.
- **Crowds:** varied heights, ages, postures and groupings. Never rows of one shape.
- **Acting:** clear, deliberate gestures that tell the story.
- **Clothing:** plain ancient Near Eastern dress, with no modern or fantasy details.

The older "dot eyes on medium shots" rule is retired because of Marcus's Nov 2 note that the characters look childish. His earlier ideas of minimal faces and identity through silhouette stay.

## Environment rules

- **Depth:** foreground, subject, middle ground, far ground and sky.
- **One key light,** motivated by the story.
- **One colour family per scene,** plus one accent.
- **Period detail:** low goat-hair tents, stone, scrub, dust. Nothing decorative that competes with the idea.
- **Reuse:** one set per story run, seen from different positions. Change light, time or framing, not the location.
- **Repeated framings:** a framing that returns with one change can carry the argument.
