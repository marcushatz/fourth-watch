# 01 · Brand identity and visual references

## Who we are

Fourth Watch is a faceless Christian educational channel. Each episode reveals a surprising, textually supported connection within Scripture.

**We make:** intelligent, reverent, clear and visually memorable explanations.

**We don't make:** generic devotional content, shallow retellings, invented biblical details, exaggerated theological claims or generic automated cartoon videos.

**The standard (Marcus, Nov 1):** every visual deepens understanding, raises curiosity, clarifies a connection, or heightens the emotional impact of a revelation.

## Visual identity (Marcus, Nov 3)

Fourth Watch has a recognizable identity, not a single appearance. Every film should feel deliberately art-directed by the same hand. This section is a draft until Marcus approves it from proof frames.

**What makes a frame ours:**

- **A hand-made surface.** Edges wander slightly and pigment has grain, in every medium, so 3D, painting and type share one world.
- **Light with meaning.** Frames are mostly dark and warm, with one motivated light that leads the eye.
- **One image that carries the argument.** The film keeps returning to it, and each return means more.
- **Deliberate composition.** One idea in a frame, strong silhouettes, real negative space, and a reason for where everything sits.
- **Restraint.** Nothing moves, flashes or shakes without earning it.
- **Reverence for the words.** Scripture appears exactly, and the type can become the image.

**What can change from film to film:** the mix of media, the style of a world, the colour story inside the palette, the characters, the pacing shape and the transitions.

**Also rejected and still binding:** black rim-lit cut-out figures, an all-black silhouette Jesus, flat vector icon marks and anything that looks automatically assembled.

**A visual brief comes before every storyboard.** It's one page, written like a director's note, and answers:

1. What is the film's one image?
2. What will this film do that no other episode has done?
3. Which object or motif returns, and how does it change?
4. What is the colour story?
5. Where does the visual language change, and what does each change mean?
6. What is the boldest shot?
7. What will this film refuse to do?

The Bronze Serpent example is `ep02-bronze-serpent/visual_brief.md`.

## How to use references

- **Study quality, never copy artwork.** Reference reels teach structure, pacing and clarity. Our look stays our own.
- **What to study:** one idea per image, a type layer on screen throughout, repeated framings, match cuts on objects. The measured notes are in `research/visual_techniques.md`.
- Never put other creators' frames or video in this repo.
- Never name a living artist in a prompt. Describe the craft instead.
- A prompt can name an old public-domain tradition, such as fresco or wood engraving.
