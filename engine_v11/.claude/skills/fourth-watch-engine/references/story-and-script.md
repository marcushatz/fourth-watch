# Creative story engine

## First decide the promise
A compelling question should be felt before explained, consequential if answered, honestly resolvable or explicitly bounded, and connected to the story's emotional events. A passage oddity alone is not a promise. "How did Peter get back to the boat?" is a weak promise because Scripture doesn't give mechanics; the word "they" is not a meaningful climax. "Why did Jesus wait?" can create tension but no evangelist explicitly states the motive. If used, narration must acknowledge the gap without cheating. "Why did Jesus mean to pass by?" raises a debated reading (Mark 6:48), not a certain answer.

## Story selection workflow
1. Study all scenes from protagonist viewpoint: wants / obstacle / costly decision / surprise / changed relation.
2. List candidate curiosity promises (max 3) from viewer language, not researcher delight.
3. For each, identify an **actual** resolution from the passage and a before->after understanding.
4. Select one whose middle carries a causal progression. Don't pick an ending and retroactively force every line toward it.
5. Draft with scene-specific acts, not a mechanical retell -> lecture -> moral structure.
6. Revise orally: each line changes image, tension, knowledge, feeling or evidence. Cut needless metacommentary.
7. Final beat must accomplish the emotional/intellectual promise; a branded outro is not the resolution.

## Legitimate narrative forms
- `dramatic_scene`: characters act, oppose, misunderstand, choose and change. Ideal when person-centered narrative carries emotional force.
- `investigation`: an actual question, evidence, new interpretive constraints, responsible answer. Ideal for textual puzzle and Christian apologetics where Scripture allows resolution.
- `visual_explainer`: concrete objects, comparisons and causal diagrams; answer may arrive early, followed by meaningful depth.
- `literary_pattern`: repeated imagery/wording through biblical text; source caveats and significance must be explicit.
- `portrait`: person/story whose decisions express a bigger pattern; no need for a rhetorical question.
Hybrid only when the transition is earned.

## Sounding human
- Begin with a concrete happening or instantly understandable real question.
- Short sentences are a technique, not a permanent cadence. Vary length and punctuation.
- Do not inflate lines with 'but what happened next will shock you', 'you won't believe', 'a secret hidden' unless evidence warrants it.
- Don't make Scripture sound like a dubious puzzle-solving hack; reverence and curiosity coexist.
- Don't let 'one takeaway' become a cliché. Practical application should emerge naturally when it strengthens the ending.

## Revision record, not numeric theater
For each serious revision log: exact old line; evidence of defect; proposed replacement; why it is better; what uncertainty remains. Actual viewers, not synthetic agent scoring, determine how well an episode moves people. An AI critique is a fallible diagnostic.

## Reference contrasts
- Lintel tree question: a counterfactual is built into a thought experiment, so sequence of logic matters. The device isn't a requirement for narrative biographies.
- Lintel Cain's wife: an apparent textual omission is the central tension; family/context resolves it. The audience must care about the missing person.
- BibleProject Elijah: a life portrait need not ask an overt question; a unifying thematic motif can pay off at the end.
- Fourth Watch storm draft: the puny 'they' ending cannot bear the story's emotional weight even if technically sourced.
