# Director's grammar: make the camera express the story

## Declare the read first
For a shot, write one viewer-read sentence: "We see X doing Y, and understand Z." Before any move, the composition must stage the subject against recognizable foreground/background and preserve a clear action line. The camera exists to change *knowledge or emotion*, not because a preset is available.

## Shot-size vocabulary (not quotas)
- `XWS` / extreme wide: geography, isolation, duration, scale; tiny character requires identifiable motion or contrasting silhouette.
- `WS` / wide: positions, movement across a space, relationship; keep silhouettes distinct.
- `MS` / medium: an action legible through arms, body and prop.
- `MCU` / medium close-up: a response, attention, uncertainty; don't over-rely on non-expressive faceless figures.
- `CU` / close-up: a hand, grasp, dropped object, face silhouette, physical decision.
- `ECU` / insert: critical physical evidence or a motif, not random eye candy.

## Camera angles as relational information
- Eye-level: keeps viewer beside the subject. Useful for a human turning point.
- Slight low: stresses power, discovery or imposing scale when the text deserves it.
- High: clarifies layout, vulnerability or isolation; *not* an automatic synonym for shame.
- OTS: places viewer on a side of dialogue, observation or fear. Must be able to see what the subject sees.
- True top-down: a map, pattern, multitude or spatial cause-effect; do not use for a personal prayer unless expressive reason.
- Silhouette profile: anticipation, a recognizable posture against light.
- Off-axis / slight dutch: rare instability; overuse looks like a template.

## Camera movements (choose by narrative job)
| Move | Valid job | Common failure | Implementation idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| locked | concentrate on changing action/face, weight of silence | still figure with no readable event | layer or acting movement |
| slow push | move toward small revelation / private response | unmotivated push every shot | 1-7% viewport scale, no fixed default |
| pull / reveal | change viewer's understanding of context/scale | reveal already known | start with hidden context, end with answer |
| tracking | travel *with* a participant | disconnected drone drift | preserve screen direction |
| pan | discover adjacent information in same place | chase movement that's not important | anticipate gaze with lead room |
| crane | change physical/ethical scale of place | spectacle with no readability | stage strong foreground/parallax |
| orbit | expose 3D relation or object significance | fake 'cinematic' spinning | tie orbit to object-side change |
| overhead transition | convert landscape to map/pattern | abrupt abstraction | match recognizable landmark |
| rack focus | transfer attention foreground to background | pointless blur | make both subjects relevant |
| snap/whip | shock, sudden new actor, sharp contrast | generic energy | reserve for meaningful turn |

## Editing grammar
- Cut *on a thought*: new information causes new composition.
- Preserve action direction unless the cut marks confusion or a switch in point of view.
- Match on shape, motion, spatial axis, semantic object, or sound. Match cuts are not mandatory, and continuity errors are not artistic.
- A repeated composition gains meaning when a prop, character or light has **changed**. Repeating the identical tableau without a new read is dead time.
- Establish a geographic map before complex travel if the viewer couldn't infer relative position from action.
- Adjust pace to information density and emotion, not a universal 2-second beat law. Document source study's measured medians only as historical observations.

## Example: storm camera arc (direction candidate, not approved)
1. OTS from rower toward absent shoreline: make distance legible, don't invent a verbal complaint.
2. High WIDE of small craft crossing dark water: resistance shown by water displacement and sail/rowing, not oversized disaster waves.
3. Camera favors distance when figure approaches; shift to eye-level as disciples try to see who it is.
4. Peter's action only if Matthew's account is part of the approved script: close insert on foot crossing gunwale; wide shows him walking; CU captures rescue hand.
5. Later Mark's note about the loaves can be interpreted with a cut to earlier bread only if the script earns and accurately explains that cross-reference.
6. Avoid mechanically using a cross as the final visual because it is 'theological'; make the ending specific to the narrated encounter.
