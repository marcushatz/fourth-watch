# Storm pilot — director's camera and blocking workbook

**Status:** rehearsal, not a locked script, not an approved creative treatment. Source passages Mark 6:45–52; Matthew 14:22–33; John 6:16–21. This plan does not claim all reported details belong to one continuous single-camera eyewitness account.

## Visual theme: distance / failure to recognize / contact
A boat alone against dark water becomes smaller when Jesus is observed from the shore. The first visual reversal is not spectacle but misrecognition: the shape that should reassure the disciples initially terrifies them. Peter's movement collapses a vast distance into a hand's reach. The interpretive question about the loaves is **not** yet a scripted revelation; visual imagery must not manufacture a reason Mark does not state.

## Motion rules drawn from the existing repo's source observations
- Different camera moves are not automatically interesting. A **new event, comparison, or consequence** must become visible.
- Character action is blocked by the verb: prepare -> act -> contact -> response; shorten where action does not justify all four.
- Do not set a fixed 3–5 s shot quota. Alternate conceptual transitions with held comprehension beats when needed.
- Compositing and captions must pass a phone-width review, not merely large-monitor inspection.
- On silent playback, an unfamiliar viewer should identify: the opposed boat, distant approaching figure, fearful reaction, Peter's step/fall, immediate grasp and return.
- Camera sizes are **intentions** pending animation; the placeholder animatic does not demonstrate physically convincing performance.

## Three priority real-production tests
1. **S01 resisted oar:** watch with sound off; if it looks like effortless rowing, rebuild stroke/hull relation.
2. **S07–S09 Peter's step + rescue:** same rig/body proportions and sea plane across contiguous shots, no teleportation.
3. **S10 boat return:** stage boat and both characters clearly, without inventing a narrated mode of transport.

## Independent Scripture checks before approval
- Matthew adds Peter and states wind ceases as they board; Mark lacks Peter here and emphasizes loaves and hardened hearts; John adds shore/distance details. Attributes must remain explicit when joining versions.
- Avoid attributing Mark's “meant to pass by” motive or the timing to a specific reason that no Gospel states.
- Mark 6:52 has multiple interpretive approaches; never title a speculative reading as the Gospel's explicit explanation.
