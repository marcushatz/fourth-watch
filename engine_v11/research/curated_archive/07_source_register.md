# Source register and provenance

**Audit date:** 3 November 2026. This library distinguishes four evidence classes:

- **P — primary Scripture:** the chapter/verse itself. Strong for *what a passage records*, not automatically for a theological interpretation.
- **R — prior Fourth Watch research:** user-owned existing transcripts, timing logs and shot-grid analyses. Stronger for *what the researcher archived observing*, not independent proof about virality.
- **E — external academic/creator documentation:** research on narrative psychology, sharing or skill structure. Not a guarantee for short-form Bible video outcomes.
- **J — new synthesis/judgment:** this library's explanatory mechanism hypotheses, editorial warnings and potential hooks. Must be tested and may be revised.

The per-video “story mechanisms” are coded by a single analyst and could be challenged.

- [Luke 24:13–35](https://www.esv.org/verses/Luke%2B24%3A13%E2%80%9335/): Emmaus, delayed recognition and the return.
- [Ruth 1:19–22](https://www.esv.org/verses/Ruth%2B1%3A19%E2%80%9322/) and [Ruth 4:13–17](https://www.esv.org/verses/Ruth%2B4%3A13%E2%80%9317/): grief, Ruth's presence, restored family line.

Do not infer what one Gospel leaves unsaid by silently stitching together another Gospel as a single chronological eyewitness scene. The actor, author and chapter should be specified when differences matter.

## E — independent external evidence

- [Claude skill authoring best practices (official)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices): concise main `SKILL.md`, references loaded as needed, test against representative tasks. Describes a *software packaging method*, not a shortcut to original craft.
- [Anthropic: equipping agents with skills](https://www.anthropic.com/research/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills): evaluation-driven, progressive disclosure and modular references.
- [Green & Brock, 2000 — narrative transportation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/): experimental and correlational evidence about immersion and persuasive beliefs in narratives.
- [Berger & Milkman, 2012 — online content sharing](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353): New York Times articles and experiments linking content emotion/arousal and sharing; not an Instagram formula.
- [Lehne & Koelsch, 2015 — suspense theory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4324075/): a proposed general psychological model of tension involving conflict and anticipation, not a validated editing cadence.
- [BibleProject — how it makes videos](https://help.bibleproject.com/hc/en-us/articles/4479380169879-How-do-you-create-your-videos): the creators publicly describe production using artists, animators and editing tools. This argues against assuming a magic skill-file-only workflow for every successful channel.
- [Bible Animations “Jesus' disciples were kids” — published video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GvA9-V2mj0g) and [BibleProject “The Lesson Elijah Teaches You”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rlFAnG_VR20): examples externally verified for title/uploader and historical publication; archived project estimates and counts should not be conflated with fresh YouTube counts.

## Evidence exclusions and explicit unknowns

- We have **not** obtained actual Instagram minute-by-minute retention curves for Lintel, account analytics, click-through funnels, paid spend or audience demographics.
- We have **not** run a blinded human test of the new library, and it does not yet establish lift in script quality.
- The original creator's source scripts and imagery are copyrighted; this deliverable supplies analysis, not verbatim replication.
- We don't know whether other creators rely on private Claude skills or how much of their writing is AI-assisted. No claim about their private process is warranted.
- We have **not** claimed modern academic consensus around disputed Old Testament typology, Hebrew word-splitting or the exact chronology of Jesus' interactions at the boat.

## Source policy for future editions

Every new proposed teaching should include a traceable link, the exact claim it supports, confidence category, and at least one plausible alternative reading or counterexample. Untraceable “research says...” rules should not be added. Each version must record what was deleted and why.
