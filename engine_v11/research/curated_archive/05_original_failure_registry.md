# Fourth Watch failure registry — the editorial knowledge that should not be lost

This registry preserves **the causes of rejected writing decisions**, not every adjective or phrasing preference. It is grounded in uploaded documents *Fourth Watch Writer Comparison*, *Fourth Watch Debut Script*, *Bronze Serpent Story Architecture* and the project's `research/feedback_rules.md`. It is **not** a survey of outside creators. Only recurring or consequential failures appear here.

| Failure | Specific archived example | Why it failed the actual promise | Replacement principle | Regression prompt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01: Detail too small to carry the story | Storm: “How did Peter get back to the boat?” → ending “They.” | Matthew says they returned, not how; one pronoun does not fundamentally reframe a miracle | Choose a consequential mystery or character problem; require a substantive conclusion | “If the final word is deleted, is the story still substantially changed?” |
| F02: Unanswerable motive sold as a secret | Mary: “Why did Jesus appear first to Mary Magdalene?” | John does not supply the reason; the audience expects a causal answer | Reframe as an observed encounter, not the hidden intentions of Jesus | “Does this text actually answer its opening ‘why’?” |
| F03: Interpreted verse made the whole hinge | Storm: “He meant to pass by them” linked to Moses/Job | The theological reading is debated; all three Claude comparison scripts converged on it | May use a proposed reading as an *optional* interpretive layer; anchor core plot in uncontroversial events | “Remove this commentary; does the video still work?” |
| F04: Famous-event chronology with no transforming middle | Storm script spent most of runtime saying boat → Jesus walks → Peter sinks → rescue | Each event was famous, while the promised insight arrived too late and too small | Let the viewer encounter the human predicament or new textual implication **during** the familiar events | “What does a viewer learn/feel by halfway?” |
| F05: Explanations after emotional peak | Mary: recognition at her name followed by repeated claims about remaining | The emotional climax had already occurred; postscript diluted it | End at the recognition or show the recorded *consequent action* (her announcement) | “Does the story become weaker if the explanation after the climax remains?” |
| F06: Theology inflated by cross-references | Bronze Serpent iterated through numerous typological/lexical links; viewers were not sure why the story mattered | Research outpaced a human narrative spine | Every reference must alter a specific inference, action, stake or consequence | “Can a first-time viewer state the core change without the references?” |
| F07: Rhetorical elegance mistaken for importance | “They.” and other short ‘chills’ endings | Shortness or silence can feel sophisticated but offer too little conceptual reward | Emotional simplicity is earned by what came before; audit actual meaning | “Would this be compelling in a flat human read?” |
| F08: Constrained self-judging cycle | Prior rubric, 28/35 threshold, AI agents repeatedly grading Claude's own drafts | Model outputs and model scores formed a feedback loop but not audience evidence | Use independent human listeners and observable behavior; scores remain internal and provisional | “What evidence comes from outside the writer?” |
| F09: Avoidance disguised as accuracy | Claude identified “Why did he wait?” as strong human pull, then discarded it because motives are unknown | A worthwhile open question was conflated with a claim of certainty; later less answerable “pass by” remained | Invite an open mystery honestly, explain the known events, close the investigation without inventing motive | “Did we cut curiosity merely because the reason is unknown?” |
| F10: Audience's need replaced with author fascination | Hebrew word links and unusual verse details dominating drafts | The video answered what the researcher found interesting, not what a viewer would care about | Start from a human problem or illuminating contradiction; source work must strengthen it | “Who besides a Bible researcher asks this and why?” |
| F11: Obscure brand attribution | Trying to use Mary during darkness to explain Fourth Watch | It linked two separate events merely because both occurred before dawn | Brand title is a bonus, not proof of topic quality; do not imply matching chronology | “Would it work if the channel were named differently?” |
| F12: Difficult verse framed as only a PR problem | Hagar: command to return and submit | Avoiding passage dodges biblical fidelity; a single glib disclaimer also weakens careful pastoral explanation | Acknowledge the recorded command; distinguish singular historical directive from advice to current sufferers | “Could a vulnerable viewer mistake this for a command to accept abuse?” |
| F13: Overfitting a creator's success recipe | Forcing Lintel's exact timing, 3–5 s shots, discovery quota, ‘no retelling’ | BibleProject counterexamples show successful retelling/visual explanations with different shapes | Keep observed mechanisms as options; select genre before structure | “Which contrary hit breaks this ‘rule’?” |
| F14: Storytelling process changed after each disagreement | A new prompt, script, scoring system and writing architecture after each weak draft | It created endless process optimization without delivering a video | Freeze this research version; one creative revision loop; produce/measure; only update after independent evidence | “Are we repairing the story or rebuilding the system to avoid a decision?” |

## The failure-to-correction sequence worth preserving

1. **Do not erase the earlier work.** It exposes where the author tried to create intrigue mechanically.
2. **Do not promote every complaint into a rule.** “This line isn't interesting” may be a local defect; only document a transferable principle if a counterexample would make the distinction meaningful.
3. **Make uncertainty explicit.** A question may be profound even if Scripture does not answer it; a specific causal claim still requires support.
4. **Require a real listener before locking a candidate.** If a listener cannot state what changed, neither more visuals nor more AI judges will establish that the script works.
5. **Freeze the system for the pilot.** The storm story should be an evaluation case for the library, not an excuse to rewrite the library every time a draft fails.

## What is genuinely proprietary here?

The **archived decisions**, the contrast between the exact rejected lines and why they were rejected, and eventual audience evidence are the personalized part. The general principles are widely available. If the skill alone is leaked, another writer can still copy the principles. If our actual reception and editorial feedback remain private and accumulate, they become harder to reproduce. The repo that contains much of the older research was public when reviewed; store any genuinely private findings outside a public repo unless you deliberately change its visibility.
