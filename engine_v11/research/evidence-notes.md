# What the source base actually supports

## Provenance, not mythology
The existing Fourth Watch repo already included an extensive study of Lintel Network, BibleProject and Bible Animations. The accompanying indexed 22 cases are **prior-study records from October/early November 2026**. Most views were recorded at particular historical snapshots and across different platforms; counts are not current metrics and are not normalized for audience size, topic age or algorithmic exposure. Some shot timings were human-coded from frame grids; treat them as estimates. Some author analyses include judgment, not objectively measured outcomes.

## Three observed contrasts worth retaining
1. The Lintel Network's popular 'Why didn't God remove the tree?' illustrates an argument unfolding through consequences. Its framing is unlike a dramatic, person-centered narrative. Mechanical imitations are not appropriate for every Bible story.
2. BibleProject's 'Choosing a Bible Translation' uses a continuously transforming diagram and answers its practical question early. That is a direct counterexample to rigid late-payoff rules.
3. Bible Animations' 'David replaced Goliath?' had faster cut cadence than several higher-view examples yet ended with a tease toward a longer video. Rapid cutting alone cannot make a satisfying standalone story.

## Cautions
- Do not copy third-party transcripts from the source repository into an exported skill; retain metadata, limited analytic summaries, and links. Scripts are copyrighted original works even if displayed on YouTube.
- No causal proof from a small observational sample. The difference in views could reflect topic, publication year, channel popularity, editing, distribution or random variation.
- Some reviewed third-party theology is debatable or flawed. Good storytelling must not override accurate Scripture or intellectual honesty.
- External academic literature on narrative transportation/viral sharing is contextual theory, not a how-to for Christian reels.
