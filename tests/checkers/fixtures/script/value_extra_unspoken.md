# Why did Jonah run from God instead of going to Nineveh?
series: ASK THE TEXT
target_seconds: 105
answer: The reason Jonah gives is not fear: he says he ran because he knew God is merciful, slow to anger and relenting from disaster; when God spared Nineveh, it displeased him exceedingly.

## Hook
VO: Why did Jonah run from God instead of going to Nineveh?
ON-SCREEN: WHY DID JONAH RUN FROM GOD INSTEAD OF GOING TO NINEVEH?
VISUAL: A harbour. Jonah's hand drops coins into a sailor's palm on "run"; the mooring rope slips free on "Nineveh" and the ship pulls away as the camera pulls back. Pill: TARSHISH.

## Stakes
VO: God told him to go to Nineveh and call out against it, because of their evil. Jonah found a ship going to Tarshish instead, and paid the fare. [Jonah 1:2-3]
VO: It's easy to guess he was afraid. But Jonah gives his reason himself. [Jonah 4:2]

## Story
VO: You know the next part: the storm, the sailors, the great fish. Then he went to Nineveh. [Jonah 1:4-3:3]
VISUAL: A fast run, one shot per item (1.1–2.2 s each): the storm; the sailors hurl him into the sea (1:15); the fish; Jonah on dry land (2:10); then Jonah walking into the city.

## Turn
VO: But notice how short his message was. As the book records it, one sentence: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" [Jonah 3:4]
VISUAL: A counter on screen: 40 DAYS.
VO: And the people of Nineveh believed God. [Jonah 3:5]
VISUAL: The people put on sackcloth, "from the greatest of them to the least of them"; then the word reaches the king, who sits in ashes (3:5–6). No animals in sackcloth (only the decree mentions them, 3:8).
VO: When God saw them turn around, He didn't bring the disaster. [Jonah 3:10]
VO: That's when we finally hear why Jonah ran. [Jonah 4:1-2]
VERSE: "That is why I made haste to flee to Tarshish; for I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful," — Jonah 4:2 (ESV)
VO: So the reason Jonah gives isn't fear. He says he ran because he knew God is "slow to anger" and relents from disaster. And when God spared Nineveh, "it displeased Jonah exceedingly." [Jonah 4:1-2]

## Landing
VO: And the ending is stranger than the fish.
VO: He asked God to take his life: "it is better for me to die than to live." [Jonah 4:3]
VO: God made a plant grow to shade him, and Jonah was overjoyed. At dawn, God appointed a worm, and the plant withered. God asked, "Do you do well to be angry for the plant?" Jonah said, "Yes, I do well to be angry, angry enough to die." [Jonah 4:6-9]
VO: God said, "You pity the plant, for which you did not labor… And should not I pity Nineveh, that great city…?" [Jonah 4:10-11]
VO: More than 120,000 persons, "and also much cattle." The book ends right there, on God's question. We never hear Jonah answer. [Jonah 4:11]
VO: So why did Jonah run? Because he knew how patient God is: patient enough to spare Nineveh. And the ending shows He's patient enough to keep reasoning with Jonah too. [Jonah 4:2, 4:4-11]

## Question
VO: Who surprises you more in this story: Jonah, or the people of Nineveh? [Jonah 3:5, 4:1]

## Value
- Jonah gives his own reason for running, and it is God's mercy, not fear of Nineveh [Jonah 4:2]
- The book records one sentence of his message to Nineveh, and the city believed God [Jonah 3:4-5]
- The book ends on God's unanswered question about 120,000 persons "and also much cattle" [Jonah 4:11]
- The sailors "feared the LORD exceedingly" before Jonah ever reached Nineveh [Jonah 1:16]

## Caption
