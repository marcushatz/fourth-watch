# 06c · Shot recipes: proven ways to film the beats every Bible reel has

**Use this when** you write a shot list (`05_shot_list.md` §2) and a line is one of the kinds below. Each recipe is a starting point that already obeys the rules: every clause shown happening on its word, a beat about every 2 s, a size and angle with a reason, nothing that needs a face. Adapt it to the line. Never paste it in unchanged.

**Where the devices come from:**
- The Lintel Network: pills, quote cards, book-intro cards, numbered stamps, highlight rings, UI mock-ups, the comment card.
- Bible Animations and BibleProject: speech bubbles, whoosh-ins, the blank-faced example figure, the "?" silhouette, panels, one object that transforms through a list, scale as data, the fast climax run.

Evidence for each: `research/direction_rules.md`, `research/other_ref/_crosscheck.md`, `research/lintel_ref/yt/_patterns.md`. Engine status of each device: `05_shot_list.md` §4 (exists / to build).

Each recipe gives: **when**, **shots** (size · angle · move), **beats on words**, **sound**, **traps**. Examples come from Ep 1 (Samson) and the bank's #1 (Jonah).

---

## 1. The hook question (0–5.5 s)

- **When:** the first line, always.
- **Shots:**
  - **A, the detail-to-situation pull-out:** CU or INS on the key object, low or eye level → a pull-out to WS on the question's key word (−30 to −45%).
  - **B, the action already underway:** MS on a verb in progress, with a cut to the reveal on the key word.
- **Beats:** something moves by 1.3 s (anticipation, a hand, a rope); the big change lands on the key word. The question sits as the top caption from frame one, never a title card.
- **Sound:** the bed is already in at frame one; one soft hit on the key word.
- **Examples:**
  - Samson's hands on the Gaza gate posts → pull-out to the night street.
  - Coins drop into a ship-master's palm on "run" → the rope slips on "Nineveh" and the ship pulls away.
- **Traps:**
  - No static first second.
  - Frame the hook's object so it can return at the end (§16).

## 2. "God said / God told him…"

- **When:** God speaks or commands (the text says so).
- **Shots:** the listener in MCU, high or eye level, with gold light rising from the upper left on "God"; the command as gold text in the light or as a verse card.
- **Beats:** the light rises on "God"; the listener looks up to the upper left on the command's verb.
- **Sound:** a soft shimmer on the light.
- **Traps:**
  - Never draw God the Father as a figure. The voice is light and words.
  - Gold only where the text says God acted or spoke.

## 3. A journey or a flight

- **When:** someone goes somewhere ("found a ship going to Tarshish", "went to Gaza").
- **Shots:**
  - a follow of the walker (WS, eye level, truck ≤ 25% of the frame width per second);
  - or a map card with a line drawing itself, the place names as pills.
- **Beats:** the step-off on the verb; the place pill on its name ("JOPPA · A PORT").
- **Sound:** footsteps or creaking wood; a soft tick for the pill.
- **Traps:**
  - Don't add geography the text doesn't give ("the opposite direction").
  - Use a map only where the place matters to the answer.

## 4. A number the voice says

- **When:** "three times", "forty days", "1,100 pieces of silver", "more than 120,000 people".
- **Shots:** hold the set; the device does the work.
- **Device:**
  - `counter` ticking up on the number word;
  - or scale as data (figures multiply to fill the frame);
  - or a `tally` (✓ ✓ ✓ ✗) for past cases and the one that fails.
- **Beats:** the count starts on the number word and lands on the last syllable.
- **Sound:** an accelerating tick, then a ring on the final number.
- **Traps:** count only what the text counts ("FROM EACH LORD" was a fact; "all" or "every" must hold for every item).

## 5. The famous part, told in one line (a list run)

- **When:** the part everyone knows, condensed to one line ("Then came the storm, the sailors, and three days and three nights in a great fish").
- **Shots:** the fast run: 3–5 shots of 1.1–2.2 s, one per spoken item, each a different size and angle.
- **Beats:** each item's noun is a cut. The last item settles into a hold.
- **Sound:** a hit per item, rising; the bed carries under it.
- **Traps:**
  - Never slow down here. This is where viewers swipe ("I know this part already").
  - Nothing under 1 s.
  - Show what the text says ("a great fish", not "a whale").

## 6. A character's quoted words

- **When:** a person's line in quotes ("Pick me up and hurl me into the sea").
- **Shots:** MCU on the speaker, with a `bubble` on the speaker holding the quote, or an OTS from the listener.
- **Beats:** the bubble pops on the first word; the speaker's pose ('call', 'point') fires on the key verb.
- **Sound:** a paper pop for the bubble.
- **Traps:**
  - The quote must be word for word ESV.
  - Short quotes only. Long ones become a verse card (§7).

## 7. A verse card

- **When:** the decisive ESV quote, read in full.
- **Shots:** the live scene dims under a parchment card (the reference below). The scene keeps a slow drift behind it.
- **Beats:** the card lands on the first quoted word. Then **marks**: the phrase that proves the point turns gold (or ember) on its spoken word (`VERSES[i].marks`).
- **Hold:** 3.5–5 s still, or longer only with marks acted on it.
- **Sound:** a soft bell on each mark.
- **Traps:**
  - Follow every quote with a line saying what it proves.
  - Check the card character for character against the ESV.

## 8. The turn ("But notice…")

- **When:** the overlooked detail, by ~25 s.
- **Shots:** a push into the revealing object or person (+6 to +12% over 1.5–3 s), often from high; or a match cut from the famous image to the detail.
- **Beats:** the push starts on "notice"; the detail lands with a highlight ring or a pill on its word.
- **Light:** the light changes here if the story calls for it (the gold fades at the shave, 16:19). Only on a story beat.
- **Sound:** the bed dips for a beat, then returns.
- **Traps:** this is the stillest moment allowed, but still one change about every 2 s inside it.

## 9. The core answer (35–50% of the runtime)

- **When:** the plain answer in the hook's words.
- **Shots:** the hook image returns (callback) with one change that carries the answer: a stamp, a pill, the object transformed. On Bible Animations and BibleProject the hits returned to the hook image at the answer 5 of 6 times; the lows 1 of 4.
- **Beats:** the stamp or pill lands on the answer's key word.
- **Sound:** a firm hit on the stamp; the bed opens (minor → major) if the answer brings hope.
- **Trap:** the stamp's words must be the answer, not a verdict on the viewer.

## 10. An idea or abstract line

- **When:** a line about a concept ("mercy", "set apart", "used ≠ approved").
- **Shots:** one object that something happens to, plus a 2–5 word pill. A lamp dims for "the LORD had left him"; a coin purse empties; a stamp lands.
- **Beats:** the object changes on the idea's key word.
- **Traps:**
  - Never a person standing while the voice explains.
  - No door imagery (Lintel's identity).

## 11. A crowd responds

- **When:** "the people of Nineveh believed God", "the Philistines rejoiced".
- **Shots:** XWS or WS high to show scale, then one MS of a single figure doing the verb.
- **Beats:** staggered: each figure starts 2–4 frames after the last ('kneel', 'raise', 'cheer'). A costume change happens on its verb, and only where the text says it (Jonah 3:5: they "put on sackcloth").
- **Sound:** a rising murmur, or one low swell.
- **Traps:** show only what the text says the crowd did.

## 12. A prayer or a cry to God

- **When:** "Samson called to the LORD", Jonah's prayer in the fish.
- **Shots:** high angle, the figure small in the frame (weakness); the head lifts toward the upper left on the first word of the prayer. Then a verse card for the words.
- **Light:** gold only if and where the text says God answered or acted. If it doesn't say, the light stays as it was.
- **Sound:** the bed thins, nothing new added.
- **Trap:** never imply an answer the text doesn't give ("God answered").

## 13. A death or a judgment (no gore)

- **When:** the roof falls; Ananias "fell down and breathed his last"; Lot's wife.
- **Shots:** a fast run: the cause (WS), the topple out of frame or into dust (MS), the aftermath from top-down or a crane up.
- **Beats:** the impact on the verb; dust on the next beat; a hold.
- **Sound:** a low hit, rumble and debris; then the bed settles low. Never silence.
- **Traps:**
  - Use the text's verbs ("breathed his last", not "God struck him" unless it says so).
  - Never show a body.

## 14. "Some say…" / a wrong reading refuted

- **When:** naming a common belief, then what the text says.
- **Shots:**
  - a UI mock-up: a search bar typing the belief, or comment bubbles;
  - or a pill with the belief, struck through on "never says" while the text's word replaces it.
- **Beats:** the strike or red X on the refuting word.
- **Trap:** never mock the people who believe it. Strike the claim, not a person.

## 15. A contested point (a fair note)

- **When:** Christians differ ("Some believe… others…").
- **Shots:** two equal pills or two figures side by side, the same size and light; a third pill below for what every view agrees on.
- **Beats:** each view lands on its own words.
- **Trap:** no stamp, no verdict, no gold on one side.

## 16. The re-ask (75–90%)

- **When:** the hook's words again, then the one-line answer with hope.
- **Shots:** the hook's composition again (same size, height in frame and angle), now changed by the story. The Gaza gate on the hill at dawn; Jonah outside the spared city.
- **Beats:** the camera starts the same move as the hook in reverse (a slow push-in instead of the pull-out) on the re-asked question; the light warms on the hope line.
- **Trap:** the hope line fits every viewer and uses "we" if it speaks to anyone.

## 17. The closing question

- **When:** the last spoken line (≤ 17 words).
- **Shots:** the re-ask's set holds; answer buttons pop on their words (same-type options, Marcus's form); a comment types in and finishes within 1–2 s of the last word.
- **Sound:** a pop per button, then the logo tone.
- **Trap:** the reel ends ≤ 4.5 s after the last word.

## 18. The outro

- Jesus appears slowly and calmly (~2 s fade-in, ease-in-out) and stays perfectly still. The camera is locked; the wordmark (style 4) fades in with no rise. Option A (under the question) or B (after it) is decision 2 in `DECISIONS.md`.

## Choosing angles quickly

| The moment is… | Shoot it… | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weak, small, watched, fated | High | The camera looks down on him |
| Strong, threatening, triumphant | Low | The camera looks up |
| Plain information | Eye level | Neutral |
| A plan, a pattern, an end | Top-down | God's-eye or a map |
| "We are with him" | OTS | The viewer stands in his place |
| What he can't see | POV, dark edges | The blind Samson |

Pair them on purpose: the same person high when weak and low when strong tells the story's turn without words (`06` §1).
