Why did Jesus try to walk past his own disciples? It's in Mark 6. They're fighting the wind in the dark. Jesus comes to them, walking on the sea. And Mark writes, "He meant to pass by them." Why walk out to exhausted men and keep going? Mark doesn't say. But Job says God alone "trampled the waves of the sea," and three verses later, "he passes by me, and I see him not." And when Moses begged to see God's glory, God said, "while my glory passes by I will put you in a cleft of the rock." So this is how Scripture talks about God. The disciples don't see that. They see a ghost, and they scream. He answers, "Take heart; it is I." He gets in the boat, and the wind stops. Then comes the strangest line in the story: "they were utterly astounded, for they did not understand about the loaves, but their hearts were hardened." The loaves? It's four in the morning. Why is Mark talking about bread? Because hours earlier, five loaves went through their hands and fed five thousand. They gathered twelve baskets of leftovers. They'd handled the miracle, and they still didn't know who he was. Moses hid in a rock while the glory passed by. These men didn't understand him at all. And he didn't pass by. He got in the boat.
