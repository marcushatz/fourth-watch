# Angles (Stage 3)

Judged in words. Overlap with Lintel: none of these is a Lintel topic. Their "Why doesn't God show Himself?" touches the same theme as the Job beat, but it argues from philosophers and Exodus at large. Ours is one line in Mark, with its own thread and discoveries, and shares no wording or premise.

### Angle 1: He meant to pass by them
Question (<=12 words, third person): Why would Jesus walk past his own disciples?
The common answer people give: a test, or it only looked that way.
Answer (one sentence): Mark never says, but passing by is how God showed himself to Moses, so it may be revelation rather than abandonment; and what the text records is that he got in.
What the person wants, fears and faces: to reach the far side as told; a wind against them all night; then a figure on the water.
Thread: "passing by", and the boat.
Discoveries, in order: boat not sinking (TEXT Mark 4:37 vs 6:48); hours for an hour's walk (TEXT + arithmetic); the line (TEXT 6:48); Moses (TEXT + reading); Job (TEXT + reading); "they all saw him and were terrified" (TEXT 6:50); he got in, they still didn't understand (TEXT 6:51-52).
The hinge: "they all saw him and were terrified", straight after Job's "I see him not".
Principle: left unspoken. He didn't wait for them to understand before he got in.
Screenshot line: "Job said God passes by unseen. Jesus got into the boat with them."
Familiar story needed: little. It is entered at the wind.
Risks: the Exodus and Job links are readings; must be labelled. The hook asks a why that Mark doesn't answer, so the script must say so out loud.
Original? Yes. The line is known to commentators, but rarely to viewers, and the Job turn plus the ending are not the standard sermon.
Deep? Yes. The story changes, from rescue to something stranger.
Would someone stop? Yes. It sounds wrong.
Clear? Two Old Testament names, each with one image.
Send? To a friend stuck while doing the right thing.
**Verdict: KEEP. Chosen.** It is the only angle whose hook is a real puzzle in the text and whose payoff is a recorded event.

### Angle 2: Who then is this?
Question: What did the first storm leave unanswered?
Thread: the disciples' own question (Mark 4:41; Matt 8:27), answered in Matt 14:33.
The hinge: the same men, in a boat, after the wind stops, finally answer it.
Risks: clean and textual, but lower human cost. "It is I" needs the Greek to land.
**Verdict: KEEP as the runner-up and next video.** Not chosen: nobody stands to lose anything, so it delivers awe with no ache.

### Angle 3: Why did he wait until the fourth watch?
Question: Why did Jesus wait until the fourth watch?
The strongest human pull of any angle.
**Verdict: DROP (concept).** No Gospel says why he waited, or even when he first saw them. The hook would promise what Scripture doesn't provide. Its true material (the hours, "not yet") moves into Angle 1's setup.

### Angle 4: What did bread have to do with the boat?
Question: Why does Mark blame the disciples' terror on bread?
**Verdict: DROP (concept).** A fine puzzle, but Mark doesn't say what they should have grasped, and any answer leans on a reading to carry the climax. Its best line becomes Angle 1's ending, where it is simply reported.

### Angle 5: Why did Peter sink?
**Verdict: DROP (concept).** The standard sermon, with a predictable conclusion; "took his eyes off Jesus" isn't even the text's wording.

### Angle 6: Did Jesus send them into the storm on purpose?
**Verdict: DROP (concept).** Needs a claim about foreknowledge the passage doesn't make, and it is a common sermon.

**Choice:** Angle 1. As creative director I'd defend it because the puzzle is real, it can be told in plain words, and its end needs no theology: he got in. Basis: editorial judgment and Scripture. No audience data.
