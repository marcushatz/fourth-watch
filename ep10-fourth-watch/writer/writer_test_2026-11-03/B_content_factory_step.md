# Why Was Jesus Going to Walk Past His Disciples?

## Narration

Why was Jesus going to walk right past his own disciples?

Mark's Gospel says it: "He meant to pass by them."

He sent them. He made them get into the boat and stayed behind to pray.

He saw them from the land, "making headway painfully, for the wind was against them."

He came to them, about the fourth watch of the night, the last hours before dawn, walking on the sea.

And he meant to pass by.

Scripture had used those words before, when God passed by Moses and Elijah.

And Job said God alone "trampled the waves of the sea." Then: "he passes by me, and I see him not; he moves on."

In the boat, they all saw him. "It is a ghost!"

No Gospel says they were afraid until this moment. Not the wind. Him.

They cried out, and immediately he spoke. "Take heart; it is I. Do not be afraid."

In Job, he moves on.

Here, "he got into the boat with them, and the wind ceased."

And those in the boat worshiped him, saying, "Truly you are the Son of God."

So why walk past them? Mark never says. One reasonable reading: he was showing them who he is.

He meant to pass by. They cried out. He got in.

Has God ever seemed to walk past you? Did you cry out?

## Notes

Central question: why Jesus "meant to pass by" the men he sent, saw and came to. It is the text's own puzzle, and Mark leaves it open. Want: the far shore he ordered them to. Fear: the first one named is of him. Peter's fear of the wind comes later, so Peter is left out to keep one thread. The ending admits Mark's silence, lands on what he reports, and gives the viewer a question, not a promise. Interpretation: the Moses, Elijah and Job parallels, "In Job, he moves on," and "One reasonable reading." "Not the wind" rests on the texts' silence. "Last hours before dawn" is historical background.
