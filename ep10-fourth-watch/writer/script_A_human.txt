Why did Jesus send his disciples into the storm? That afternoon they'd watched him feed five thousand people, and the crowd wanted to make him king. Then Mark says, "Immediately he made his disciples get into the boat." He made them. And he stayed behind. John writes, "It was now dark, and Jesus had not yet come to them." The wind turned against them. They rowed through the evening. They rowed past midnight. After about nine hours, they'd gone three or four miles. They were out there because they'd obeyed him. Then, sometime after three in the morning, they see a figure on the water, walking. They scream, "It is a ghost!" And through the wind, a voice they know: "Take heart; it is I. Do not be afraid." Peter says, "Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water." "Come." He steps out. He walks. Then he sees the wind, and he sinks. "Lord, save me." "Jesus immediately reached out his hand and took hold of him." They get in the boat. The wind stops. And the men who screamed "ghost" worship him: "Truly you are the Son of God." The crowd got bread. The disciples got the fourth watch. And only one of those told them who he was.
