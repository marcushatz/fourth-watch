## Method and limits

- **Search demand.**
  - **Google autocomplete:** for each question I opened Google's suggestion endpoint (`suggestqueries.google.com`, client=firefox), which is rule 14 in `script_rules_from_hits.md`. The suggestions shown are quoted as returned.
  - **WebSearch:** confirmed dedicated Q&A pages, mostly GotQuestions (which answers questions readers submit), plus other Q&A sites. These pages' **titles were seen in search results but the pages were not opened.**
  - **Gaps:** Google "People also ask" is not reachable with these tools. Searches restricted to reddit.com and stackexchange.com were rejected by the search proxy, so there are **no Reddit reply counts**.
- **Text verification.**
  - Every quote was checked on a biblehub.com/esv chapter page or a biblegateway.com ESV passage page, one page per fetch.
  - The fetch tool returns at most 125 characters per verbatim quote, so long verses were verified in pieces. A " / " in a quote marks a join between verified pieces.
  - The fetch tool reads pages through a summarizer, so I asked for every quote verbatim and report it as returned. **Run the quotes through `fw_check` at script lock anyway.**
- **LORD casing.** Both sites' extracted text shows the ESV's small-caps divine name as "Lord". In the quotes below, Old Testament divine-name instances are printed **LORD**, as the ESV prints them, and New Testament instances stay "Lord".
- **Scores (1–5).**
  - **Demand:** 5 = the exact question autocompletes with 5+ variants, has a dedicated GotQuestions page and several other Q&A pages. 4 = it autocompletes with fewer or mixed variants. 3 = only a narrower phrasing autocompletes.
  - **Clarity:** 5 = one verse states the answer. 4 = the answer needs two passages or a nuance. 3 = contested.
  - **Visual:** 5 = every beat is a person acting or an object acted on. 3 = partly abstract.
  - **Surprise:** 5 = the detail overturns the common assumption.
  - **Ties** are ordered by demand, then surprise, then lower risk.

## 1. Jonah — why he ran

- **Hook (11 words):** Why did Jonah run from God instead of going to Nineveh?
- **Sharper:** God told him, "Arise, go to Nineveh." Jonah "paid the fare" for a ship to Tarshish.
- **Answer:** Jonah says it himself: he fled because he knew God was "gracious" and "merciful" and would spare Nineveh (Jonah 4:2). **Settled**, since the text gives his own stated reason.
- **But notice:** the reason is held back until chapter 4, *after* Nineveh repents. Then Jonah says "That is why I made haste to flee to Tarshish" (Jonah 4:2), and in the next verse asks to die (4:3).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. Jonah slept through the storm: he "had lain down and was fast asleep" (Jonah 1:5). He then told the sailors, "Pick me up and hurl me into the sea; then the sea will quiet down for you" (1:12).
  2. His whole recorded message to Nineveh is one sentence: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" (3:4). The next sentence is "And the people of Nineveh believed God." (3:5).
  3. The book ends on God's unanswered question, counting "more than 120,000 persons who do not know their right hand from their left, / and also much cattle?" (4:11).
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "That is why I made haste to flee to Tarshish; for I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful," (Jonah 4:2)
  - "Therefore now, O LORD, please take my life from me, for it is better for me to die than to live." (Jonah 4:3)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* Jonah drops coins into a ship-master's palm at a dock and steps onto the gangplank. The coins clink and the ship's rope slips free.
  - *Final:* the same coin purse, now empty, lies beside Jonah as he sits outside the city that was spared. The text card reads "gracious … merciful" as the camera pulls back over the crowd of 120,000.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did jonah run" returned: "why did jonah run away from god", "why did jonah run from god", "why did jonah run away from nineveh", "why did jonah run to tarshish", "why did jonah run from the lord" (10 suggestions).
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why did Jonah try to go to Tarshish instead of Nineveh?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Jonah-Tarshish-Nineveh.html); [GotQuestions: Why was Jonah angry that the Ninevites repented?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Jonah-angry.html); [Bible Hub Q: Why did Jonah flee from the LORD?](https://biblehub.com/q/Why_did_Jonah_flee_from_the_LORD.htm); [Ligonier: Running Away from God](https://learn.ligonier.org/devotionals/running-away-god)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 · Clarity 5 · Visual 5 · Surprise 5 = **20**
- **Risk notes:**
  - Do not say "whale". ESV 1:17: "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah."
  - Do not add geography the text doesn't give (that Tarshish was "the opposite direction" or "in Spain").
  - Do not add Assyrian cruelty as the motive. Jonah's stated motive is God's mercy (4:2), and outside history would be filler.
  - Low doctrinal risk.

## 2. The forty years in the wilderness

- **Hook (11 words):** Why did the Israelites wander in the wilderness for forty years?
- **Sharper:** Moses says the trip to Kadesh-barnea was "eleven days' journey." His very next verse begins "In the fortieth year."
- **Answer:** The people refused to enter the land after the spies' report, so God set one year of wandering for each of the spies' 40 days (Num 14:34). **Settled.**
- **But notice:** the people themselves had cried "Would that we had died in this wilderness!" (Num 14:2). God answered, "what you have said in my hearing I will do to you" (14:28).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. The spies carried back one cluster of grapes "on a pole between two of them" (Num 13:23).
  2. The forty years mirror the forty days: "At the end of forty days they returned from spying out the land." (13:25); "a year for each day" (14:34).
  3. The children the people feared for are the ones God brought in: "But your little ones, who you said would become a prey, I will bring in" (14:31). Of the adults, only "Caleb the son of Jephunneh and Joshua the son of Nun" (14:30).
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "According to the number of the days in which you spied out the land, forty days, a year for each day, / you shall bear your iniquity forty years, and you shall know my displeasure." (Num 14:34)
  - "It is eleven days' journey from Horeb by the way of Mount Seir to Kadesh-barnea." (Deut 1:2)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* two spies stagger under a giant grape cluster hung on a pole. The pole bows and a day-counter in the corner ticks "DAY 1 … 11".
  - *Final:* the 40 day-tally marks flip one by one into 40 years. The children from the hook, now grown, carry the grape pole across into the land.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did the israelites wander" returned: "…for 40 years", "…in the desert for 40 years", "…for 40 years instead of 10 days", "…for so long", "why did the jews wander for 40 years".
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why was Israel cursed with forty years of wilderness wandering?](https://www.gotquestions.org/wilderness-wandering.html); [BibleTalk.tv question](https://bibletalk.tv/question/5584); [eBible Q&A](https://ebible.com/questions/22285)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 · Clarity 5 · Visual 5 · Surprise 5 = **20**
- **Risk notes:**
  - The 11 days is "from Horeb … to Kadesh-barnea" (Deut 1:2), not "from Egypt to Canaan". Don't overstate it.
  - The forty years count the time already spent. Use the text's "forty years" and do not say "38".
  - "Grasshoppers" was the spies' self-image ("we seemed to ourselves like grasshoppers," 13:33), not a measured fact.

## 3. Uzzah and the ark

- **Hook (10 words):** Why did God strike Uzzah dead for steadying the ark?
- **Sharper:** The oxen stumbled. Uzzah "put out his hand to the ark of God," and he died beside it.
- **Answer:** The text says God struck him "because of his error" (2 Sam 6:7). David later names the error: the ark was not carried "according to the rule", which meant Levites carrying it with poles (1 Chr 15:13, 15). **Settled** that the text gives this cause. The law also forbade touching it (Num 4:15).
- **But notice:** the ark was riding "on a new cart" (2 Sam 6:3). That was the Philistine priests' method: "take and prepare a new cart" (1 Sam 6:7). God's design was poles that "shall not be taken from it" (Ex 25:15).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. The ark had been kept in Abinadab's house. "Some twenty years" passed there even before Saul was king (1 Sam 7:1–2), and Uzzah was one of "the sons of Abinadab" (2 Sam 6:3).
  2. The law gave this warning about the Kohathites who carried it: "they must not touch the holy things, lest they die." (Num 4:15)
  3. On the second try, "the Levites carried the ark of God on their shoulders with the poles" (1 Chr 15:15). Meanwhile the ark's three-month host was blessed: "the LORD blessed Obed-edom and all his household" (2 Sam 6:11).
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "and God struck him down there because of his error, and he died there beside the ark of God." (2 Sam 6:7)
  - "Because you did not carry it the first time, the LORD our God broke out against us, / because we did not seek him according to the rule." (1 Chr 15:13)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* a gold-covered ark rides on a new ox cart across a threshing floor. An ox stumbles, the ark tilts, and Uzzah's hand shoots out.
  - *Final:* the same ark is now steady on Levites' shoulders with poles, moving "six steps" at a time. The empty new cart sits abandoned at the roadside.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did uzzah" returned: "why did uzzah die for touching the ark", "…die when he touched the ark", "…have to die", "why was uzzah killed when he touched the ark" (10 suggestions).
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why did God strike Uzzah dead for touching the Ark of the Covenant?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Uzzah.html); [Life, Hope & Truth: death of Uzza unfair?](https://lifehopeandtruth.com/bible/holy-bible/old-testament/the-writings/1-chronicles/death-of-uzza-unfair/); [Monergism: Uzzah struck dead](https://www.monergism.com/uzzah-struck-dead-touching-ark-2-samuel-66-11); [Bible Hub Q: Why did God kill for minor offenses?](https://biblehub.com/q/why_did_god_kill_for_minor_offenses.htm)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 · Clarity 4 · Visual 5 · Surprise 5 = **19**
- **Risk notes:**
  - Doctrinally sensitive (God kills a well-meaning man). Answer with the text's own two reasons, without softening or adding motive.
  - The text doesn't say Uzzah "should have let it fall", that the cart caused the stumble, or what tribe Uzzah was from. Don't claim any of these.
  - "Sons of Abinadab" may mean descendants. Say "the sons of Abinadab", as the text does.
  - Don't claim the ark sat there exactly 20 years until David. 1 Sam 7:2 counts "some twenty years" only up to that point.

## 4. Ananias and Sapphira

- **Hook (12 words):** Why did Ananias and Sapphira drop dead after giving to the church?
- **Sharper:** They sold land and laid the money at the apostles' feet. About three hours apart, both "breathed" their last.
- **Answer:** They kept back part of the price while presenting the gift as the whole; Peter calls it lying to God: "You have not lied to man but to God" (Acts 5:4). **Settled** on what the sin was. The text does not explain why death followed beyond this.
- **But notice:** they never had to give: "While it remained unsold, did it not remain your own? And after it was sold, was it not at your disposal?" (Acts 5:4).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. The scene just before is Barnabas, who "sold a field that belonged to him and brought the money and laid it at the apostles' feet" (Acts 4:37). Ananias copied the gesture: "laid it at the apostles' feet" (5:2).
  2. Sapphira came in "about three hours" later, "not knowing what had happened" (5:7). Peter asked her the price first: "Tell me whether you sold the land for so much." She replied, "Yes, for so much." (5:8)
  3. Peter says they "agreed together to test the Spirit of the Lord" (5:9). It was planned by two people, not a slip by one.
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "While it remained unsold, did it not remain your own? And after it was sold, was it not at your disposal?" (Acts 5:4)
  - "You have not lied to man but to God." (Acts 5:4)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* a money pouch thuds down at the apostles' feet beside Barnabas's earlier pouch. The camera tilts up to a second, hidden pouch glinting in Ananias's belt.
  - *Final:* both pouches sit side by side on the floor, the hidden one now exposed. On screen: "did it not remain your own?"
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did ananias and sapphira" returned: "…die", "…have to die", "…lie", "…die instantly", "…lie to the holy spirit".
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why did God kill Ananias and Sapphira for lying?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Ananias-and-Sapphira.html); [Grace thru Faith: Ananias and Sapphira](https://gracethrufaith.com/?p=167338)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 · Clarity 5 · Visual 4 · Surprise 5 = **19**
- **Risk notes:**
  - The text says each "fell down and breathed his/her last" (5:5, 5:10). It does not narrate "God struck them". Use the text's verbs.
  - Avoid claims about their salvation (contested) and any gore.
  - Don't turn it into a giving lesson aimed at the viewer (the no-viewer-conduct rule).

## 5. How many wise men

- **Hook (10 words):** How many wise men visited baby Jesus at the manger?
- **Sharper:** Nativity scenes show three kings at the manger. Matthew says they found "the child" in "the house."
- **Answer:** Matthew never gives a number. He lists three gifts, and the visit was in a house, not at the manger. **Settled.**
- **But notice:** "And going into the house, they saw the child with Mary his mother" (Matt 2:11). The manger belongs to Luke's shepherds, who found "the baby lying in a manger" (Luke 2:16).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. Herod killed boys "two years old or under, according to the time that he had ascertained from the wise men" (Matt 2:16).
  2. The text never calls them kings. The ESV footnote on "wise men" reads "Greek *magi*" (Matt 2:1).
  3. They went to Jerusalem first: "wise men from the east came to Jerusalem," (2:1). They did not go straight to Bethlehem.
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "And going into the house, they saw the child with Mary his mother, and they fell down and worshiped him." (Matt 2:11)
  - "they offered him gifts, gold and frankincense and myrrh." (Matt 2:11)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* a classic nativity diorama with a manger, animals and three crowned kings kneeling. Two of the kings fade to dotted outlines and a "?" counter appears.
  - *Final:* a house doorway with the child and Mary, and three gifts labelled on the floor. The crowns from the hook drop away, and the manger is now Luke's shepherds' scene, shown in a corner inset.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "how many wise men" returned: "…were there", "…visited jesus", "…came to see jesus", "…visited baby jesus", "…in the bible" (10 suggestions).
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: What does the Bible say about the three wise men (Magi)?](https://www.gotquestions.org/three-wise-men.html); [National Geographic: three wise men or twelve?](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/who-were-the-magi-three-kings-wise-men-star-of-bethlehem-jesus-christmas-story-gospel-of-matthew); [jw.org: three wise men](https://www.jw.org/en/bible-teachings/questions/three-wise-men-star-of-bethlehem/); [LWF: How many wise men visited Jesus?](https://lwf.org/questions-and-answers/how-many-wise-men-visited-jesus)

## 6. Moses and the Promised Land

- **Hook (9 words):** Why wasn't Moses allowed to enter the Promised Land?
- **Sharper:** He led Israel out of Egypt and through the desert. Then he struck a rock twice, and God said he would not bring them in.
- **Answer:** At Meribah God told Moses to speak to the rock, and Moses struck it. God said, "you did not believe in me, to uphold me as holy" (Num 20:12). **Settled**, since the reason is stated.
- **But notice:** forty years earlier at Horeb, God had *commanded* him to strike a rock: "and you shall strike the rock" (Ex 17:6). This time the command was "tell the rock" (Num 20:8), and Moses said "shall *we* bring water for you" (20:10).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. Aaron was barred too. The verdict begins "And the LORD said to Moses and Aaron," (Num 20:12).
  2. Moses begged to go in, and God said, "Enough from you; do not speak to me of this matter again." (Deut 3:26)
  3. God still showed it to him: "I have let you see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there." (Deut 34:4). The water came anyway, "abundantly" (Num 20:11).
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "Hear now, you rebels: shall we bring water for you out of this rock?" (Num 20:10)
  - "Because you did not believe in me, to uphold me as holy in the eyes of the people of Israel" (Num 20:12)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* Moses raises the staff over a rock before a thirsty crowd. The staff comes down hard, then a second time.
  - *Final:* Moses stands on a mountaintop, staff lowered, looking over green land he cannot enter. The rock and the water from the hook are tiny in the valley below.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did moses not" returned: "…enter the promised land", "…make it to the promised land", "…go into the promised land", "…see the promised land", "…enter canaan".
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why was Moses not allowed to enter the Promised Land?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Moses-promised-land.html); [Christianity.com](https://www.christianity.com/wiki/bible/why-was-moses-not-allowed-to-enter-the-promised-land.html); [Catholic Answers](https://catholic.com/qa/god-barred-moses-from-the-promised-land-for-disobeying-him); [Our Daily Bread](https://ourdailybread.org/2012/03/02/understanding-why-gods-faithful-servant-moses-wasnt-permitted-to-enter-the-promised-land-discover-the-word)

  - "Moses lost his temper" is a common gloss, not the stated reason. The stated reason is unbelief (20:12). Ps 106:33 adds "he spoke rashly with his lips"; quote that if needed.

  - Don't place the Transfiguration "in the Promised Land" (the location isn't stated).

## 7. Abraham and Isaac

- **Hook (10 words):** Why did God tell Abraham to sacrifice his son Isaac?
- **Sharper:** God said, "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love." Isaac climbed the mountain carrying the wood.
- **Answer:** The text says it was a test, "God tested Abraham" (Gen 22:1), and God stopped him before he harmed the boy (22:12). **Settled.**
- **But notice:** before the climb, Abraham told the servants "I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you." (22:5). Hebrews explains: "He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead," (Heb 11:19).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. Isaac carried the wood, while Abraham carried the fire and the knife (Gen 22:6). Isaac asked, "Behold, the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?" (22:7)
  2. God's words in 22:12 are "now I know that you fear God, / seeing you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me."
  3. "Moriah" appears only twice in the ESV: here (Gen 22:2) and where "Solomon began to build the house of the LORD in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah" (2 Chr 3:1).
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "After these things God tested Abraham" (Gen 22:1)
  - "Stay here with the donkey; I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you." (Gen 22:5)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* a father and son climb a rocky slope, the son bent under a bundle of wood and the father carrying a fire pot. The fire pot swings and its embers glow with each step.
  - *Final:* a ram "caught in a thicket by his horns" stands where the boy stood. The two figures walk back down toward the waiting servants, so the promise "come again to you" is kept.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did god ask abraham to" returned: "…sacrifice isaac", "…sacrifice his son", "…kill isaac", "…sacrifice his only son".
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why did God command Abraham to sacrifice Isaac?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Abraham-Isaac.html); [Denison Forum](https://www.denisonforum.org/?p=23205); [Christian Post](https://christianpost.com/voice/why-did-god-tell-abraham-to-sacrifice-isaac.html); [Christianity.com: Why was Abraham tested?](https://www.christianity.com/wiki/bible/why-was-abraham-tested-by-god.html)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 · Clarity 5 · Visual 5 · Surprise 4 = **19**
- **Risk notes:**
  - **High sensitivity** (a child and a knife). Never show the knife raised over the boy. Cut on the hand being stopped.
  - Isaac's age isn't given (the "boy" footnote reads "Or *young man*").
  - State the Moriah fact as a name match only. Don't assert that it is the same hill as the temple or Calvary.
  - Any Christ typology must be framed as a connection, not stated as the text's meaning.

## 8. The golden calf

- **Hook (13 words):** Why did Aaron make a golden calf while Moses was on the mountain?
- **Sharper:** Moses had been gone "forty days and forty nights." Aaron told the people to bring him their gold earrings.
- **Answer:** The people demanded gods because "Moses delayed to come down from the mountain" and "we do not know what has become of him" (Ex 32:1), and Aaron did as they asked. **Settled.**
- **But notice:** Aaron's excuse to Moses was "I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf." (32:24). But verse 4 says he "fashioned it with a graving tool and made a golden calf."
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. They gave the calf credit for the exodus: "These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!" (32:4). One verse earlier they had said the same of Moses: "the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt" (32:1).
  2. Aaron called the party a feast to God: "Tomorrow shall be a feast to the LORD." (32:5)
  3. Moses "burned it with fire and ground it to powder / and scattered it on the water and made the people of Israel drink it." (32:20). The tablets he smashed were "the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God" (32:16).
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him." (Ex 32:1)
  - "So they gave it to me, and I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf." (Ex 32:24)

## 19. The fig tree out of season

- **Hook (12 words):** Why did Jesus curse a fig tree when it wasn't fig season?
- **Sharper:** Mark says "it was not the season for figs." The next morning it was "withered away to its roots."
- **Answer:** The text gives no one-line reason. It records three things:
  - the tree was "in leaf" but had "nothing but leaves" (Mark 11:13);
  - Mark wraps the curse around Jesus clearing the temple (11:15–17);
  - Jesus uses the withered tree to teach "Have faith in God." (11:22).

  **Contested.** Views: an acted warning against leaves without fruit (the temple scene in the middle); a lesson in faith and prayer (stated in 11:22); or both.
- **But notice:** Mark splits the story in two and puts the temple scene in the middle: the curse (11:14), "a den of robbers" (11:17), then the withered tree the next morning (11:20).
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. "And his disciples heard it." (11:14): Mark notes they heard the curse.
  2. Jeremiah used the same picture: "nor figs on the fig tree; even the leaves are withered" (Jer 8:13).
  3. Jesus told a parable of a fruitless fig tree that got one more year: "Sir, let it alone this year also" (Luke 13:8). Matthew says the cursed tree "withered at once." (Matt 21:19)
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "When he came to it, he found nothing but leaves, for it was not the season for figs." (Mark 11:13)
  - "Rabbi, look! The fig tree that you cursed has withered." (Mark 11:21)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* a lush fig tree in full leaf by the road. Hands part the leaves and the stems are empty.
  - *Final:* the same tree stands bare, "withered away to its roots", with the temple behind it. The leaves from the hook blow off-frame.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Autocomplete for "why did jesus curse the" returned: "…fig tree", "…fig tree when it was out of season", "…fig tree when it was not the season for figs", "…fig tree in mark 11".
  - Search results: [GotQuestions: Why did Jesus curse the fig tree?](https://www.gotquestions.org/curse-fig-tree.html); [Logos](https://www.logos.com/grow/bsm-why-did-jesus-curse-a-fig-tree/); [Enduring Word](https://enduringword.com/why-did-jesus-curse-a-fig-tree-qa-for-may-27-2021/); [Ligonier: Mark, fig tree and temple](https://learn.ligonier.org/sermons/mark-fig-tree-and-temple)

  - The "sandwich means judgment on the temple" reading is an inference. Say "Mark places…", not "Mark means…".

## 20. Saul and the witch of Endor

- **Hook (10 words):** Why did King Saul go to the witch of Endor?
- **Sharper:** Saul himself "had put the mediums and the necromancers out of the land." Then he went to one, in disguise.
- **Answer:** God had stopped answering him: "the LORD did not answer him, either by dreams, or by Urim, or by prophets" (1 Sam 28:6). When he saw the Philistine army, "he was afraid, and his heart trembled greatly" (28:5). **Settled** for why he went. Whether it was truly Samuel is **contested**: the text calls him Samuel; some read it as God allowing a real appearance, others as a deception.
- **But notice:** "When the woman saw Samuel, she cried out with a loud voice." (28:12). The medium herself was shocked.
- **3 things most viewers don't know:**
  1. "So Saul disguised himself and put on other garments and went" (28:8).
  2. The message: "and tomorrow you and your sons shall be with me." (28:19)
  3. Chronicles' verdict: "So Saul died for his breach of faith … and also consulted a medium, seeking guidance." (1 Chr 10:13)
- **ESV quotes:**
  - "And when Saul inquired of the LORD, the LORD did not answer him, either by dreams, or by Urim, or by prophets." (1 Sam 28:6)
  - "When the woman saw Samuel, she cried out with a loud voice." (1 Sam 28:12)
- **Visual plan:**
  - *Hook:* at night, a king pulls a plain cloak over his robe and slips his crown under it. He walks out past the camp fires toward a lamp-lit hut.
  - *Final:* Saul "bowed with his face to the ground" (28:14), and the crown from the hook lies on the dirt floor beside him.

## Reserves (verified, not in the 20)

**R1. "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"**
- **Hook:** Why did Jesus cry "My God, why have you forsaken me" on the cross? (12 words)
- **Verified facts:**
  - The cry is word for word the first line of Psalm 22 ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?", Ps 22:1; Matt 27:46).
  - The same psalm says "they divide my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots." (Ps 22:18), which John says was fulfilled at the cross (John 19:24).
  - Psalm 22's "He trusts in the LORD; let him deliver him" (22:8) is echoed by the mockers: "He trusts in God; let God deliver him now" (Matt 27:43).
  - Bystanders misheard: "This man is calling Elijah." (27:47)
  - The psalm turns: "he has not hidden his face from him, but has heard, when he cried to him." (22:24)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 (autocomplete: 10 variants incl. "…if he is god"; [GotQuestions: seven sayings](https://www.gotquestions.org/seven-sayings-Christ.html), [GotQuestions: why did He call God "My God"?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Jesus-say-my-God.html)) · Clarity 3 · Visual 3 · Surprise 5 = **16**.
- **Why cut:** this is the most doctrinally sensitive pick in the set (Trinity, and what "forsaken" means). Its meaning is contested, and it needs crucifixion imagery. Also, the Ps 22:16 "pierced" line carries an ESV textual footnote ("most Hebrew manuscripts *like a lion*"), so don't use it.

**R2. Balaam's donkey**
- **Hook:** Why was God angry at Balaam for going when He said "go"? (11 words)
- **Verified facts:**
  - God said "If the men have come to call you, rise, go with them; but only do what I tell you." (Num 22:20). Yet "God's anger was kindled because he went" (22:22).
  - The angel said "your way is perverse before me" (22:32).
  - Peter gives the motive: Balaam "loved gain from wrongdoing" (2 Pet 2:15).
  - "a speechless donkey spoke with human voice and restrained the prophet's madness." (2 Pet 2:16)
  - The donkey saved his life (22:33), and the angel then repeats "Go with the men, but speak only the word that I tell you." (22:35)
- **Scores:** Demand 3 (autocomplete "why was god angry with balaam" and variants; [GotQuestions: Why does God tell Balaam to go … and then get angry?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Balaam-God-change-mind.html)) · Clarity 3 · Visual 5 · Surprise 4 = **15**.
- **Why cut:** lower demand and a contested answer. A strong visual pick if the bank runs low.

**R3. Cain's rejected offering**
- **Hook:** Why did God reject Cain's offering but accept Abel's? (9 words)
- **Verified facts:**
  - Cain brought "of the fruit of the ground", and Abel "of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions" (Gen 4:3–4).
  - God "had regard for Abel and his offering" (4:4): the man is named before the gift.
  - Hebrews: "By faith Abel offered to God a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain" (Heb 11:4).
  - John: "Because his own deeds were evil and his brother's righteous." (1 John 3:12)
  - God to Cain: "If you do well, will you not be accepted?" (Gen 4:7)
- **Scores:** Demand 5 (autocomplete incl. "…got questions"; [GotQuestions: Cain and Abel](https://www.gotquestions.org/Cain-and-Abel.html)) · Clarity 3 · Visual 4 · Surprise 3 = **15**.
- **Why cut:** contested (faith, the heart, best-of-the-flock, or blood), lower surprise, and it sits right next to Lintel's hit "Cain's wife".
