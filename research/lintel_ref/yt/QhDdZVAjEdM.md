## Techniques (with time stamps)

- **Hook = the end image first.** The blood-marked door glowing at night (0–4.9 s) shows the answer object while the question is asked, then a whip-travel jumps back 400 years (4.9 s).
- **A shot per clause, often per phrase.** 26 shots in 59.7 s; the Moses section is 5 shots in 4.6 s (15.4–20.7 s): basket, travel, Moses enters, Moses demands, Pharaoh refuses. Each verb gets its own action.
- **Painting the door in four cuts** (31.6–35.4 s, 0.5–1.3 s each). The words "marked / doors / blood / spotless lamb" each land on a new step of the action, and the shots get shorter as the line builds.
- **Camera travel as the timeline.** Long pans across the same continuous Egypt set connect years and places (4.9, 16.7, 22.0, 28.0, 41.2 s) instead of cuts. "Decades later" is a camera move.
- **Death shown as a moving shadow** across the village, and Passover shown as which houses stay lit (35.6–41.0 s).
- **Callback to the hook**: the doorway returns at 46.0 s (day, family) and 51.1 s (night, red frame alone).
- **Brand reveal as the ending.** "The top beam… is called the lintel": the act of painting the lintel cuts straight to the logo, whose glyph is that doorframe (52.2–54.9 s). The CTA is "follow along", not a comment question.
- **Contrast with the hits**: this is the fastest and most camera-active video but one of the lowest performers. Fast cutting and camera travel alone did not produce views here (inference; topic and format, a story vs a question, also differ).
