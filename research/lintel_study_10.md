## Script mechanics

1. **Length.** Shorts run 60–137 s, typically 85–115 s, about 140–155 wpm, so 220–300 words. Our Ep 1 at 414 words / 2:39 is longer than every Short they have made.
2. **Hook = the viewer's real doubt, phrased as "If [thing you believe], [troubling question]?"** Often a second sentence sharpens it ("And why would a loving God send anyone there forever?").
3. **Second beat names who asks or claims it (a steelman with a real source):** "Many critics say no…", "Philosophers call it divine hiddenness, and J. L. Schellenberg argues…", "In 2006 … the Gospel of Judas made headlines…", "People ask this in our comments all the time", "It's one of the most asked questions about the Bible, and most Christians can't answer it." This gives stakes and authority in about 5 seconds.
4. **A short pivot line into the text:** "It sounds convincing until you open the book of Exodus." / "But listen to what Jesus himself said…" / "So let's allow the Bible to answer it." / "But look at what the question assumes."
5. **Body formats (chosen per question, never forced):**
   - numbered answers spoken as ordinals: "First, ask who hell was made for." "Second, look at who is working…" "The third answer is…";
   - an escalation ladder with no numbers: "But death was only the first thing to change…" → "The change at home would be even more striking…" → "Yet even that is not the greatest loss.";
   - an evidence list to a verdict (Judas);
   - a counterfactual walk-through (remove the tree → remove every temptation → "a world with no choice left in it");
   - a puzzle and its clue ("The clue is one chapter later.").
6. **Each point runs claim → one quote → a plain-English reframe in one short line.** "It was never made for you or for me." / "He knocks, but he never kicks the door in." / "Seeing God did not make them love him."
7. **Quotable aphorisms, about one every 20–30 s:** "Knowing something is not the same as causing it." / "A belief can begin as an inheritance and become a choice the day you decide to stay." / "Sin came in through a tree in a garden, and Jesus paid for it on a second tree." / "Money … has never once promised to stay." / "And the cure is a greater love."
8. **A modern-life bridge, shown in the same art style:** "Life works the same way. A colleague lies about you and you lose your job. Years later God opens a better door. You'll thank God for the new door, but that will never make the lie right." (Judas). There's also "Your great-grandparents would still sit at your dinner table" (Adam). This is the strongest device for making a point land as personal value.
9. **Specific numbers and names in every story line:** 2006, 1,700-year-old, 400 years, nine plagues, 930 years, 30 pieces of silver, $100,000, "about two hours".
10. **Fairness lines on contested questions:** "Christians still disagree on exactly how… Some believe… others believe… But every one of those views agrees…"
11. **Outside voices, used sparingly and always attributed:** Pascal, Kierkegaard, C. S. Lewis, Plantinga, a 2023 study. Scripture still settles the answer.
12. **Ending:** restate the title question, then a one-line answer ("So, was Judas a bad person or only an instrument? He was a man who made his own choice, and God turned even that betrayal into our salvation."). They often add a final reframe that puts the question back on the viewer ("So the question isn't only what happens to people who never heard. It's also who is going to tell them.").
13. **Christ-centered landing in about 7 of 10:** the cross, "God came as a servant", "the family Christ came to save". (Marcus declined this for Ep 1. It's an option, not a rule.)
14. **CTA:** a question tied to the video's central image ("If Jesus is knocking, what keeps people from opening the door?"). It's sometimes either/or, sometimes open or personal ("Tell us the moment it became your own", "Think of the person who first told you about Jesus and say thank you to them", "What's one thing money could never buy you that God gave you anyway?"). Two other moves: naming the community ("And welcome to the Valiant.") and equipping the viewer ("next time someone asks you…, you'll know what to say").
15. **Long form:** a cold open, then the series intro ("Welcome to The Bible Explained…") at 0:50; an explicit roadmap promise ("By the end of this video, you'll know…"); historical context (the Ephesus riot, Acts 19); a Greek word card (philarguria); research; "three honest questions" as a self-check; chapters; a next-episode tease.
16. **Translation:** they quote the KJV for weight. We stay ESV (binding).

## Visual and camera mechanics

- **Look:** isometric, low-poly 3D dioramas with a tilted overhead camera, small simple characters, vertical god-light shafts from above, dust motes and a vignette. Night scenes are navy; day scenes are warm tan.
- **Cold open, then the title:** 0–3 s on an establishing scene with the first caption words, then a big two-line title card over the live scene at about 3–4 s ("WHY IS THERE / A HELL?"), with the key word in their accent red.
- **Captions** sit at the TOP (about the upper 12–15%), in a small italic serif, one or two lines, left-aligned full sentences, key words in red caps. The middle and lower frame stay free for the scene.
- **Shot length:** about 3–5 s, cut on phrase boundaries. Mostly hard cuts with slow drifts and push-ins on the diorama, plus a few cross-dissolves.
- **Concept label pills** in a dark rounded pill at the bottom-center of the scene: "AN ACCIDENT OF BIRTH", "THE GENETIC FALLACY", "NO CHOICE LEFT", "THE MEMORY OF EDEN". There's also a red stamp ("STILL WRONG").
- **Quote cards:** a parchment card with an italic serif quote, key words in red caps and the reference below. The card sits in the upper middle while the scene stays alive, dimmed, behind it.
- **Book-intro cards:** an open Bible with rays and the book's name ("EXODUS"; "REVELATION · the last book of the Bible"), placed before the story is quoted.
- **Visual metaphors that literalize a phrase:** red theatre curtains for "playing a part", dice for "accident of birth", puppets on strings for "no choice", a door being knocked on, sticky notes for inherited beliefs, fingerprint rings for blame, a rope line for "distance", a signpost reading LIFE, a gift box vs. weights for "gift or burden", a candle for Pascal's light, a scale for judgment, a search bar for "most asked question", logic and timeline diagrams, a line chart for research.
- **Modern scenes in the same style:** office desks, a family dinner table.
- **Highlight rings and dotted frames** around the subject; big number cards and counters ("1", "30 pieces of silver").
- **Callback shots:** the opening title or image returns near the end.
- **CTA screen:** a dark card with the question in big caps plus either two answer pills or a comment bar with text typing.
- **Outro:** "WELCOME TO THE VALIANT" (community shield over a crowd in a spotlight), then a hard cut to the logo: a simple red doorframe icon, the wordmark, and the tagline "Covered, every day."
- **Brand-origin Short (Exodus 12):** the story builds to "the top beam they struck first … it's called the lintel", the doorway becomes the logo, then "Follow along as we walk through the Bible, one story at a time."

## What this means for Fourth Watch

1. **Target Reels at 90–120 s (240–310 words).** Anything longer belongs in a long-form episode.
2. **Second beat:** name who believes or asks the question, using a real source, a common saying or "people ask".
3. **Every point gets one plain reframe line.** Aim for at least two quotable aphorisms per script.
4. **Add one modern-life bridge per script,** in the same art style, for the point that is most about the viewer.
5. **End with an echo:** restate the hook question, give the answer in one line, and add a comforting or responsibility reframe. Then a CTA tied to the central image.
6. **Visual-metaphor pass on the shot list:** for every abstract phrase, ask "what object literally shows this?"
7. **Optional brand moves:** a community name ("welcome to …") and a brand-origin Reel explaining "Fourth Watch".
