# Script rules from the Lintel hits (words: structure, timing, phrasing)

**Date:** Oct 31, 2026.
**Scope:** the narration only. Shots, camera and on-screen devices are covered in `research/lintel_ref/yt/_patterns.md` and `research/direction_rules.md`; this file does not repeat them.
**Binding over everything here:** Marcus's rules in `research/feedback_rules.md`:
- ESV only; quoted verbs must match the ESV; nothing implied is stated as fact; never "God answered".
- Never assume the viewer has strayed; no finger-pointing.
- A closing question to viewers is allowed.
- No spoken intro and no "thanks for watching".

Where a finding below conflicts with one of his rules, his rule wins, and the conflict is named.

---

## 0. Data and method

**Sample.** 14 Lintel Shorts. Born elsewhere (20K at 1 day old) is measured but left out of every comparison, which leaves **13 compared videos**.
- **Hits:** Tree 7.6M · Cain's wife 1.8M · Never heard 1.6M.
- **Mid:** Suffering 528K · Judas 338K · Methuselah 325K. "Top six" means the three hits plus these three.
- **Lows:** Hell 194K · Show Himself 75K · Hide 48K · A&E never ate 35K · Exodus 12 33K · Job prayer 31K · 7 days 24K.

**Where the words come from.**
- **12 videos:** YouTube's timed auto-captions, as written into the shot tables in `lintel_ref/yt/<id>.md`. Sentence times inside a shot are interpolated by word position (about ±1 s). Auto-caption punctuation decides the sentence breaks, so sentence counts are approximate.
- **Suffering and Methuselah:** read by me from the burned-in captions in the 1 fps frame sheets.
  - Suffering: `lintel_ref/gsheet_0..5.jpg`. Methuselah: `lintel_ref/sheet_0..4.jpg`.
  - Video time is the recording time minus 2 s (±1 s).
  - Words that never built on screen are shown in [brackets] and are not counted.
  - Methuselah's first 4 s show a counter with no caption, so its first spoken words are unknown. Its word count is a few words low.
  - Both scripts are given in full in the appendix.
- **Ep 1 v57:** exact word timings from the v57 take, generated from `episode.md` v57.
  - The final render runs **149.92 s**.
  - The voice ends at 143.02 s, then a 1.3 s hold, then the 5.6 s outro.

**Category coding (my judgement, one coder).** Every second of runtime was given one category:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Q | The hook question |
| F | Framing: stakes, "people ask this", a roadmap, a re-ask |
| R | Retelling the famous story the viewer probably knows |
| L | A lesser-known story told as evidence (Cornelius) |
| E | Evidence or new information: quotes, little-known details, reasoning, thought experiments, data |
| X | The answer block: the first plain statement of the core answer, and its final restatement |
| A | Application aimed at the viewer: "you" lessons, modern-life bridges |
| C | The closing question or CTA |
| Z | Music and logo |

Three summary shares follow from these codes:
- **Story** = R.
- **Argument** = E + X. L is reported separately.
- **Viewer** = A.

**Answer timing.**
- *First answer piece* is the first line that gives any reason toward the answer.
- *Core answer* is the first plain statement, in the narrator's words, of the idea the ending restates.

**Counts.**
- **Concrete nouns:** a fixed list of things you could point at (tree, gate, wife, silver, cross…). Abstract nouns are excluded.
- **Names:** people, places and books of the Bible, excluding God, LORD, Jesus and Christ.
- **Numbers:** quantities only. Signposts ("One:", "three reasons") and chapter references are excluded.
- **"You" lines:** sentences with you/your outside quoted Scripture.

**Caveats (read before using any number).**
- The sample is 13. Views come from Instagram, while the scripts come from the identical YouTube uploads.
- **Topic is a confound.** The three hits are classic, widely asked puzzles. Four of the seven lows are not even question videos: a story retelling (Exodus), a prayer (Job), a KJV reading (7 days) and a devotional (Hide).
- Posting order and account growth are also confounds.
- Everything below marked **measured** is a count or time from the material. Everything marked **inferred** is my reading of why.

## 2. What separates the hits (and the mid performers) from the lows

### 2a. Measured differences

Each point gives the top six, then the lows, then Ep 1 where it matters.

1. **Very little retelling of the famous story.**
   - The 3 hits spend **0%, 5% and 0%** of their runtime retelling a story the viewer already knows. The top six spend 0–20%.
   - Never heard does tell a story (Cornelius, 21%), but it is a little-known case used as evidence, not a retelling.
   - The two most-retold videos in the set are the two lowest question-free videos: Exodus 69% and 7 days 82%.
   - Low retelling is **necessary, not sufficient**: Hell and A&E retell 0% and are still lows.
   - Ep 1 retells for 49%.
2. **The core answer lands by the midpoint.**
   - Top six: 33%, 38%, 36%, 45%, 46%, 50%.
   - Lows, among the question videos: 59% (Hide), 74% (Show), 80% (Exodus), 82% (Hell).
   - The lows drip an early fragment (at 15–26%) and only state the real answer in the last quarter. The gap between the first answer piece and the core answer is 50–68 s for Hell, Show and Hide, against 0–24 s for the top six.
   - **This is the cleanest script-level separator in the set.**
   - Ep 1: 45%, with a 14.6 s gap. **Matches.**
3. **The turn comes early.**
   - Every one of the top six pivots on a "But notice / But listen / Yet the Bible says / If God had…" line at **14–26% (14–25 s)**, after 0–14 s of story.
   - Hide's first turn is at 35%.
   - Some lows also turn early (Hell 16%, Show 14%), so this is necessary, not sufficient.
   - Ep 1 turns at 36% (53.5 s), after 48 s of story.
4. **Little "you" application.**
   - Top six: 1–3 you-lines each, and 0–11% of runtime aimed at the viewer (the 11% is Judas's modern-life bridge).
   - The three videos that spend 19–57% of their runtime applying it to the viewer, with 5–9 you-lines each, are all lows: Hide, A&E and Job.
   - Ep 1: 4 you-lines (hook, the two answer sentences, CTA), 0% application block. Borderline.
5. **No outside authorities.**
   - The 3 hits and Suffering and Methuselah quote no one but Scripture.
   - The videos built on named thinkers are lows: Show (Schellenberg, Kierkegaard, Pascal) and A&E (C. S. Lewis). Born (Plantinga) has too little data.
   - Judas names the Gospel of Judas only as the claim it refutes.
   - This fits our ESV-only rule. Ep 1 has none.
6. **No follow or community ask.**
   - All six endings that ask the viewer to follow (Exodus, Job, 7 days) or say "welcome to the Valiant" (Show, A&E, Born) are lows or new.
   - The hits end on one comment question, a personal action, or nothing (Cain).
   - Ep 1 is clean.
7. **The hook's own words come back near the end.**
   - All 3 hits repeat the hook's exact words at **82–89%** of runtime:
     - "So, why didn't God remove the tree?"
     - "…where Cain's wife came from"
     - "…what happens to people who never heard of Jesus"
   - Judas, Hell, Hide and Exodus do it too, so it is necessary, not sufficient.
   - **Ep 1's echo is at 45% only; its last 20% never returns to the hook's words.**
8. **Length.**
   - The top six run **84–137 s** (median 107 s) with **218–414 words** (median 275).
   - The only longer Lintel video is Hide (164 s), a low. Length alone did not separate hits from lows.
   - Ep 1: 150 s, 379 words.

### 2d. Inferred

- **Question type.**
  - The hits answer a *puzzle about the text* that has a definite, checkable answer: why the tree stayed; who Cain married; what Scripture says about the unreached.
  - The weaker question videos argue a stance (Hell, Show), speculate (A&E) or console (Hide).
- **Who the hook is about.**
  - All three hits ask about God or Bible people in the third person.
  - The second-person or "we" hooks are all lows: Job "Are you looking for a job?", Hide "Why do we hide…?", and Born, which is too new to judge.
  - The sample is tiny. Treat this as a direction, not a law.
- **What the second half does.**
  - After the core answer, the hits keep adding new evidence for 42–93 s: more verses, a timeline, a refuted alternative ("Some say God made other people outside Eden. Genesis never says that."), a fair note where Christians differ, then the link to Christ.
  - They do not add more story or more lessons.

## 3. Script rules (checkable, with evidence)

Every rule is checked at script lock, before any shot list. **Measured** marks a count in this sample; *(inferred)* marks a judgement.

1. **Word budget: 230–300 words, runtime 90–120 s.**
   - Check: words ≤ 300. Words ÷ 2.65 + about 9 s of closing should come out ≤ 120 s.
   - Evidence (measured): top six 218–414 words (median 275) and 84–137 s (median 107 s). The only longer Lintel Short (Hide, 164 s) is a low. Ep 1: 379 words, 150 s.
   - Length alone did not separate hits from lows, so this is a budget, not a cause.
2. **The hook asks about a named Bible person, object or event, in the third person, and is fully spoken by 6 s.**
   - Check: the question contains a name or object from the text; there is no "you/your/we" in the question; the question is complete by ≤ 6 s.
   - Evidence: all 3 hits do this (Eve, fruit, tree; Cain's wife; a man who never heard of Jesus). The second-person and "we" hooks are all lows *(inferred; topic confound)*.
   - A third-person hook also can never point a finger at the viewer (Marcus's rule).
3. **The second sentence sharpens the puzzle with a concrete Bible fact.** It takes the form "If [fact], [sharper question]?" or "He knew… and still…".
   - Check: sentence 2 contains a name or concrete noun and raises the stakes.
   - Evidence (measured): Tree, Cain, Never heard and Suffering all do this. Among the lows, only Hell and A&E do. Ep 1 already does it well with the Gaza lines.
4. **The "But notice…" turn comes by 25 s (≤ 25% of runtime), with no more than about 10 s of story before it.**
   - Check: time of the first line that brings in the overlooked detail, the thought experiment or the decisive verse.
   - Evidence (measured): top six turn at 14–25 s (14–26%) after 0–14 s of story. Ep 1 turns at 53.5 s after 48 s of story.
   - Practical form: open the story at the scene that contains the detail, and cover the earlier events in one or two lines.
5. **Story is at most 25% of runtime (Marcus's hard cap is 40%, Round 8). Retelling the famous parts is at most 10%.**
   - Check: add up the seconds of lines that narrate events.
   - Evidence (measured): the hits retell 0–5%. The top six spend 0–21% on any story. The two story-led videos are near the bottom (69%, 82%). Ep 1: 49%.
   - The 25% target is stricter than Marcus's 40% cap and is his call. The 40% cap stays binding regardless.
6. **State the core answer plainly, in the narrator's words, by the midpoint (35–50%), then spend the second half adding 2–4 new evidence beats.**
   - No partial answers dripped across the video: the gap from the first answer piece to the core answer is ≤ 25 s.
   - The new beats can be a verse, a little-known detail, a refuted alternative, or a fair note where Christians differ.
   - Check: the core-answer line's time; the gap; the count of evidence beats after it.
   - Evidence (measured): top six core answer at 33–50%, gap 0–24 s, and 42–93 s of added evidence after it. The question-video lows answer at 59–82% with a 50–68 s gap. Ep 1: 45%, gap 15 s. Passes.
7. **Every quote is followed by a plain line that says what it proves** ("So…", "which means…", "Notice…").
   - Check: the line after each quote states its meaning or consequence.
   - Evidence (measured): 9 of 12 quote beats in the hits, including all of Tree and Cain. The three exceptions are in Never heard, where the numbered claim comes before the verse. Ep 1: 2 of 4. The Matthew 7 quote is followed by a transition, and 16:30 by a new point.
8. **Scripture alone carries the argument: no outside authorities.**
   - A claim being refuted may be named as the question (as Judas does with the Gospel of Judas).
   - Check: the count of non-Bible thinkers cited as support is 0.
   - Evidence (measured): 0 in the hits, Suffering and Methuselah. The lows lean on them (Show 3, A&E 1). This matches the ESV-only rule.
9. **At most 3 "you" lines, used in the hook (optional), one heart line and the CTA. Viewer application is ≤ 10% of runtime.**
   - No line describes the viewer's conduct (Marcus's rule).
   - Check: count you/your sentences outside quotes.
   - Evidence (measured): top six 1–3 lines and 0–11% application. Hide, A&E and Job (5–9 lines, 19–57%) are all lows. Ep 1: 4 lines.

## 6. For the next script (rewording ideas; Ep 1 is locked)

Every new line still goes through the full script loop: the checker, the independent ESV fact-check and the first-time viewer.

1. **Hook form.** "Why did God [keep doing X / not do Y] when [Bible fact]?", asked about the person, not the viewer.
   - Illustration only (from `master_scriptwriting.md` §4): "Why did God keep giving Samson his strength while he kept sinning?"
   - Second sentence: one concrete fact, as the Gaza line already does.
2. **Open on the scene that holds the overlooked detail**, and put everything before it in one or two lines.
   - For a Samson-type story: start at the fourth call (16:20) and give the Gaza night and the three traps in one sentence each.
   - Target: the turn by about 20 s.
3. **Core answer in at most 15 words, using the hook's own words, at 35–50%**, straight after the decisive verse:
   - [verse] → "So…" → answer.
4. **Second half = new evidence, not more story.** Two to four beats:
   - another verse that shows the same pattern;
   - a little-known detail;
   - a refuted wrong reading, in the form "Some say… The text never says that.";
   - a fair note if Christians read it differently.
5. **At 80–85%: re-ask the hook in its exact words, then a one-line answer that carries the hope**, so the ending still fits every viewer and never says "come back". Then the closing question.
6. **Budget:** 240–300 words; at most 3 you-lines; no outside authorities; a closing question of at most 17 words that names where to answer ("…in the comments").
7. **A Christ link in the last quarter** appears in all three hits, but also in the lows, and Marcus declined it for Ep 1. Use it only where the text itself makes the connection, and only if he wants it.
8. **Topic selection:** shortlist questions that already autocomplete in search (rule 14), and give Marcus the options to choose from (Round 5).
