# Scripture and source proof

Each consequential claim is an atomic ledger row with `id`, `text`, `kind`, `scripture`, `source_url`, `support_quote_or_summary`, `risk` and `decision`. Kinds:
- TEXT: exact canonical passage asserts it. Specify narrator / gospel / verse, do not flatten parallel accounts.
- INFERRED: reasonable implication, not explicit. Don't claim the reader *must* accept it.
- INTERPRETATION: disputed exegesis, typology, motive or link. Credit an interpretable tradition/commentator with actual source.
- HISTORICAL: external fact; cite an independent credible source.
- VISUAL_SYMBOL: atmospheric/conceptual, not historical claim; document risk of misleading depiction.
- UNSUPPORTED: remove or rewrite; cannot enter locked script.

## Cross-Gospel rule
Matthew 14:22-33 includes Peter walking, sinking and the disciples' worship. Mark 6:45-52 includes the 'meant to pass by' wording and the disciples not understanding the loaves. John 6:16-21 includes their rowing distance and Jesus coming to the boat. Narrators have different emphases; a new script may harmonize facts only with defensible sequencing and attribution. Don't synthesize undocumented quotes or a new composite point of view.

## Two storm interpretive traps
1. **Why 'pass by'?** Mark reports an intention but does not explain the cause. Theophany readings evoke OT divine self-revelation (e.g., Ex 33-34 and Job 9), but must be introduced as scholarly interpretation, not the only meaning.
2. **What did the loaves mean?** Mark 6:52 says the disciples failed to understand; exact content of what they failed to infer is argued. Do not claim certainty about their psychology or the precise miracle relationship beyond text.
3. **Why the fourth watch?** The time is recorded; a divine reason for the delay is not supplied. Time as narrative tension is valid; a definitive why-answer is not.
4. **Storm claims:** The text reports the wind opposing them. Don't say the boat was about to sink, or the men were panicking at the wind, unless the selected Gospel explicitly supports it.

## Illustrations as factual assertions
A period-accurate-looking 3D shot may imply events the text never says happened. Treat face-to-face dialogue, Jesus' hand gestures, weather severity, crew count, walking route, biblical age, clothing detail and unseen conversations as uncertainty-sensitive. An illustrative visualization can be appropriate without being labeled documentary reconstruction; avoid prominent invented moments that reverse the meaning.

## Citation/translation review
Record exact verse reference for every quoted phrase; quotations must match declared Bible translation. Check copyright/licensing and required credit in the release phase; a historical repo policy alone is insufficient. Do not mix ESV prose quotation with invented KJV-like 'thee/thou'. When a translation differs on a key word, note it in the claim ledger.
