# Narration, score, sound and rights

## Narrative voice direction
- Pick one narrator identity for the season, with human-like phrasing, diction and appropriately variable emotional delivery; do not clone another creator's timbre.
- Script locked first; record a voice audition over the same 15-25s passage in 2-3 clearly different reads. Evaluate *listening*, not text or waveform alone.
- Prosody map: `emphasis`, `pause`, `urgency`, `understatement`, `intimacy`. Avoid artificial 'trailer voice' on every phrase.
- Keep exact audio and word timing transcript together; never repair word sync by rewriting approved narration silently.

## Sound scene logic
Three possible components: narration; environmental diegetic audio (oars, water, clothes, foot contact); score/texture and transitions. Every sound must have a function: orientation, contact, emotional tension/release, transition or depth. Don't put an impact on every popup. A silent moment can be deliberate and powerful.

## Practical mix chain (targets require listening, not arbitrary law)
1. Verify voice intelligibility alone, noise and plosives.
2. Set music under voice using listening in headphones, earbud and mobile speaker; automated sidechain compression can help but can pump audibly.
3. Keep low frequencies controlled: mobile speaker loses them. Use harmonics/transients to preserve a feeling of impact.
4. Avoid true-peak overs, harsh sibilance, clipped effects and muddy midrange.
5. Check integrated loudness, short-term loudness and true peak with ffmpeg/EBU R128 *against platform requirements at publication time*. Historical project target around -14 LUFS is an operational reference, not a universal rule.
6. Create stems for voice/music/SFX; archive rights and generation method.

## Soundboard for storm (hypotheses, not an approved mix)
- Scene geography: oar stroke + hull creak + controlled wind direction; thin and distant when camera is far.
- Presence: remove or narrow selected ambient bands when a figure becomes visible; subtle rather than supernatural sound cliche.
- Rescue: hand contact should be audible, but no theatrical boom that turns a human moment into a game effect.
- Mark's loaves: if narrated, a gentle sound/object callback to earlier bread passage is more meaningful than a thunder hit.
- Outro: transition to established quiet Fourth Watch stamp without a generic speech or stretched dead time.

## Permissions
External voice/audio jobs require exact user authorization and price cap. Keep rights ledger for original score, licensed tracks, recorded voices and any generated assets. Disclosure obligations vary by platform and content—check current official rules before release. Never say external service has rendered a file until verified.
