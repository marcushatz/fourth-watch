## 1. Why did God strike Uzzah dead for catching the ark? (23/25)

**Common answer:** "God is holy; you can't touch the ark." That's true, but it leaves the viewer thinking God punished a man for being helpful.

**Thread:** *how the ark was carried.*

**Discoveries, in order:**
1. **Uzzah reached out because the cart lurched.** "Uzzah put out his hand to take hold of the ark, for the oxen stumbled" (1 Chr 13:9 ✓). He was struck down "because he put out his hand to the ark" (13:10 ✓).
2. **The ark was never meant to be on a cart.** It was to be carried on Levites' shoulders: "to the sons of Kohath he gave none [no wagons], because they were charged with the service of the holy things that had to be carried on the shoulder" (Num 7:9 ✓). And "they must not touch the holy things, lest they die" (Num 4:15 ✓).
3. **The hinge: where did the cart idea come from? The Philistines.** When Israel's enemies sent the captured ark home, they said "prepare a new cart" (1 Sam 6:7 ✓). Israel copied the enemy's method: "they carried the ark of God on a new cart" (1 Chr 13:7 ✓).
4. **David's own verdict, three months later.** "Because you did not carry it the first time, the LORD our God broke out against us, because we did not seek him according to the rule" (1 Chr 15:13 ✓). The second time, "the Levites carried the ark of God on their shoulders with the poles, as Moses had commanded" (15:15 ✓).

**Answer:** Uzzah's touch was the last step of a bigger mistake. Israel handled God's presence the way the Philistines did, instead of the way God had said.

**Principle:** good intentions don't replace doing God's work God's way.

**Screenshot line:** "They learned how to carry God from people who didn't know Him."

**Risks:** the death must be shown reverently. "Uzzah was guilty" vs "the system was" needs care: the text says God struck him "because he put out his hand".

**Scores:** Originality 4 · Depth 5 · Curiosity 5 · Clarity 5 · Shareability 4.

## 2. Why did Jesus wait two days while Lazarus was dying? (24/25)

**Common answer:** "To do a bigger miracle." That's true, but it makes Jesus sound cold.

**Thread:** *the delay, and what it cost.*

**Discoveries:**
1. **The word "so".** "Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. **So**, when he heard that Lazarus was ill, he stayed two days longer" (John 11:5-6 ✓). John links the delay to his love, not to its absence.
2. **He said he was glad he wasn't there.** "for your sake I am glad that I was not there, so that you may believe" (11:15 ✓). The delay had a purpose for the people watching.
3. **He knew how it would end, and he still wept.** "Jesus wept" (11:35 ✓) comes after he has said he will raise him (11:11 (to fetch)). Knowing the ending didn't cancel the grief.
4. **The hinge: the miracle cost Jesus his life.** Some witnesses reported it to the Pharisees (11:46 ✓). "So from that day on they made plans to put him to death" (11:53 ✓). The plot then widened to Lazarus too (12:10 (to fetch)).

**Answer:** the wait wasn't indifference. John says it came *from* love, and the miracle he waited for set his own death in motion.

**Principle:** a delay isn't proof of distance. Jesus wept beside people while knowing the ending.

**Screenshot line:** "The miracle that saved Lazarus is the one that got Jesus killed."

**Risks:** "got Jesus killed" must stay accurate. John 11:53 says the plans began "from that day on". Say it as "set in motion", not as the sole cause.

**Scores:** Originality 4 · Depth 5 · Curiosity 5 · Clarity 5 · Shareability 5.

## 3. Why did Jesus ask Peter "Do you love me?" three times? (23/25)

**Common answer:** "Once for each denial." That's right, but it stops there.

**Thread:** *the fire.*

**Discoveries:**
1. **Peter denied Jesus beside a charcoal fire.** "the servants and officers had made a charcoal fire, because it was cold" (John 18:18 ✓). Peter was standing at it, warming himself.
2. **Jesus restored him beside a charcoal fire.** "they saw a charcoal fire in place, with fish laid out on it, and bread" (21:9 ✓). The Greek word for "charcoal fire" (*anthrakia*) appears in only these two places in the whole New Testament (Strong's 439 concordance ✓). The smell and the setting of his worst night, recreated by Jesus.
3. **Three questions for three denials, and it hurt.** "Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, 'Do you love me?'" (21:17 ✓).
4. **Each answer came with a job.** "Feed my lambs… Tend my sheep… Feed my sheep" (21:15-17 ✓). He wasn't just forgiven; he was put back to work.

**Answer:** Jesus didn't avoid Peter's failure. He rebuilt the scene of it, and turned each denial into a commission.

**Principle:** Jesus restores people at the place they failed, not around it.

**Screenshot line:** "Jesus lit the same kind of fire Peter failed beside."

**Risks:**
- "Jesus lit it" is an inference. The text says "they saw a charcoal fire in place", so say "Jesus had a charcoal fire waiting".
- The two-occurrence fact is a concordance fact, labeled TEXT (Greek), not interpretation.
- The agapaō/phileō debate is contested; leave it out.

**Scores:** Originality 4 · Depth 4 · Curiosity 5 · Clarity 5 · Shareability 5.

## 4. Why did Elijah ask to die right after his greatest victory? (23/25)

**Common answer:** "Burnout." That's true, but it misses what God did about it.

**Thread:** *God's answer to "I'm done".*

**Discoveries:**
1. **The request.** After Mount Carmel, Elijah "ran for his life", then prayed, "It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life" (1 Kgs 19:3-4 ✓).
2. **God's first answer was sleep and food,** twice (19:5-8 (to fetch)). There was no rebuke first.
3. **Then a question and a whisper.** "What are you doing here, Elijah?" (19:9 ✓). Then the wind, the earthquake and the fire, "but the LORD was not in" them, and "after the fire the sound of a low whisper" (19:11-12 ✓).
4. **Then a correction and company.** Elijah said "I, even I only, am left" (19:10 ✓). God answered "I will leave seven thousand in Israel" (19:18 ✓), and gave him Elisha (19:16 ✓).
5. **The hinge: the man who asked to die never died.** "Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven" (2 Kgs 2:11 ✓).

**Answer:** God didn't take Elijah's life. He gave him rest, a gentle word, the truth that he wasn't alone, a friend, and in the end a departure without death.

**Principle:** God's first answer to despair was rest and a whisper, not a lecture.

**Screenshot line:** "He asked God to take his life. God took him to heaven instead."

**Risks:** suicidal-despair content. Show it as despair, never as method, and add a caption resource line (Marcus's call). "Never died" must be worded as "Scripture records no death; he was taken up".

**Scores:** Originality 4 · Depth 5 · Curiosity 4 · Clarity 5 · Shareability 5.

## 5. Why did one mistake keep Moses out of the Promised Land? (21/25)

**Common answer:** "He hit the rock instead of speaking to it." That's right, but it sounds petty.

**Thread:** *two rocks, forty years apart.*

**Discoveries:**
1. **Forty years earlier, God told him to strike a rock** (Ex 17:6 (to fetch)).
2. **This time: "tell the rock before their eyes to yield its water"** (Num 20:8 ✓).
3. **What Moses actually said:** "Hear now, you rebels: shall we bring water for you out of this rock?", and he "struck the rock with his staff twice" (20:10-11 ✓). The "we" is the problem.
4. **God's stated reason:** "Because you did not believe in me, to uphold me as holy in the eyes of the people" (20:12 ✓). Psalm 106 says "he spoke rashly with his lips" (106:33 (to fetch)).
5. **The hinge: Moses does stand in the land later, beside Jesus.** "Moses and Elijah appeared to them, talking with him" (Matt 17:3 (to fetch)). This is INTERPRETATION: it's a common reading, and the location is "a high mountain", not named.

**Answer:** it wasn't the tap. In front of everyone, Moses took God's credit ("shall *we*") and treated God's word as optional. Leaders are judged by what their example teaches.

**Principle:** the more people watch you, the more your shortcuts teach.

**Screenshot line:** "He hit the rock, but the problem was the word 'we'."

**Risks:**
- The Transfiguration payoff is a reading. Either keep it labeled, or end on Deut 34:4 (God showed him the land).
- It sits one chapter before the Bronze Serpent (Numbers 20 → 21). That works well as a follow-up episode.

**Scores:** Originality 4 · Depth 4 · Curiosity 5 · Clarity 4 · Shareability 4.
