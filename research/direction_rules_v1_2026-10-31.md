# Fourth Watch: direction rules (how a script becomes shots)

**Status:** binding for every shot list from Ep 1 v58 on. `tools/shotlist_check.js` (before rendering) and `fw_check.py video` (after rendering) enforce the measurable rules. The rest go through the shot-list rubric in the showrunner skill.

**Evidence:**
- All 14 Lintel Network Shorts, measured shot by shot on Oct 31, 2026: 12 in `research/lintel_ref/yt/<id>.md` (summary in `research/lintel_ref/yt/_patterns.md`), plus Suffering and Methuselah in `research/lintel_ref/` (ref1/ref2).
- Instagram view counts as of Oct 31: Tree 7.6M, Cain's wife 1.8M, Never heard 1.6M, Suffering 528K, Judas 338K, Methuselah 325K, Hell 194K. The rest are 20K–75K.
- Marcus's verdict on Ep 1 v57 (Oct 31): "pacing, length, the motion, the camera, what was going on cinematically… these other accounts succeeded with coding the videos just like us… worse quality render but proper visual storytelling, camera angles, pacing, audio".

Numbers below are measured unless marked *(inference)*. With 12–14 videos and views from another platform, read them as patterns, not causes.

---

## 0. The one principle

**Every spoken clause gets a picture of that clause happening.**
- If the narration says a verb, a character does it on screen, on that word.
- If it states an idea, an object shows the idea and a 2–5 word pill names it.
- The caption building at the top is never the only thing that changes in a shot.

Ep 1 v57 broke this most often: Samson or Delilah standing, facing the camera, while the voice says what they did. The rig already had most of the actions (section 11). The failure was in the shot list, not the engine.

## 1. Rhythm

| Rule | Lintel numbers |
| --- | --- |
| One new picture per spoken sentence | 1.02 shots per sentence across 12 videos (hits 0.95–1.04) |
| A typical shot holds one clause: 3–5 s | all 265 shots: median 3.8 s, mean 4.1 s, middle half 2.5–5.1 s. Hits: 50% of shots are 3–5 s (lows 27%) |
| Shots under 2 s only for lists and parallel items, one shot per item (2–3 s shots are normal: 22% of all Lintel shots) | Tree "every war / every sickness" 1.7 / 1.5 s; Job "application / interview / door" 1.5 / 1.3 / 1.3 s; Exodus door painting 4 cuts of 0.5–1.3 s |
| Long shots (6–10 s) only for a verse card, one unbroken camera journey linking scenes, or a held diagram, and something must keep changing inside | Cain 9.2 s travel; Never heard 17.9 s diagram (the one >10 s, a building diagram); A&E 9.2 s quote card |
| Nothing over 10 s; nothing under 1 s except a 0.2 s flash transition | hits: 0 shots under 1 s |
| Avoid both extremes | the two lowest-rhythm videos are lows: Exodus median 1.8 s (80% ≤3 s), Hide median 6.2 s |
| Speech rate 2.6–3.0 words/s (156–180 wpm) | hits 2.6–3.0 words/s; the slowest videos (1.75–1.8 words/s) are both lows |
| Length 85–135 s for ASK THE TEXT | hits 84, 89 and 137 s; length alone did not separate hits from lows |

## 2. The hook (0–5.5 s)

1. **Frame one** is a person at medium size together with the topic's key object:
   - Eve beside the tree with the snake and the fruit
   - Cain beside a faceless wife with a "?"
   - a lone man before his lit house at night
2. **Something moves within about 1 s:** a reach, the "?" rising, a pull-out starting. Lows held a still picture for 4–9 s (Show, Hide, A&E).
3. **The question is fully spoken by 2.8–5.5 s.** Lows finished at 6.0–8.9 s.
4. **The question is the top caption** (italic serif, key words coloured), not a big centred title. 0 of 3 hits used a big title; 5 of 9 non-hits did. Our ON-SCREEN line is that caption, fully on screen from frame one.
5. **The first big camera move is a reveal that lands on the question's key clause.** Tree's pull-out (−42%, mostly in 1.2 s) lands on "why didn't He remove the tree". Never heard's pull-out (−64%) turns "never heard" into one lit house in the dark.
6. **Return to the hook image,** within 8 s (Cain 7.4 s) and/or when the answer lands (Tree 63.3 and 73.6 s; Never heard 44.9 and 122.5 s).
7. *(Inference)* The hits are puzzles people already search for, shown as a person plus an object. Pick questions that are already being asked.

## 3. Story lines (the narration says what someone did)

1. **One action per verb, on the verb.** A sentence with three verbs gets three beats on the same set, joined by soft cuts or time jumps.
   - Never heard 15.8–38.4 s:
     - "gave much to the poor": hands to a poor man
     - "prayed continually": he kneels
     - "an angel told him": a beam and the angel
     - "send for Peter": messengers walk off
     - "Peter walked into that house": Peter arrives and Cornelius bows
2. **One set per story run.** Change the framing, light or time of day, not the location: the Cornelius street (5 shots), the Eden garden (6 visits in Tree).
3. **Name pill on first appearance,** with the reference folded in ("CORNELIUS · ROMAN OFFICER", "SARAH · GEN 20:12").
4. **Quoted speech is a card over the speaker's scene, and the scene keeps acting:** Judas bargaining under "What will ye give me"; Adam pointing at Eve under "the woman… she gave me".
5. **Contrast characters by direction of travel:** Peter walks back toward Jesus, Judas walks away along the wall.
6. **Show counts, never just say them:** the coin counter ticking 21 → 30, three red tallies for three denials, ten glowing dots over Sodom.
7. **Walking is the workhorse verb:** about half of all action shots, toward or away from a light, a door or a person.
8. **God's action is a beam of light from above** (all 12 videos). Our accuracy rule limits where it goes:
   - Use it only where the text says God acted ("the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him", "the LORD had left him", where the light goes out).
   - Never use it to imply an event the text doesn't state, such as "God answered" at the pillars (Judges 16:28–30). There, show the prayer and the push, not a beam.

## 4. Idea lines (the narration states an idea)

1. **Act the hypothetical out literally:** a beam removes the tree; great-grandparents at the dinner table; Judas on Peter's shore.
2. **Turn the idea into one physical object, then do something to it:**
   - "sin waiting at the next command": a snake coiled at the next stepping stone
   - "no choice left": people become puppets on strings
   - "playing a part": a theatre stage
   - "accident of birth": dice
   - "judge fairly": a scale
   - "inheritance → choice": a lamp passed from an old woman to a young man, who walks off with it
3. **Show a consequence by subtraction:** things vanish (stall goods, the neighbour's animals, a village), and leaves fall off a man.
4. **Recurring symbols:** doors (in 6 of the 12 videos), forked or lit paths, light against dark.
5. **Verdicts get a stamp, strike or red X:** "PAID", "STILL WRONG", a red X on a wrong idea.
6. **Logic gets a simple diagram:** a timeline (ADAM → ABRAHAM → MOSES); two groups under one beam; two labels either side of one person.
7. **Social proof gets a UI mock-up:** a search bar autocompleting the question; comment bubbles with objections.
8. **Modern analogies get modern sets in the same style:** an office, a living room, a desk.
9. **Every abstract shot carries a 2–5 word pill** that pins the meaning ("NO CHOICE LEFT", "KNOWING IS NOT CAUSING", "HE NEVER FORCES THE DOOR").

## 5. Something changes in every shot

Change types, in rough order of use:
- a person acts
- an object appears, vanishes or transforms
- a pill or stamp lands
- a counter ticks
- light grows or dies
- the camera arrives somewhere new

In the hits, fewer than 1 scene shot in 10 is fully static (Tree: 2 of 20). A person visibly acts in 39% of all shots, and 46–57% in Tree and Never heard. Cain's wife (21%) shows that objects, labels and diagrams can carry a video too. A verse card's only change may be its text building, but it should sit over a scene that keeps acting.

## 6. Camera

- **Default framing:** a high-angle isometric wide on a small diorama set, 60–70% of shots. Medium shots and two-shots are for the hook and intimate beats.
- **Mostly still:** the camera is locked or drifting a few % in about 60% of shots. It moves ≥5% in 41% of shots, and ≥15% in only 14%.
- **Big moves have exactly four jobs:**
  1. the hook reveal (pull-out from a character to the world);
  2. travel that links scenes or time;
  3. following a walker;
  4. pushing into a revelation (Athens altar +22%; through the opening door +37%; candle +12%).
- **Transitions:**
  - a 0.2 s cream or white flash on a scene change, landing on the first word of the new clause;
  - or a 0.2–0.3 s crossfade (Cain uses 18);
  - one match-dissolve for a symbolic link (tree → cross);
  - a dip to dark before the end card.
- The total amount of camera movement did **not** separate hits from lows. Don't add motion for its own sake: give each big move one of the four jobs.

## 7. Text on screen

- **Narration caption:** the top third, italic serif, 2–3 lines building word by word, 1–3 key words per sentence in gold (ours).
- **Persistent header:** series and episode tag (ours: FOURTH WATCH · ASK THE TEXT … SAMSON).
- **Label pills:** about half of scene shots. They land 0.3–1 s after the cut and stay until the next cut. Fold the reference in.
- **Verse cards:**
  - median 4.35 s on screen; hits average 4.1 s, lows 5.7 s
  - keep ours at 3.5–5 s, up to 6.5 s only when the VO reads the whole verse
  - place the card in the top half over a scene that keeps acting, or full screen over the faded scene
  - always next to a shot of what the verse proves
- **No big centred titles,** in the hook or at section turns (only non-hits use them).
- **Numbered sections:** a red numeral pop plus a bottom counter pill held through the section (Never heard), when the format has points.

## 8. The ending

1. The answer line plays over an **"arrival" image**: people walking into a lit doorway, a crowd at the cross, the messenger reaching the house. Pair it with the **callback to the hook image**.
2. A 0.2–0.3 s flash or dip.
3. A **CTA card of about 5 s:**
   - the closing question with 2–3 option pills that pop on their words;
   - a mock comment field typing an example answer.
4. A **logo of 2–3 s** (1.9–2.0 s plain; 2.7–3.3 s with a tagline).
5. **Tail** (closing segment start to the end): 7–9 s. The hits ran 7.3, 8.2 and 8.7 s, and lows 9.2–15.1 s.
6. **No community card:** the three videos with a "Welcome to the Valiant" crest are all lows.

*Pending decision:* Marcus's calm Jesus outro (v3) runs 5.6 s in Ep 1, which pushes our tail to about 14 s. The data says 2–3 s. Ask before shortening it; Marcus asked for a slow, calm reveal.

## 9. What did not separate hits from lows

How much the camera moves, the number of verse cards (hits had 1, 4 and 6), video length (84–137 s), and art style (3D low-poly and flat figures both hit). Don't over-optimise these.

## 10. Audio (provisional: one Lintel reel with sound, "Suffering", 528K)

Measured with `tools/reference_study/audiostudy.py`: 50 ms levels against the 90th percentile of the voice band (300–3400 Hz).

| | Lintel Suffering | Our Ep 1 v57 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bass band (30–180 Hz), median | −7.6 dB | −19.5 dB |
| Bass band, loud moments (p90) | −1.4 dB | −8.0 dB |
| Whole-mix floor between words (p10) | −7.4 dB | −13.4 dB |
| Voice-band floor (p10) | −14.8 dB | −16.7 dB |
| Steady beat | none clear (pulse strength 0.07) | none clear (0.03) |
| Sharp onsets | 36/min | 36/min |

- **Reading:** their bed is a full, sustained, bass-heavy cinematic layer that sits about 12 dB stronger than ours in the low end. It fills the space under and between the words without competing with the voice band. Ours is thin by comparison.
- **What to change:** add a sustained low layer (a cello/bass drone, a low pad an octave down, soft low hits on scene changes) and keep the voice band clear.
- **Caveat:** this is one reel from a screen recording. Confirm on more reels before treating the numbers as targets.
- **Until then:** keep the music 9–10 dB under the voice in the voice band. Add one SFX for every on-screen action (snap, burst, coins, stamp, whoosh on a flash). Marcus judges by ear.

## 13. The process that enforces this

1. **Script loop** (showrunner §4):
   - Write for pictures: every sentence names a showable action or object (section 0).
   - Lists of parallel items are welcome.
   - Speech rate 2.6–3.0 words/s; length 85–135 s.
2. **Shot list** (showrunner §5) in the new format (`ep*/film*/score.js` fields `say`, `act`, `change`, `pill`, `cam`, `job`). Run `node tools/shotlist_check.js` until it passes, then get Marcus's approval.
3. **Motion draft:** render a low-res draft of the sections that changed (`node engine/render.js clip A B 2 --draft`) and watch it as video before the full render.
4. **Full render.** Then `fw_check.py video` checks the rhythm, static shots, hook, tail and length. Then the visual review.
