## What we want to be known for

**Bible stories that help people recognize their own struggles, discover meaningful truths in Scripture, and understand God more deeply.**

Our advantage is the quality of our insights and how memorably we tell them. It isn't expensive animation, artificial suspense or shallow motivational language.

## Relate → Reveal → Transform

Every episode should do three things:

| | What happens | What the viewer thinks |
|---|---|---|
| **Relate** | They recognize something from their own life | "I've experienced that." |
| **Reveal** | They discover something meaningful in Scripture that they had overlooked or misunderstood | "I never noticed that." |
| **Transform** | They leave with a clearer understanding of God, and something meaningful to consider or apply | "That changes how I think or live." |

This is a philosophy, not a formula. The three can overlap, and they don't have to arrive in that order or in separate sections. The story should feel natural, not manufactured.

**The human struggle is our entry point. Scripture is our foundation. Understanding God is our ultimate purpose.**

## Who it's for

We make videos for Christians, for people exploring Christianity, and for people who wouldn't normally watch religious content. They should feel understood without feeling manipulated.

Starting points include:
- insecurity and the desire for approval;
- fear of rejection;
- regret after a mistake;
- anxiety about the future;
- loneliness and feeling overlooked;
- temptation and self-control;
- betrayal and broken relationships;
- pride and the need to prove oneself;
- feeling inadequate or unqualified;
- struggling to forgive;
- waiting without understanding why;
- questions about identity, purpose and God.

These are starting points. They don't limit what Scripture we teach.

## What counts as value

Making viewers feel good isn't enough. A valuable video leaves them with new understanding that matters. That can come from:

1. an overlooked detail in a biblical narrative;
2. why a character made a decision, when the text supports it;
3. the historical or literary context of a passage;
4. a real connection between two passages;
5. correcting a common misunderstanding;
6. a difficult question explored without oversimplifying it;
7. a meaningful contrast between two biblical characters;
8. the consequences of someone's actions;
9. how a biblical teaching meets a recognizable modern struggle.

Never manufacture a revelation, present speculation as biblical truth, or exaggerate a passage to make it more interesting. If the best insight is already obvious, go deeper into the text rather than dressing a shallow idea up in dramatic language.

## The content pillars

| Pillar | Starting stories |
|---|---|
| Identity and self-worth | Gideon, Moses, David |
| Failure, shame and restoration | Peter, Adam and Eve, the prodigal son |
| Anxiety, fear and uncertainty | Elijah, the disciples in the storm, Jehoshaphat |
| Relationships, rejection and betrayal | Joseph and his brothers, David and Saul, Ruth and Naomi |
| Temptation, compromise and self-control | Samson, Joseph and Potiphar's wife, David and Bathsheba |
| Purpose, patience and waiting | Joseph in prison, David before he became king, Abraham |

The pillars overlap, and the list isn't exhaustive. Never force a story into a pillar when the text points somewhere else.

## How a strong episode is built

We prefer storytelling to lecturing. A strong episode generally has these five parts. The story decides their order and weight.

- **A compelling opening.** Open on a specific human struggle, contradiction, question or consequential event, made concrete and immediately recognizable. Not "Have you ever struggled with life?"
- **The biblical narrative.** Bring viewers into the actual story: what happened, who was involved, what was at stake and why the decisions mattered. Use specific details that create curiosity and involvement.
- **A meaningful discovery.** Show something the text reveals that deepens understanding. It is earned by the narrative and supported by Scripture.
- **A connection to everyday life.** Show how the same struggle appears today, without pretending modern circumstances are identical to the biblical event.
- **A memorable conclusion.** End on a specific insight, a thoughtful question or a practical application that grows out of the story. Never manufacture an emotional climax with dramatic, empty language.

## Scripture integrity is nonnegotiable

Interesting storytelling must never come at the expense of truth. For every script:

- Identify the primary passage, and read the whole narrative and its context.
- Verify every factual detail, and every quotation against the ESV in the same session.
- Investigate the relevant supporting passages.
- Keep what the text says separate from interpretation and illustration, in the claim table and in the narration itself.
- Make no unsupported claims about a character's private thoughts or God's motives.
- Never invent dialogue, and never distort Scripture to strengthen a hook.
- When condensing, keep the passage's meaning.

**Marcus's example:** Genesis 3:9 records God asking Adam, "Where are you?" Genesis 3:10 records Adam saying he was afraid. A video can investigate the meaning of God's question, but it must keep the text separate from any interpretation of why God asked it.

Every consequential claim carries one label (details in `.claude/skills/fourth-watch-writer/reference/verification.md`):

| Label | Meaning | How the narration may say it |
|---|---|---|
| TEXT | The passage says it | Plainly, naming the book when it matters |
| INFERRED | A fair inference from the text | "It seems," "this suggests," or a line that shows the reasoning |
| INTERPRETATION | A reading that interpreters hold and others may not | Attributed: "Many Christians read this as..." It never carries the episode alone |
| HISTORY | An outside fact from a named source | As history, never as Scripture |
| VISUAL | A picture or symbol, not a claim about events | Flagged in the plan if it could mislead |
| UNSUPPORTED | A motive, thought, feeling or detail the text doesn't give | Cut or rewritten |

## How we write

Scripts should be:
- specific rather than generic;
- emotionally meaningful rather than artificially dramatic;
- educational rather than merely motivational;
- accessible rather than unnecessarily theological;
- thought-provoking rather than argumentative;
- concise without losing the necessary context;
- faithful to Scripture rather than built around viral claims.

Avoid repetitive rhetorical questions, empty inspirational phrases and exaggerated descriptions of every moment. Don't keep telling viewers how they should feel. Let the events, choices, consequences and discoveries create the emotion. Write narration that sounds natural spoken aloud, and that someone who has never opened a Bible can follow on one listen.

## Developing an idea

Investigate several angles before choosing a hook. For each candidate, establish:

1. the relatable human struggle;
2. the specific biblical passage;
3. the narrative conflict;
4. the strongest text-supported insight;
5. what the viewer learns that they might not already know;
6. the emotional turning point;
7. the practical application;
8. any important interpretive uncertainties;
9. why the story is worth watching, rather than reading a generic inspirational quote.

Reject weak ideas even when their titles sound appealing. A strong hook without meaningful substance is not a successful episode. The card is `templates/idea_card.md`.

## Before any script is approved

Answer each question in words, pointing to the lines that prove it. There are no scores. A script never passes just because it follows a structure or meets a word count.

| Question | What a pass looks like |
|---|---|
| **Relatability** | Viewers will recognize a meaningful human experience |
| **Curiosity** | The opening gives a compelling reason to keep watching |
| **Narrative** | There is a clear sequence of meaningful events |
| **Discovery** | There is a genuine insight, supported by Scripture |
| **Clarity** | Someone unfamiliar with the Bible can follow the story |
| **Emotional impact** | The narrative earns its emotional moments |
| **Application** | The conclusion offers something specific and useful |
| **Biblical accuracy** | Every claim and interpretation is defensible |
| **Originality** | The episode is worth more than a generic Bible summary |
