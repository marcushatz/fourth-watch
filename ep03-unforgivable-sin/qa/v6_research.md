## Conclusions for the script
1. **The answer holds.** In Mark 3:30 the text ties the warning to the leaders' saying ("for they were saying, 'He has an unclean spirit'"). The views differ on whether the Pharisees had fully committed it (The Gospel Coalition: "on the brink"). So the answer stays at v5's careful wording: "tied to what those men were doing". It does NOT say "that IS the sin".
2. **"Two main ways" is a fair simplification.** The views cluster as (a) knowingly or maliciously calling the Spirit's clear work evil (Calvin, Wesleyan, the dispensational reading of the Pharisees' act, Catholic Encyclopedia) and (b) final impenitence, refusing God's mercy until death (Augustine, the Catechism, Billy Graham, Got Questions' "continued unbelief").
3. **"It isn't something done by accident" holds under every view found.** Each uses deliberate, knowing, malicious, hardened or lifelong language, and The Gospel Coalition says it outright.
4. **"Many pastors say ... that fear is a sign you haven't"** is stated by The Gospel Coalition and Got Questions and summarised by Wikipedia as the general Christian view. It's an attributed pastoral teaching, not Scripture, and the script attributes it. It's the one non-Scripture claim in the script.
5. **Considered and dropped: "even his own family said he was out of his mind" (Mark 3:21).** The "they were saying" in 3:21 is read by some as the family and by others as people in general. That's too contested to state as fact.
