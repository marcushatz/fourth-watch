**Pages opened (2):** esv.org Matthew 12:22-32 / Mark 3:22-30 (re-opened for 12:22, 12:25-26, Mark 3:23, 12:32) and esv.org 1 Timothy 1:13. Galatians 1:13 is from recall, not opened.

**L1: VERIFIED.** Matthew 12:32 reads "whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven, either in this age…". The quote is exact, and cutting the tail doesn't change the sense.

**L2: OVERCLAIM (first sentence only).**
- "Across traditions agree" is too strong. Calvin (via TGC), CCC 1864 (recall), the dispensational view and TGC itself all say deliberate. But TGC also lists an early-church view, with a Cyril of Jerusalem quote ("either from ignorance or assumed reverence"), that is less clearly deliberate. My earlier "holds for every view" glossed over that, so treat it as softened.
- Fix: "Most Christian explanations agree on one thing: it isn't something done by accident."
- The second sentence is VERIFIED. TGC: "a reliable sign that you have not committed it." gotquestions: "rest assured that you have not committed the unpardonable sin."

**L3: OVERCLAIM (minor).**
- Matthew 12:22 says "a demon-oppressed man who was blind and mute was brought to him, and he healed him." It never says he cast out a demon. That is implied only by the accusation in 12:24.
- The quote "the prince of demons" is exact.
- Fix: "Jesus had healed a demon-oppressed man who was blind and mute, and the Pharisees said it was 'only by Beelzebul, the prince of demons.'"

**L4: OVERCLAIM.**
- "How can Satan cast out Satan?" appears only in Mark 3:23. It is addressed to "the scribes who came down from Jerusalem" (3:22), and Mark has no blind and mute man.
- Matthew 12:26 is a statement, not a question: "if Satan casts out Satan, he is divided against himself."
- As written, the join gives Mark's words to Matthew's Pharisees.
- Fix: "In Mark's version of the same charge, Jesus asks, 'How can Satan cast out Satan?' And Mark says why he warned them: 'for they were saying, "He has an unclean spirit."'"

**L5: VERIFIED as paraphrase.**
- It is grounded in Matthew 12:28 and 12:31 and Mark 3:30, and "in other words" flags it as inference.
- Optional: the bridge that Jesus said his work was "by the Spirit of God" no longer comes before this line. If you restore it, keep the "if" from Matthew 12:28.

**L6: VERIFIED.** Matthew 12:32: "whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven." Reading "Son of Man" as Jesus is the standard reading.

**L7: OVERCLAIM.**
- "In every case" reaches past the two views named and past the L2 caveat.
- Fix: "In each of these, it isn't something done by accident."
- "God's clear work" is acceptable.

**L8: VERIFIED, with one nit.**
- 1 Timothy 1:13 reads "though formerly I was a blasphemer, persecutor, and insolent opponent. But I received mercy because I had acted ignorantly in unbelief." "Blasphemer" and "But I received mercy" are exact.
- Galatians 1:13 says "persecuted the church of God" (recall), so "persecuted Christians" is a fair paraphrase.
- Nit: the quote stops mid-sentence with the period inside the quotation marks. Use "But I received mercy…" as the original did, so the ignorance clause isn't silently dropped.

**Answer metadata: OVERCLAIM.**
- "In every Christian view" has the same problem as L2 and L7.
- Fix: "…and in the main Christian explanations the sin is deliberate, never an accident."
- The quotes ("the prince of demons"; "they were saying, 'He has an unclean spirit'") are exact.

**Follow-up on L3 in plain words** ("Jesus had healed a man who was blind and mute and troubled by a demon. The Pharisees said he cast out demons by 'the prince of demons.'"):

VERIFIED. Matthew 12:22 says "a demon-oppressed man who was blind and mute was brought to him, and he healed him," so "troubled by a demon" is a fair plain-word rendering. It doesn't state a casting-out as fact. "the prince of demons" is exact from 12:24. Dropping "only" and "Beelzebul" doesn't change the accusation.
