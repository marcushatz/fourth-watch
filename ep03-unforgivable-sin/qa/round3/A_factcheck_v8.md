**Pages opened (2):** esv.org Mark 14:66-72, and esv.org John 20:1-29 / 21:1-24, which covers 21:14-17. I found the URLs by WebSearch.

**N1: VERIFIED.**
- Mark 3:30 ("for they were saying…") gives the reason for the warning, and Matthew 12:28 supplies "the Spirit of God."
- "Men" is a safe word, since Mark names scribes and Matthew names Pharisees. It doesn't say they committed the sin.

**N2: VERIFIED.**
- Mark 14:68, 70 and 71 are three denials. In the third, Peter "began to invoke a curse on himself and to swear" and says "I do not know this man of whom you speak." Your truncation with "…" is fine, and "calling down a curse on himself" fairly renders "invoke a curse on himself."
- "The night Jesus was arrested" rests on Mark 14:30 "this very night" and the same-night trial. I know that from recall, and it wasn't on the page I opened.

**N3: words VERIFIED, placement OVERCLAIM.**
- John 21:14 says this is after he "was raised from the dead." The question is "Simon, son of John, do you love me?" three times, with "more than these" added only the first time. "Feed my sheep" is 21:17, after "Feed my lambs" and "Tend my sheep."
- The text never uses "forgive" in John 21, and never calls Peter's denial a "word against the Son of Man." Putting Peter right after "words against Jesus himself will be forgiven" implies both. The three questions echoing three denials is a common reading, not something the text states.
- Fix: "After Jesus rose, he asked Peter three times, 'Do you love me?' and each time gave him a charge, ending with 'Feed my sheep.'" Don't say "forgiven" or "restored" as fact.

**N4: OVERCLAIM (light).**
- It fits CCC 1864 (recall): "no limits to the mercy of God… anyone who deliberately refuses to accept his mercy." It also fits Got Questions' "continued unbelief" and TGC's rejection of the "really bad sin" idea.
- It fits less well for TGC's own view, which is a decisive rejection by attributing Jesus' works to Satan. It fits poorly for Got Questions' account of the original sin, which it says "cannot be duplicated today."
- "Many" is defensible, but a safer phrasing is: "Christians explain it in a few ways, but one common way to put it is: it isn't a sin too big for God to forgive. It's forgiveness someone keeps refusing."

**N5: VERIFIED.** CCC says "deliberately," Calvin says "evil intention," and TGC says "not an accidental, impulsive, or unguarded slip." "Most" correctly leaves room for the early-church view I flagged earlier.

**Answer metadata: OVERCLAIM.**
- "It isn't done by accident" is stated flat, right after "In the text," but the text doesn't say it. That is interpretation.
- Fix: "Most Christian explanations agree it isn't done by accident, and one common way to explain why it can't be forgiven is: it isn't a sin too big for God to forgive; it's forgiveness someone keeps refusing."
- The quote "they were saying, 'He has an unclean spirit'" is exact.
