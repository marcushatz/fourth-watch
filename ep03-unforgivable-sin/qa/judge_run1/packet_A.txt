### S02
Where did Cain's wife come from? If Adam and Eve were the first people and Cain and Abel were their sons, who did Cain marry? It's one of the most asked questions about the Bible and most Christians can't answer it. After Cain killed Abel, he settled in the land of Nod and the Bible says Cain knew his wife and she conceived. But notice, it never says he met her in Nod. She just appears. The clue is one chapter later. Adam begat sons and daughters and lived 930 years. Cain and Abel weren't the only children. They're just the ones the story follows and Eve is called the mother of all living, which means Cain married a sister or another close relative. It sounds wrong to us, but the law against it came much later through Moses. Even Abraham married his half-sister Sarah. In the beginning, there was only one family. Some say God made other people outside Eden. Genesis never says that. Paul says God hath made of one blood all nations of men. One blood. Every person you've ever met is family. The same family that fell in a garden is the family Christ came to save. So the next time someone asks you where Cain's wife came from, you'll know what to say.

### S01
What if Adam and Eve had never eaten from the tree? What would our world look like today? with the first consequence God named. In the day that thou eatest thereof, thou shalt surely die. Your great-grandparents would still sit at your dinner table, and grief would be a word humanity never needed to invent. But death was only the first thing to change. Adam had work before he ever sinned. The curse did not create labor. It turned the ground against the laborer, answering effort with thorns. Work in that world would feel like tending something you love. The change at home would be even more striking. When God confronted Adam, he answered, 'The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree.' Blame entered the world in the same breath as sin, and every broken relationship since carries its fingerprints. Yet even that is not the greatest loss. God once walked in the garden in the cool of the day, as naturally as a neighbor at sunset. Every prayer you have ever prayed is an attempt to reach across a distance that was never meant to be there. C. S. Lewis once wrote, 'If I find in myself a desire which no experience in this world can satisfy, the most probable explanation is that I was made for another world. That longing you carry for something better may be the memory of Eden. If you had been born into that world, which part of today's world would you be most glad to leave behind? Tell us in the comments, and welcome to The Valiant.

### S04
If God knew Eve would eat the fruit, why didn't He remove the tree from the garden? He knew Adam and Eve would disobey. He knew every war and every sickness that would follow, and still He left the tree of the knowledge of good and evil standing in the middle of Eden. If God had taken the tree away and Eve never touched it, sin would still be waiting at the very next command. God says, 'Thou shalt not steal.' So, He would have to take away everything you could ever be tempted to take. He says, 'Thou shalt not covet.' So, He would have to hide every good thing your neighbour owns. For every law He gave, He would have to clear away every way to break it. That wouldn't be obedience at all. It would be a world with no choice left in it. God never wanted people who obey because they can't do anything else. He wanted children who love Him freely. 'I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing. Therefore, choose life.' So God gave us free will, and He tells us plainly which way to choose. And God was ready for our wrong choice before we ever made it. Sin came in through a tree in a garden, and Jesus paid for it on a second tree, the cross. So, why didn't God remove the tree? Because love has to be chosen, and God made a way back for everyone who chooses Him. Do you think free will is a gift or a burden? Tell us why in the comments.

### S09
Why do we hide away? Why do we hide from God after we sin? After Adam and Eve sinned, God came looking for them, and they hid. If we're honest, we do the same thing. You mess up, and suddenly you don't feel like praying. You skip church. You avoid your Bible. Not because you stopped believing, because you don't think you can face Him. Look at what they did first. The Bible says their eyes were opened. They knew that they were naked, and they sewed fig leaves together. Their first instinct wasn't to run to God. It was to cover themselves. Then they hid among the trees. But here's the question we don't ask. Did God need to find them? He's God. He knew exactly where they were. So why call out, 'Where art thou?' It wasn't a search. It was an invitation. He wasn't asking for their location. He was asking them to come out. And Adam gave the answer we still give today. I was afraid because I was naked, and I hid myself. That's what shame does. It tells you to hide until you've fixed yourself. But you can't fix it with fig leaves. Now, watch what God does next. Before they leave the garden, He makes them coats of skins and clothes them. They tried to cover themselves with leaves. God covered them with something that cost a life. That's the first covering in the Bible and it points to the last one. While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. Not after we cleaned up, while. So, if you've been hiding from God because of what you did, He already knows. He's not coming to shame you. He's coming to cover you. Stop sewing fig leaves. Come out from the trees.

### S03
What is the unforgivable sin? In Matthew 12, Jesus said one sin "will not be forgiven, either in this age or in the age to come." Many people fear they've done it by accident. But look at what had just happened. Jesus healed a demon-oppressed man who was blind and mute, and the Pharisees said he cast out demons only by "Beelzebul, the prince of demons." Then came the warning, and it starts with forgiveness: "every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven people." Even words against Jesus himself: "whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven." Mark tells the same scene and gives the reason Jesus said it: "for they were saying, 'He has an unclean spirit.'" But Jesus said he cast out demons "by the Spirit of God." That's what he was warning them about. Christians read it two main ways: knowingly calling the Spirit's clear work evil, as those men were doing, or a final, lifelong refusal of the Spirit's call to repent. Either way, it isn't something done by accident. And who received mercy? Paul wrote that he was once "a blasphemer, persecutor, and insolent opponent. But I received mercy because I had acted ignorantly in unbelief…" Peter denied Jesus three times. The last time, he swore, "I do not know this man…" Later Jesus asked him three times, "Do you love me?" and told him, "Feed my sheep." So what is the unforgivable sin? In the text, it's tied to what those men were doing: calling the Spirit's work the devil's. And in the same breath, Jesus holds out forgiveness for everything else, even words against himself. What surprised you more: that the warning starts with forgiveness, or why Jesus gave it?

### S06
Was Judas a bad person or the hero who made salvation possible? In 2006, a 1,700-year-old text called the Gospel of Judas made headlines around the world because some scholars said it showed Judas only did what Jesus asked him to do. But listen to what Jesus Himself said about Judas the night before He died. 'Woe unto that man by whom the Son of Man is betrayed. It had been good for that man if he had not been born. Nobody speaks that way about a man who was only playing a part he was given. The Old Testament did say the Messiah would be betrayed by a close friend, but it never named Judas. John tells us Judas was already a thief, stealing from the money bag he kept for the disciples. Then Judas walked to the chief priests himself and asked, 'What you give me, and I will deliver Him unto you?' The deal was his idea, and he settled for 30 pieces of silver. God knew what Judas would choose, but knowing something is not the same as causing it. Peter later preached that Jesus was handed over by God's plan, yet taken by wicked hands. Life works the same way. A colleague lies about you, and you lose your job. Then years later, God opens a far better door. You'll thank God for the new door, but that will never make the lie right. That same night, Peter denied Jesus three times. Peter went out and wept bitterly, and then he came back. While Judas felt remorse, but never returned to the one who could have forgiven him. So, was Judas a bad person or only an instrument? He was a man who made his own choice, and God turned even that betrayal into our salvation. If Judas had come back to Jesus the way Peter did, do you think Jesus would have forgiven him? Tell us in the comments.

### S05
What happens to people who never heard of Jesus? If Jesus is the only way to the Father, what about a man born in a remote village who died without ever hearing His name? People ask this in our comments all the time. So, let's allow the Bible to answer it. In Caesarea, there lived a Roman officer named Cornelius, a Gentile who had never heard the gospel preached. Yet, the Bible says he feared God with all his house, gave much to the poor, and prayed to God continually. One afternoon, an angel told him to send for a man called Peter, who was lodging by the sea in Joppa. When Peter walked into that house, he said, 'God is no respecter of persons, but in every nation, he that feareth Him is accepted with Him. So the first answer is this: God saw a man reaching for Him with the little light he had, and He sent the gospel straight to his door. The second answer is that no one is born without any light at all. Paul writes that God's eternal power is clearly seen in creation, and that His law is written on the hearts of people who never had the scriptures. When Paul walked through Athens, he found an altar carved with the words 'to the unknown God,' built by people reaching for a God they couldn't name. The third answer is that God will judge every person fairly by what they were given. Jesus said, 'Unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required.' Centuries earlier, Abraham pleaded with God for the city of Sodom, and asked, 'Shall not the judge of all the earth do right?' God agreed to spare the whole city if even 10 righteous people could be found inside it. Christians still disagree on exactly how God deals with those who never heard. Some believe a person must hear of Jesus and trust Him in this life, while others believe Christ can save people who never learned His name. But every one of those views agrees that no one is saved except through Jesus. Then, Paul asks a question that puts the weight back on our shoulders. How shall they hear without a preacher? So the question isn't only what happens to people who never heard of Jesus, it's also who is going to tell them. Think of the person who first told you about Jesus and say thank you to them.

### S10
God is love, why is there a hell? And why would a loving God send anyone there forever? Some Christians also wonder about this question sometimes. First, ask who hell was made for. Jesus called it everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his angels. It was never made for you or for me. So why does anyone end up there? Second, look at who is working to take people there. Jesus said, 'The thief cometh not but for to steal and to kill and to destroy. I am come that they might have life.' The enemy wants you lost and Jesus came to do the opposite. Then, does God force anyone either way? Look at how the Bible ends. In Revelation, Jesus says, 'Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If any man hear my voice and open the door, I will come in.' He knocks, but He never kicks the door in. And the Bible's final invitation reads, 'Whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.' Third, the price has already been paid. God is not willing that any should perish. So, Jesus carried the judgment for us on the cross. Why is there a hell then? Because evil will finally be judged and the devil with it. But God has opened a way out for every one of us and He is still knocking. So, tell us, if Jesus is knocking, what keeps people from opening the door? Let us know in the comments.

### S07
Why would God tell a whole nation to paint blood on their doors? 400 years after entering Egypt as guests, the Hebrews were millions. Fearing an uprising, Pharaoh enslaved them and ordered their baby boys thrown into the Nile. One survived, Moses. Decades later, Moses demanded their freedom. Pharaoh refused, ignoring nine catastrophic plagues that brought Egypt to its knees. Then came the final warning. At midnight, the firstborn in every house would die, those who marked their doors with the blood of a spotless lamb. At death struck the nation, but wherever the blood was applied, it passed over. That very night, Pharaoh yielded, ending four centuries of slavery. Why blood on a doorway? Because it was the only thing standing between a family and death. And the top beam they struck first? ### S08
If God exists, why doesn't He show Himself to everyone once and for all? Philosophers call it divine hiddenness. And JL Schellenberg argues that a loving God would never leave a sincere seeker unsure. It sounds convincing until you open the book of Exodus. Israel watched the sea split and heard God's voice thunder from Sinai. And within weeks, they were dancing around a golden calf. Seeing God did not make them love Him. Jesus said as much. If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead. So why does God hold back? Søren Kierkegaard told a parable about a king who loved a humble maiden. Arriving in royal splendour would only make her bow in fear. So He came to her as a servant to win her heart freely. Christianity says God did exactly that and His name is Jesus. He that hath seen me hath seen the Father. So God has shown Himself. The question is what we did when He came. Pascal put it this way. There is enough light for those who only desire to see and enough obscurity for those who have a contrary disposition. God reveals enough to draw a willing heart, and He hides enough to leave love free. And Jesus said, 'Blessed are they that have not seen and yet have believed.' Would seeing God with your own eyes make it easier to trust Him or harder to love Him freely? Share your thoughts in the comments. And if you're here to stand for God's word, welcome to the Valiant.
