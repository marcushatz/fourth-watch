### What changed after round 5

- **The hero case comes from the story now.** Both round-5 viewers called the Hebrews line the weakest, a verdict "stapled on", so it is gone from the voice-over (Hebrews stays in the caption). Instead, the prayer itself is quoted: "please remember me" in the same breath as revenge "for my two eyes", with the whole verse on a card (Judges 16:28).
- **The mission is set up in the first ten seconds.** Before his birth, his mission was to "begin to save Israel" from the Philistines (13:5), and he married one. "His mission was God's" now pays that off, and it answers the pop-culture viewer's question, "what did his fight do for his people?": he was sent to begin the rescue, not finish it.
- **The famous part is named, not skipped.** "Delilah had his head shaved." Both viewers said leaving the hair out read like a gap.
- **Fact-check fixes:** "thirty men of Ashkelon", because the text says "men of the town", not "Philistines". "His people gave up. He didn't." is gone, because he agreed to be tied up (15:12) and said he fought for revenge (15:7).

**Still open:** whether "hero" is the right word. Every viewer so far rated it 3/5 or lower, and three of them, separately, said the evidence makes him "God's weapon" rather than a hero. The quoted prayer is aimed at that, but no viewer has heard it yet. One fact-checked option keeps both: "Hero or villain? A flawed hero, and God's weapon. His motives were his own. His mission was God's." It adds about a second. "Neither: God's weapon" was ruled out, because Hebrews counts him among those "commended through their faith" (11:39).
