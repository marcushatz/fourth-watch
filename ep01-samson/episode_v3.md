# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 50
answer: His failures were his own. His strength was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?
VISUAL: Blind Samson between two huge stone pillars, a hand on each; a crack climbs one pillar; crowds on the roof above.

## Stakes
VO: Most think his strength was in his hair. It came from God. [Judges 16:17, 16:20]
VISUAL: Close on Samson's seven long locks in silhouette; on "It came from God" the camera rises off the hair to a shaft of gold light above him.

## Story
VO: Three times, the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him. [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
VISUAL: Three gold rushes of light from the top left, one per cut: he tears apart a young lion with nothing in his hand; he strides toward the walls of Ashkelon in gold light; the ropes on his arms burn away like flax. The scale appears at the top of the frame; each rush drops a weight of gold light into the left pan.
VO: His weakness? His eyes. [Judges 14:1, 16:1]
VISUAL: Extreme close-up on Samson's eyes; slow push-in.
VO: "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." [Judges 14:3]
VERSE: "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." — Judges 14:3 (ESV)
VISUAL: In Timnah, Samson points toward a Philistine woman (screen right) while his father and mother (screen left) object.
VO: That's how Judges ends: everyone did what was right in his own eyes. [Judges 21:25]
VISUAL: The camera pulls back and rises from Samson to a whole village at dusk; dozens of small figures, each turned their own way.
VO: He saw a prostitute. He told Delilah everything. [Judges 16:1, 16:17]
VISUAL: Night in Gaza: Samson walks toward a lamp-lit doorway; the door closes. Then Delilah in the foreground as Samson tells her. Each time, Samson's own hand drops an ember weight onto the right pan.
VO: His head was shaved, and the Lord had left him. [Judges 16:19–20]
VISUAL: He sleeps on Delilah's knees; a man shaves off seven locks, which fall onto the right pan. The gold light drains up out of the left pan and the spotlight above him goes dark; the scale slams to the right.
VO: The man who lived by his eyes lost his eyes. [Judges 16:21]
VISUAL: Philistine silhouettes close in from screen right; push in on his face; hard cut to black on "lost his eyes". Then the prison mill: eyes closed, bronze shackles, short new hair catching the light.

## Turn
VO: Blind, he prayed, "Please strengthen me only this once." [Judges 16:25–28]
VERSE: "…please strengthen me only this once…" — Judges 16:28 (ESV)
VISUAL: An extreme wide of the packed house, about 3,000 on the roof; a young man leads Samson to the two middle pillars. Then close on his face lifted upward, one hand on each pillar; the camera holds completely still.
VO: And the house fell. [Judges 16:30]
VISUAL: He bows with all his strength; the pillars give; the roof comes down; dust fills the frame. As he bows, a beam of gold light returns into the left pan.
VO: Yet the New Testament lists him among the faithful. [Hebrews 11:32]
VISUAL: First light: the dust settles into dawn over the ruins; the camera rises into the warm sky.

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? His failures were his own. His strength was God's. [Judges 14:3, 16:17, 16:20, 16:28]
VISUAL: The scale in full: the ember weights on the right pan are the ones Samson's hand placed; the left pan is lit by a beam from above. On "His strength was God's" the camera rises up the beam into the light, then dips back to the opening frame.

## Value
- His strength came from God, not his hair: three times "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him," and when his head was shaved, "the LORD had left him" [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14, 16:20]
- He chose by his eyes, "she is right in my eyes", the same words the book of Judges ends on [Judges 14:3, 21:25]
- The man who lived by his eyes lost his eyes [Judges 14:1, 16:1, 16:21]
- His last prayer asked God for strength "only this once", and the house fell [Judges 16:28–30]
- The New Testament still lists him among the faithful [Hebrews 11:32]
