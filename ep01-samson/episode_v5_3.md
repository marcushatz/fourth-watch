# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 57
answer: A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?

## Stakes
VO: Before Delilah, the Philistines ruled Israel, and he married one. [Judges 13:1, 14:1–4]

## Story
VO: To pay a wedding bet, he killed thirty men for their clothes. [Judges 14:10–19]
VO: His wife was given away, so he burned Philistine fields with 300 foxes. [Judges 14:20, 15:1–5]
VO: The Philistines burned her and her father. [Judges 15:6]

## Turn
VO: Yet the Bible says God was behind that marriage, "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." [Judges 14:4]
VERSE: "…it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." — Judges 14:4 (ESV)
VO: 3,000 Israelites tied him up for the Philistines. Freed by the Spirit, he killed a thousand. [Judges 15:9–16]
VO: Betrayed and blinded, he prayed for revenge "for my two eyes." [Judges 16:18–21, 16:28]
VO: God gave him strength, and he died bringing the building down. [Judges 16:28–30]
VO: Hebrews 11 names him in a list. Through faith they "were made strong out of weakness." [Hebrews 11:32–34]
VERSE: "were made strong out of weakness" — Hebrews 11:34 (ESV)

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's. [Judges 13:5, 14:4, 15:11, 16:28; Hebrews 11:32–34]

## Value
- In Samson's day the Philistines ruled Israel, and he married one of them [Judges 13:1, 14:1–4]
- He killed thirty men for their clothes to pay off a bet he lost at his own wedding [Judges 14:12–19]
- His wife was given to another man; his revenge with 300 foxes led to her and her father being burned [Judges 14:20, 15:1–6]
- The Bible says the marriage that started it all "was from the LORD", who was "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines" [Judges 14:4]
- 3,000 of his own people tied him up to hand him to the Philistines [Judges 15:9–13]
- Blinded, he prayed for revenge "for my two eyes", and God gave him the strength [Judges 16:28–30]
- Hebrews 11 lists him among those who through faith "were made strong out of weakness" [Hebrews 11:32–34]

## Caption
Hero or villain? The answer is in Judges 13–16 and Hebrews 11:32–34. Read it yourself.
Three more things most people miss:
1. His own explanation for the revenge: "As they did to me, so have I done to them" (Judges 15:11).
2. Very thirsty after that battle, he gave God the credit: "You have granted this great salvation by the hand of your servant" (Judges 15:18).
3. He scraped honey out of the dead lion and fed it to his parents without telling them where it came from (Judges 14:8–9).
Send this to someone who only knows the Delilah part.
