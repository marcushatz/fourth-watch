# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 52
answer: A flawed hero. His failures were his own. His strength was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?
VISUAL: Blind Samson between two huge stone pillars, a hand on each; a crack climbs one pillar; crowds on the roof above.

## Stakes
VO: Most think his strength was in his hair. [Judges 16:17]
VISUAL: Close on Samson's seven long locks in silhouette, back-lit; slow push-in toward the hair.

## Story
VO: But three times, the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him. [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
VISUAL: Three gold rushes of light from the top left, one per cut: he tears apart a young lion with nothing in his hand; at Ashkelon a wave of gold light throws a line of Philistine silhouettes back; the ropes on his arms burn away like flax. The scale appears at the top of the frame; each rush drops a weight of gold light into the left pan.
VO: His weakness? His eyes. [Judges 14:1, 16:1]
VISUAL: Extreme close-up on Samson's eyes; slow push-in.
VO: He demanded a Philistine wife. "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." [Judges 14:1–3]
VERSE: "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." — Judges 14:3 (ESV)
VISUAL: At home, his father and mother object; Samson points off toward Timnah (screen right), where a Philistine woman stands in ember-red. His own hand drops an ember weight onto the right pan.
VO: The book of Judges ends on that exact phrase. [Judges 21:25]
VISUAL: The camera pulls back and rises from Samson to a whole village at dusk; dozens of small figures, each turned their own way.
VO: He told Delilah his hair marked him as set apart to God. [Judges 13:5, 16:17]
VISUAL: Lamp-lit room; Delilah in the foreground as Samson tells her, touching his long hair. His own hand drops a second ember weight onto the right pan.
VO: She had it shaved, and the Lord had left him. [Judges 16:19–20]
VISUAL: He sleeps on Delilah's knees; a man shaves off seven locks. Samson wakes and rises to fight; only then, on "the Lord had left him", the gold light drains up out of the left pan and the light above him goes dark. The scale slams to the right.
VO: The man who lived by his eyes lost his eyes. [Judges 16:21]
VISUAL: Philistine silhouettes close in from screen right; push in on his face; hard cut to black on "lost his eyes". Then the prison mill: eyes closed, bronze shackles, grinding.

## Turn
VO: Blind, he prayed for strength to avenge his two eyes. [Judges 16:25–28]
VISUAL: An extreme wide of the packed house, about 3,000 on the roof; a young man leads Samson to the two middle pillars. Then close on his face lifted upward, one hand on each pillar; the camera holds completely still; a faint gold light touches his hands.
VO: And the house fell. [Judges 16:30]
VISUAL: He bows with all his strength; the pillars give; the roof comes down; dust and darkness fill the frame. No gold on the collapse.
VO: The New Testament lists him among the faithful. [Hebrews 11:32]
VISUAL: First light: at dawn his brothers and family carry him home from the ruins to his father's tomb.

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? A flawed hero. His failures were his own. His strength was God's. [Judges 14:3, 16:17, 16:20, 16:28; Hebrews 11:32]
VISUAL: The scale: on "His failures were his own" the ember weights his hand placed glow on the right pan; on "His strength was God's" a shaft of light falls on the left pan from above. Then a dip back to the opening frame.

## Value
- His strength came from God, not his hair: three times "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him" [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
- His hair marked him as set apart to God; when it was shaved, "the LORD had left him" [Judges 13:5, 16:17, 16:19–20]
- He chose by his eyes, "she is right in my eyes", the phrase the book of Judges ends on [Judges 14:3, 21:25]
- The man who lived by his eyes lost his eyes, and his last prayer was to avenge them [Judges 16:21, 16:28]
- The New Testament still lists him among the faithful [Hebrews 11:32]
