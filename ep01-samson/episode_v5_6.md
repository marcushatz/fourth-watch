# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 57
answer: A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?

## Stakes
VO: Before his birth, God gave him a mission: "begin to save Israel" from the Philistines. He married one. [Judges 13:3–5, 14:1–4]

## Story
VO: To pay a wedding riddle bet, he killed thirty men of Ashkelon for their clothes. [Judges 14:10–19]
VO: His wife was given to another man, so he burned Philistine fields with 300 foxes. [Judges 14:20, 15:1–5]

## Turn
VO: Yet the Bible says God was behind that marriage, "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." [Judges 14:4]
VERSE: "…it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." — Judges 14:4 (ESV)
VO: 3,000 Israelites, resigned to Philistine rule, tied him up for the Philistines. God's Spirit freed him. [Judges 15:9–14]
VO: Delilah had his head shaved. Blinded, he prayed, "Please remember me," and asked for revenge "for my two eyes." [Judges 16:19–21, 16:28]
VERSE: "O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes." — Judges 16:28 (ESV)
VO: God answered. The building fell on the Philistine lords, all the people, and him. [Judges 16:28–30]

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's. [Judges 13:5, 14:4, 15:11, 16:28]

## Value
- Before his birth, God gave him a mission, to "begin to save Israel" from the Philistines, and he married one of them [Judges 13:3–5, 14:1–4]
- He killed thirty men of Ashkelon for their clothes to pay off a riddle bet he lost at his own wedding [Judges 14:12–19]
- His wife was given to another man, so he burned Philistine fields with 300 foxes [Judges 14:20, 15:1–5]
- The Bible says the marriage that started it all "was from the LORD", who was "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines" [Judges 14:4]
- His own people had accepted Philistine rule ("Do you not know that the Philistines are rulers over us?"), so 3,000 of them tied him up for the Philistines [Judges 15:9–13]
- Blinded, he prayed "please remember me" in the same breath as revenge "for my two eyes"; God answered, and the building fell "upon the lords and upon all the people" [Judges 16:21–30]

## Caption
Hero or villain? The answer is in Judges 13–16 and Hebrews 11. Read it yourself.
Four more things most people miss:
1. The Philistines answered the foxes by burning his wife and her father (Judges 15:6).
2. His own explanation for the revenge: "As they did to me, so have I done to them" (Judges 15:11).
3. After striking down 1,000 men with a donkey's jawbone, he boasted. Then, very thirsty, he gave God the credit: "You have granted this great salvation by the hand of your servant" (Judges 15:15–18).
4. Hebrews 11 lists him among those who through faith "were made strong out of weakness" (Hebrews 11:32–34).
