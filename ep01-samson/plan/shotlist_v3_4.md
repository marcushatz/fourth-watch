- **Two lights instead of a scale.**
  - **Gold from above is what God did.** It comes with each rush of the Spirit. A faint gold rim then stays on him through his failures, constant and never brighter on them, because the Spirit kept coming after Timnah (14:19, 15:14) and his strength outlasted Gaza (16:1–3). It fades as the last lock falls while he sleeps (16:19), so he wakes into the dark not knowing (16:20). It touches his hands after the prayer and falls on him at the verdict.
  - **Ember red is his own desires and choices.** It only ever sits on Samson: the reflection in his eyes, his face in Delilah's lamplight, his face and hands at the verdict.
  - Torches and cities stay neutral, and the woman at Timnah is lit naturally (Judges 14:4 says the match "was from the LORD"). The burial dawn is natural daylight, not the Spirit's gold.
  - There is no balance or weighing anywhere: the verdict rests on what the text says, not a tally of deeds.
- **The hair never glows.** The gold changes only when the text says the Spirit came or the Lord left, so the picture never suggests the hair itself was the power.
- **Hair continuity:**
  - seven long locks through S7a;
  - shaved in S7b and S8a;
  - short regrowth from S8b on, including S1 and S12b, because "the hair of his head began to grow again" (16:22).
- **Text stays fixed to the screen.** Captions hide while the title (0:00.0–0:03.0) or the verse card (0:15.9–0:20.5) carries the same words.
- **Screen direction:** Philistines and the woman at Timnah sit screen right; the gold light comes from the top left; he is led in moving screen right; his family carries him screen left.
- **Depth in every shot:** far layer, middle layer, subject, and a soft foreground (pillars, vines, his shoulder, Delilah, the roof crowd, debris, rubble, rock).
