- **Two lights instead of a scale.** Gold light from above marks what God did: it comes with each rush of the Spirit, drains away only on "the Lord had left him", touches his hands after the prayer, and falls on him at the verdict. An ember-red glow marks his own desires and choices, and it only ever sits on Samson: the reflection in his eyes, his face in Delilah's lamplight, his face and hands at the verdict. Torches and cities stay neutral, and the woman at Timnah is lit naturally (Judges 14:4 says the match "was from the LORD"). The burial dawn is natural daylight, not the Spirit's gold. There is no balance or weighing anywhere: the verdict rests on what the text says, not a tally of deeds.
- **The hair never glows.** The light changes only when the text says the Lord came or left, so the picture never suggests the hair itself was the power.
- **Text stays fixed to the screen.** Captions hide while the title (0:00–0:03) or the verse card (0:15–0:21) carries the same words.
- **Screen direction:** Philistines and the woman at Timnah sit screen right; the gold light comes from the top left; his family moves screen left.
- **Depth in every shot:** far layer, middle layer, subject, and a soft foreground (pillars, vines, his shoulder, Delilah, the roof crowd, debris, rubble, rock).
