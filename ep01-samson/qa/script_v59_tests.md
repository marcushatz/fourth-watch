# Ep 1 v59: simulated first-time viewer tests (AI agents, Oct 31, evening)

> **Note (Round 17):** reviewer B and the variant testers are **simulated viewers**: AI agents role-playing a first-time viewer. Their notes can flag confusion and slow, preachy or unclear lines. Their "would watch to the end" and "would send it to…" are role-play, not audience data; only real posts measure retention and sharing.

Two fresh agents with no tools, each hearing ONE variant once (brief: `prompts/F_variant_test.md`). Both heard the v59 text with the cuts. That text had the first-draft answer line, "His faith is what Hebrews names", which the ESV check later flagged as an overclaim and replaced with option 2. On-screen labels were described ("GAZA · A PHILISTINE CITY", "A VOW · NO RAZOR", "HEBREWS 11 · PEOPLE OF FAITH").

## Variant K (keeps the Matthew 7 warning), about 2:05

1. **Question and answer:** the answer was "no… Hebrews honors his faith, not his strength." **Clarity: 4/5.** It lost a point because "the final answer line goes by fast and sounds abstract".
2. **Swipe risk:** the Delilah recap: "I know this part already, so my thumb starts moving." "But he did not know that the LORD had left him" pulled them back. "Long for Reels at 2:05, and I felt that around the Jesus line."
3. **Slow, preachy or judgmental:** the "gifted…" line and "I never knew you" back to back "stop the story to deliver a sermon, and the Matthew line feels like a threat aimed at me." "Earlier… Now…" "explains a contrast I could mostly feel already, so it drags a little."
4. **Strongest and weakest:** strongest, "But he did not know that the LORD had left him" with the "as at other times" breakdown. Weakest, "Samson's strength never proved that. His faith is what Hebrews names." ("faith feels stated without being shown").
5. **The ending:** half-landed; "the Hebrews 11 twist is genuinely surprising"; the closing question "feels like generic engagement bait with three options".
6. **Overall:** would watch to the end. Would send it to "a friend who leads worship or is talented and uses 'God's still blessing me' to excuse things". Would not send it to a non-church friend, "because the 'I never knew you' line would read as condemning".

## Variant X (cuts the Matthew 7 warning), about 1:55

1. **Question and answer:** the answer was "no… Hebrews remembers his faith, not his strength." **Clarity: 4/5.** "The ending gets a bit fuzzy because his last prayer was for revenge, and right after that Hebrews praises his faith."
2. **Swipe risk:** at "Later, he fell in love with Delilah…": "it turns into the Samson-and-Delilah story I already know". On the gate: "I didn't get why it mattered… nobody says that, so it just felt like a random fact."
3. **Slow, preachy or judgmental:** the "gifted…" line is "close" to a sermon. "Earlier… Now…" "repeats a point I already got". The re-asked question "felt like a recap I didn't need".
4. **Strongest and weakest:** strongest, "But he did not know that the LORD had left him… the best part of the video". Weakest, "Earlier… Now…": "a flat contrast that doesn't add anything new".
5. **The ending:** half worked: "'His faith is what Hebrews names' makes sense in my head, but I didn't feel it, because I never saw his faith. I only saw him ask for revenge." The closing question "feels like engagement bait".
6. **Overall:** would watch to the end, "mostly because of the 'Lord had left him' turn. Nearly two minutes is long, though." Would send it to "a friend who leads worship… and says 'God's still blessing it' about stuff they probably shouldn't be doing".

## What was changed because of these tests

- **The answer line:** option 2 replaced the first draft. It points at the prayer the viewer saw: "But even at his lowest, he could still call on God." The ESV check verified it.
- **The gate:** an on-screen pill, "THE SAME NIGHT · STILL STRONG", says why it matters (S5).
- **The Delilah stretch** leads with the new facts (the counter "FROM EACH LORD"; the call stamps) and moves quickly.
- **"Earlier… Now…"** is offered to Marcus as a trim, not cut (it's his line).
- **The closing question:** a note for Marcus (his approved choice form vs. the viewers' "engagement bait" reaction).
