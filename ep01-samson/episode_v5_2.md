# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 56
answer: A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?

## Stakes
VO: Before Delilah, the Philistines ruled Israel, and he married one. [Judges 13:1, 14:1–4]

## Story
VO: To pay a wedding bet, he killed thirty men for their clothes. [Judges 14:10–19]
VO: His wife was given to his best man. So he burned Philistine fields with 300 foxes. [Judges 14:20, 15:1–5]
VO: So the Philistines burned her and her father. [Judges 15:6]
VO: 3,000 Israelites came to hand him over. [Judges 15:9–13]
VO: He told them, "As they did to me, so have I done to them." [Judges 15:11]

## Turn
VO: Yet Judges says God was behind that marriage, "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." [Judges 14:4]
VERSE: "…it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." — Judges 14:4 (ESV)
VO: Blinded, he prayed for revenge "for my two eyes." [Judges 16:21, 16:28]
VO: God gave him strength, and the building fell on them. [Judges 16:28–30]
VO: The New Testament lists him among the faithful. [Hebrews 11:32]

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's. [Judges 13:5, 14:4, 15:11, 16:28; Hebrews 11:32]

## Value
- In Samson's day the Philistines ruled Israel, and he married one of them [Judges 13:1, 14:1–4]
- He killed thirty men for their clothes to pay off a bet he lost at his own wedding [Judges 14:12–19]
- His wife was given to his best man; his revenge with 300 foxes led to her and her father being burned [Judges 14:20, 15:1–6]
- 3,000 Israelites came to hand him over, and his explanation was "As they did to me, so have I done to them" [Judges 15:9–13]
- Judges says the marriage that started it all "was from the LORD", who was "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines" [Judges 14:4]
- His last prayer was for revenge "for my two eyes", and God gave him the strength [Judges 16:28–30]
- The New Testament lists him among the faithful [Hebrews 11:32]

## Caption
Hero or villain? The answer is in Judges 13–16 and Hebrews 11:32. Read it yourself.
Three more things most people miss:
1. He scraped honey out of the dead lion and fed it to his parents without telling them where it came from (Judges 14:8–9).
2. He judged Israel for twenty years, and killed more Philistines at his death than in his whole life (Judges 15:20, 16:30).
3. The number 3,000 shows up twice: the men of Judah who came for him (Judges 15:11) and the people on the roof when he died (Judges 16:27).
