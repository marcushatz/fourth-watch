# Story Director proof of concept: the pillars (Ep 1 v57, 103.75–116.35 s, Judges 16:28–30)

**The approved narration in the window** (v57, locked):
> Now, he asked God for strength. / Then he pushed against the pillars with all his strength, and the building fell on the Philistine lords and everyone inside. Samson died with them.

**Before it:**
- "His choices had cost him his eyes and his freedom. Earlier, he had assumed he would escape." (prison, then Sorek)

**After it:**
- the verse card for 16:30, over the same set.

**What's on screen today (v57 shot S19, one 12.6 s shot):** see `v57/v57_hook_and_S19.jpg`.
- Medium low on a frontal Samson, hands raised in front of him.
- On "pushed", gold light floods in.
- Pull back to a wide; the roof falls; dust.

## A. Narrative analysis (what the viewer needs)

| Question | Answer for this window |
|---|---|
| What makes it interesting? | The man the whole reel judged by his strength is blind, mocked and helpless, and he asks for strength instead of assuming it. One line earlier: "he had assumed he would escape". The picture has to show the change from assuming to asking. |
| Central tension | Will anything happen? The viewer half-knows the ending. The suspense is how, and the cost: "Samson died with them." |
| What the viewer already knows | The temple falls. Most remember "he pushed the pillars". |
| What would surprise them | (1) It happened while they were still laughing: nothing in 16:25-30 says they stopped. (2) He died in it. Many people remember a triumph, not a death. (3) The opening image comes back: two upright posts gripped by his hands (16:3 → 16:29). |
| What they should understand, moment by moment | He's helpless and asking (not flexing) → his hands are on the two pillars → he leans with everything he has → the pillars give → the whole house falls on the lords and the crowd → he is gone with them. |
| What they should feel | Pity and stillness → effort, held breath → shock → quiet loss. Not triumph. |
| What pulls them to the next sentence | The asking (will God answer?) is left open. The text doesn't say God answered, so neither does the picture: no gold. The pillars' first give pulls them through "fell". |
| Moments that deserve emphasis | "pushed" (the verb, on its word), "fell" (the impact), "died" (the stillness after). |
| Visual payoff to the opening | The hook is a small figure, arms up, under the Gaza gate's two posts, at night. The end is a small figure, arms out, between two posts that give way, then two broken stumps where he stood. |

**Accuracy fences** (from the text and the canon; they bind every treatment):
- 16:26-29: he felt the pillars, prayed, then "grasped" them, "leaned his weight against them, his right hand on the one and his left hand on the other".
- 16:30: "he bowed with all his strength, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it".
- **No gold anywhere.** 16:28-30 never says God answered (07 §6: "never on at the pillars").
- **No hush.** The text never says the laughter stopped.
- **No bodies.** He is lost in the dust before the roof lands.
- 16:27: "about 3,000" on the roof.

## B. Three treatments

| | T1 "Two posts" | T2 "The size of the house" | T3 "From the roof" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governing image | His two hands on two posts. It rhymes with the hook's gate posts, and the ending is two broken stumps | A small man inside a vast house: scale as the story | The mockers' view: looking down on the entertainer until the floor goes |
| Staging space | Between the middle pillars, then the whole hall, then the rubble | The hall from its far end; the pillar from its base | The roof's edge and the crowd's backs |
| Camera grammar | Matched framings (hook ↔ end); tight on the hands for the verb; top-down for the end | Extreme wide, a crane up the pillar, a pull-out for the fall | High POV, locked, then a drop |
| Light | Torchlight only (the Philistines' house: ember). The dust swallows the torches: dusk | The same, plus moving shadows from the braziers | Evening sky behind the crowd; torches below |
| Transitions | Cut on the grip; cut on "building" to the wide; cut to top-down on "Samson" | Cuts on scale steps; a dust wipe | One hard cut from the POV to the wide as the roof drops |
| Risk | The top-down end needs a camera the set wasn't built for | Can read as spectacle (triumph) | Puts the viewer with the mockers; "Samson died with them" needs him seen |

**Pick: T1 "Two posts"**, with T2's crane up the pillar for "with all his strength".
- **Why:** it's the only treatment that pays off the opening, and the only one where the verb ("pushed") is the picture.
- Its ending (two empty stumps, no body) carries "Samson died with them" without gore and without triumph.
- In production Marcus picks the treatment. This demo picks so it can be built.

## F. How it's built (the existing engine, plus reusable pieces)

- **Set:** `dagon` as it is: the analytic collapse, the roof crowd, the lords, the dust, rubble and trickle systems. New shot variants `sdA`–`sdE` sit beside `pillars` and `fall`, so the existing shots don't change.
- **New reusable primitives,** in `fw/direct.js` (loaded after `fw/core.js`):
  - `DIRECT.path(t, keys)`: a camera path through timed keys, eased per segment. Keys are named marks a set publishes ("hands", "pillarUp", "hallWide", "topDown"), so a spec can say "from hands, crane to pillarUp" without world coordinates.
  - `DIRECT.act(t, at, opts)`: the acting envelope (anticipation dip → drive → overshoot → settle) for any pose's `k`.
  - `DIRECT.shake(t, at, amp, dur)`: the impact shake (≤ 12 px, gone in 0.45 s).
  - `DIRECT.tremor(t, t0, amp)`: sustained effort.
- **dagon changes, in the demo copy only:**
  - `timeline()` reads the collapse beats from the shot that carries them, not "the first 'fall'".
  - `stage()` gets the `sd` branch: gold, hint and surge are 0; no hush; head-down → lift on "asked"; the strain-grip on "Then"; the bow through `DIRECT.act`.
  - `camAt()` gets the `sd` branch: the named marks plus `DIRECT.path`.
- **Score:** `compile_spec`-style. S19 is replaced by sdA–sdE in a copy of the score; every shot carries the collapse beats (`called`, `answered`, `house`, `fell`).
- **Sound:** the v57 soundtrack, cut from the master, for the comparison. Its hits on "pushed", "building" and "fell" still line up. Its shimmer on "pushed" belonged to the gold and should go when the song is rebuilt.
