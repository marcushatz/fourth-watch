FACT-CHECK (from the text you gave me only, no tools)

P1: VERIFIED. 16:21 (blind, bronze shackles), 16:25-27 (between the pillars, entertaining the lords), 16:26 (hands on the pillars), 16:28 (he calls to the LORD). No gold is right, since 16:28 narrates a prayer and no visible divine act. Small flags: the shackles are stated at Gaza (16:21), and "on the wrists" is inferred. The lords "pointing" is staging, not text.

P2: VERIFIED. 16:29-30 (grasped, leaned his weight, bowed with all his strength). Trembling is fair strain. The pillars "lean outward" is an inferred direction, so have them give or shift.

P3: OVERCLAIM. The crowd on the roof is 16:27, and the roof tilt follows from "the house fell" (16:30). Sand pouring from the pillar tops is not in the text. It is an invented intermediate beat, so cut it or fold it into the moment of collapse.

P4: VERIFIED. 16:30, "the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people." Keeping him out of sight in the dust is a framing choice, not a claim, and it fits the no-gore rule.

P5: CORRECTION. The roof lying over the hall is 16:30. Torches and settling dust are neutral set dressing. The toppled statue is an invented event (see b).

(a) The cloth band is a fair stand-in for the blinding. It is not an event, and it keeps the picture free of gore. It does add an object the text never mentions: gouged eyes (16:21), not a blindfold. In my muted read I saw "blindfolded", not "blind", and I could not tell why. The Philistines blindfolding him as part of the entertainment would be a different story. Keep it only if the narration or an earlier shot makes the blindness clear, or use dark, shadowed eye sockets instead. Never show the band being tied.

(b) Yes, it is invented. 16:23 names Dagon as the occasion for the feast, but no verse says an image fell. A toppled idol would also make an unstated claim about God's agency and break the series rule. The toppling of Dagon is a different episode (1 Samuel 5). Leave the statue upright and untouched, or leave it out.
