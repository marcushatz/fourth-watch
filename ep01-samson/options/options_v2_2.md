# Samson: topic options, round 2 (revised after critic round 1; fact-check fixes folded in)

Rule for all: the famous story gets a fast visual montage while the narration delivers value. ESV only.

---

## OPTION 1: "Power isn't presence" (was B)
HOOK: Why did God keep giving Samson strength while he kept sinning?
ANSWER: Samson, Saul and David are the only three men the Bible says the Spirit "rushed upon"; Saul lost it, Samson didn't know it was gone, and only David is heard begging not to lose it. Power isn't proof God is pleased with you.
VO:
1. Why did God keep giving Samson strength while he kept sinning?
2. [montage: lion, Ashkelon, Lehi] Three times, "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him." No other judge has the Spirit come on him that often. [13:25, 14:6, 14:19, 15:14; cf. 3:10, 6:34, 11:29]
3. And the Spirit "rushed upon" only three men in the whole Bible: Samson, Saul and David. [Judg 14:6; 1 Sam 10:10; 16:13]
4. With Samson and Saul, it came in bursts. With David, the text adds: "from that day forward." [1 Sam 16:13]
5. The very next verse: "Now the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul." [1 Sam 16:14]
6. Samson's strength still worked the night he went to a prostitute in Gaza. He carried off the city gates. [Judg 16:1–3]
7. Then he woke up one day, and "he did not know that the LORD had left him." [16:20]
8. David sinned too, badly. When the prophet Nathan confronted him, he prayed: "take not your Holy Spirit from me." [2 Sam 12:1–13; Ps 51 title, 51:11]
9. Saul lost it. Samson didn't know it was gone. Only David is heard begging God not to take it away.
10. So why did Samson's strength keep working? It was for God's purpose, to "begin to save Israel," not a sign God approved of him. Power isn't proof that God is pleased with you. Don't judge where you stand with God by how well your gifts still work. [13:5; 16:20]
CAPTION LINE: These are Old Testament leaders. For what the New Testament says to believers, see Ephesians 1:13–14.

## OPTION 2: "Every time someone called" (A + D merged)
HOOK: Did Samson actually save Israel?
ANSWER: Not fully. The angel said he would only "begin" to save Israel. Israel had stopped crying out, and in this story, every time someone finally called, God answered.
VO:
1. Did Samson actually save Israel?
2. [montage] Not fully. Before he was born, the angel said he would only "begin to save Israel." [13:5]
3. Now look at the pattern. In every cycle before Samson, the same line appears: "the people of Israel cried out to the LORD." [3:9, 3:15, 4:3, 6:7, 10:10]
4. Samson's chapter opens with forty years under the Philistines, the longest oppression in the book. And this time, Israel never cries out. [13:1]
5. Worse. The first people to tie Samson up weren't Philistines. Three thousand men of Judah came with new ropes: "Do you not know that the Philistines are rulers over us?" [15:11–13]
6. Even Samson doesn't speak of God until he's near death from thirst. Then he calls, and God splits the ground open. They named it En-hakkore: "the spring of him who called." [15:18–19]
7. Blind, he calls one last time, and God answers again. [16:28–30]
8. Later, the people finally beg Samuel: "Do not cease to cry out to the LORD our God for us, that he may save us from the hand of the Philistines." That day, "the LORD thundered with a mighty sound… against the Philistines." [1 Sam 7:8, 7:10]
9. So did Samson save Israel? He began it. God didn't wait to be asked to start the rescue. And in this story, every time someone finally called, God answered. [15:18–19; 16:28–30; 1 Sam 7:8–10]
10. Don't get so used to what's crushing you that you stop crying out.

## OPTION 3: "Right in my eyes" (was C)
HOOK: What was Samson's real weakness?
ANSWER: Not his hair and not just Delilah: his eyes. He followed what was "right in my eyes," lost his eyes, and the book of Judges ends with all Israel doing what was right in its own eyes.
VO:
1. What was Samson's real weakness?
2. [montage] Everyone says Delilah, or his hair. But watch how often his eyes come up.
3. "He saw one of the daughters of the Philistines." Then: "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." [14:1–3]
4. And yes, the text says God was using it. God can use what's leading you. That doesn't make it safe to follow. [14:4]
5. In Gaza, "he saw a prostitute." [16:1]
6. Then the scariest line: "he did not know that the LORD had left him." The very next verse: the Philistines "gouged out his eyes." In a way, he was blind before they ever touched him. [16:20–21]
7. Even his last prayer was about his eyes: "that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes." [16:28]
8. Jewish teachers noticed it 1,800 years ago: "Samson followed his eyes," so his eyes were taken. [Mishnah Sotah 1:8, c. AD 200; on-screen tag: JEWISH TRADITION, NOT SCRIPTURE]
9. Then Judges ends with one line about the whole nation: "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Almost the same words in Hebrew. [17:6; 21:25]
10. So what was Samson's weakness? Not just lust. He made himself the judge of what was right. Live by what's right in your own eyes, and eventually you can't see.
