# Samson: topic options, round 1 (draft scripts, ~60–75 s each)

Rule for all: the story everyone knows gets one line while a fast visual montage shows it (lion, foxes, jawbone, gates, Delilah, haircut, temple). The rest is value. ESV only.

---

## OPTION A: "Nobody asked for Samson"
HOOK: Why would God choose someone like Samson?
ANSWER: Israel had stopped asking to be rescued, so God started the rescue anyway; Samson was sent to a people so used to their chains they tied up their own deliverer.
VO:
1. Why would God choose someone like Samson?
2. [montage of the famous story] You know the lion, the jawbone and Delilah. But look at how his story starts.
3. Every time Israel was crushed in Judges, one line repeats: "the people of Israel cried out to the LORD." Five times. [Judg 3:9, 3:15, 4:3, 6:6, 10:10]
4. Then Samson's chapter opens. Forty years under the Philistines, the longest oppression in the whole book. And nobody cries out. [13:1]
5. God sends a deliverer anyway. No one asked. [13:2–5]
6. Worse, they didn't want one. When Samson fought back, three thousand men of Judah came to tie him up: "Do you not know that the Philistines are rulers over us?" [15:11–13]
7. That's why the angel said Samson would only "begin to save Israel." [13:5]
8. The cry comes back a generation later. Samuel: "Do not cease to cry out to the LORD." And God, about Israel's first king: "their cry has come to me." [1 Sam 7:8, 9:16]
9. And David finishes it: "By the hand of my servant David I will save my people Israel from the hand of the Philistines." [2 Sam 3:18]
10. So why Samson? God doesn't wait to be asked. But the saddest thing in his story isn't Delilah. It's a people so used to their chains, they tied up the man sent to break them.
11. Don't get so used to what's crushing you that you fight the One sent to set you free.

## OPTION B: "Three men, one verb"
HOOK: What's the real lesson of Samson?
ANSWER: Samson, Saul and David are the only three men the Spirit of the LORD "rushed upon"; two lost God's presence and one of them didn't even notice; David is the one who begged not to lose it. Power isn't proof God is with you.
VO:
1. What's the real lesson of Samson?
2. [montage] The Spirit of the LORD came on Samson four times, more than on any other judge. [13:25, 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
3. And the Bible uses one rare word for it: the Spirit "rushed upon him." [14:6]
4. That word is used for only three men in the whole Bible: Samson, Saul and David. [Judg 14:6; 1 Sam 10:6; 16:13]
5. Watch what happens to all three.
6. Saul: "the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul." [1 Sam 16:14]
7. Samson: his strength kept working, through a prostitute in Gaza, right before he carried off the city gates. Then one morning, "he did not know that the LORD had left him." [16:1–3, 16:20]
8. David sinned too, badly. But he noticed. And he prayed: "take not your Holy Spirit from me." [Ps 51:11]
9. Three men with the same gift. Only one was afraid of losing God more than he trusted his gift.
10. So what's the lesson of Samson? Power isn't proof that God is with you. Don't wait until it's gone to notice.

## OPTION C: "Samson's real weakness"
HOOK: What was Samson's real weakness?
ANSWER: Not his hair and not just Delilah: his eyes. He followed them his whole life, lost them, and the book of Judges ends with all Israel doing the same.
VO:
1. What was Samson's real weakness?
2. [montage] Everyone says Delilah, or his hair. The text points somewhere else: his eyes.
3. "He saw one of the daughters of the Philistines." Then: "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." [14:1–3]
4. In Gaza, "he saw a prostitute." [16:1]
5. So what did the Philistines take? "They gouged out his eyes." [16:21]
6. Three thousand people "looked on" while a man who couldn't see entertained them. [16:27]
7. His last prayer: "that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes." [16:28]
8. Jewish teachers noticed this 1,800 years ago: "Samson followed his eyes," so his eyes were taken. [Mishnah Sotah 1:8]
9. Then Judges ends with one line about the whole nation: "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Same words. Samson was Israel in one man. [17:6, 21:25]
10. So what was Samson's weakness? He let his eyes decide. Whatever you let your eyes lead, eventually leads you.

## OPTION D: "Why Samson is in the Hall of Faith"
HOOK: Why is Samson listed among the heroes of faith?
ANSWER: Hebrews names him for faith, not a clean record: when his strength failed, he called on God, and God answered, twice.
VO:
1. Why is Samson listed among the heroes of faith? [Heb 11:32]
2. [montage] A man who chased foreign women, broke his own vow, and told Delilah his secret.
3. Hebrews says those men "stopped the mouths of lions" and "were made strong out of weakness." Commentators point straight at Samson. [Heb 11:33–34]
4. Here's what most people miss: Samson never mentions God at all until he's dying of thirst. [Judg 14–15:18]
5. Then he prays: "You have granted this great salvation by the hand of your servant." He calls himself God's servant. [15:18]
6. God split open the ground and water came out. They named it En-hakkore: "the spring of him who called." [15:19]
7. His last prayer, blind: "please remember me and please strengthen me only this once." [16:28]
8. God answered again. The man who lived by his strength was remembered for two prayers made when he had none.
9. So why is Samson in the Hall of Faith? Faith isn't a clean record. It's who you call when your strength is gone.

## OPTION E: "The real world behind Samson"
HOOK: Is there any real evidence behind Samson's story?
ANSWER: There's no artifact of Samson himself, but the world the story describes keeps turning up in the ground; the details fit.
VO:
1. Is there any real evidence behind Samson's story?
2. [montage] Not of Samson himself. But look at what archaeologists keep finding in his world.
3. The Philistines were newcomers from across the sea. Egyptian temple walls show them as captives, and DNA from Ashkelon found European ancestry in their earliest graves. [Medinet Habu; Feldman 2019]
4. Their cities have piles of pig bones. Israelite towns next door have almost none. That's the border Samson grew up on.
5. Samson died pulling on "the two middle pillars on which the house rested." Philistine temples have been dug up with exactly that: two central pillar bases, about two meters apart. [16:29; Tell Qasile]
6. Blind, he "ground at the mill." That was slave-girl work, and ancient records show captives were blinded and put to grinding. [16:21; Exod 11:5]
7. Delilah's silver wasn't coins. Coins didn't exist yet. It was weighed: 1,100 shekels from each lord. [16:5]
8. And his riddle, "out of the strong came something sweet," has been on a syrup tin in Britain for over 140 years. [14:14; Lyle's]
9. None of this proves the story. But the details fit the world they describe. So read it like it happened.
