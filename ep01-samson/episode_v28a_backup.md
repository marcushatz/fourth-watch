# Why did God let Samson keep his strength while he kept sinning?
series: ASK THE TEXT
target_seconds: 140
answer: The text ties his strength to God's purpose, never to a verdict on his life. A gift from God is not a verdict from God.

## Hook
VO: Why did God let Samson keep his strength while he kept sinning?
ON-SCREEN: WHY DID GOD LET SAMSON KEEP HIS STRENGTH?

## Stakes
VO: He slept with a prostitute in Gaza, and that same night he carried off the city gates. [Judges 16:1-3]
VO: Was God fine with how he lived?
VO: Let's let the Bible answer it: his story, then three truths.

## Story
VO: Again and again, "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him," more than on any other judge. [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
VO: But he demanded a Philistine wife over his parents' objection, because "she is right in my eyes." [Judges 14:2-3]
VO: Then came Delilah. [Judges 16:4]
VO: Four times she said the exact same words: "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" [Judges 16:9, 16:12, 16:14, 16:20]
VO: Three times, he broke free. [Judges 16:9, 16:12, 16:14]
VO: Then he told her all his heart, and his hair, the sign he was set apart to God, was shaved. [Judges 13:5, 16:17, 16:19]
VO: He woke up and said, "I will go out as at other times and shake myself free." [Judges 16:20]
VO: "But he did not know that the LORD had left him." [Judges 16:20]
VERSE: "But he did not know that the LORD had left him." — Judges 16:20 (ESV)

## Turn
VO: One: the strength had a purpose. [Judges 13:5, 14:4]
VO: Before Samson was born, the angel said he would "begin to save Israel." [Judges 13:5]
VO: Even when he chose by his own eyes, the text says "it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." [Judges 14:4]
VO: The strength was for God's plan, not a reward for Samson's life. [Judges 13:5, 14:4]
VO: Two: power is not proof. [Matthew 7:22-23]
VO: Jesus said many will say on that day, "Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?" [Matthew 7:22]
VO: And He will declare, "I never knew you." [Matthew 7:23]
VO: Jesus isn't talking about Samson here, but the principle holds: mighty works and knowing God are not the same thing. [Matthew 7:22-23]

## Landing
VO: Three: God didn't give up on him. [Judges 16:22-30; Hebrews 11:32-34]
VO: Two verses after the LORD left him, his hair "began to grow again." [Judges 16:20, 16:22]
VO: But he didn't pray to his hair.
VO: Blind and a prisoner, he prayed, "O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once." [Judges 16:21, 16:28]
VO: The LORD had left him, but when Samson called, God still answered: the house of Dagon fell. [Judges 16:20, 16:28-30]
VO: Hebrews 11 doesn't credit his muscles. It names him among those who "through faith" "were made strong out of weakness." [Hebrews 11:32-34]
VO: So why did God let Samson keep his strength? The text ties it to God's purpose, never to a verdict on his life. [Judges 13:5, 14:4]
VO: Was God fine with how he lived? The strength was never proof he was.
VO: A gift from God is not a verdict from God.
VO: Don't judge where you stand with God by how well your gifts work.
VO: Which hit you harder: that Hebrews 11 names him among the faithful, or that God still answered him at the end? Tell us in the comments. [Hebrews 11:32; Judges 16:28-30]

## Value
- His strength came from the LORD; the hair was the sign of his consecration [Judges 14:6, 16:17, 16:20, 16:28]
- Delilah said the exact same words four times; the fourth time, the LORD had left him [Judges 16:9, 16:12, 16:14, 16:20]
- The text ties his strength to God's purpose, "begin to save Israel" [Judges 13:5, 14:4]
- Two verses after "the LORD had left him," his hair "began to grow again," and God still answered his prayer [Judges 16:22, 16:28-30]
- Hebrews 11 names Samson among those who "through faith" "were made strong out of weakness" [Hebrews 11:32-34]
