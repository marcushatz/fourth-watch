# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 55
answer: A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?

## Stakes
VO: You know Delilah. Here's what came before her.

## Story
VO: He lost a bet at his wedding, so he killed thirty men to pay it. [Judges 14:10–19]
VO: His wife was given to his best man. So he burned Philistine fields with 300 foxes. [Judges 14:20, 15:1–5]
VO: The Philistines burned her and her father. [Judges 15:6]
VO: Then 3,000 Israelites tied him up and handed him over. [Judges 15:9–13]
VO: His defense: "As they did to me, so have I done to them." [Judges 15:11]

## Turn
VO: Yet Judges says God was behind that marriage, "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." [Judges 14:4]
VERSE: "…it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an opportunity against the Philistines." — Judges 14:4 (ESV)
VO: He judged Israel twenty years, and his death killed more Philistines than his whole life. [Judges 15:20, 16:30–31]
VO: Hebrews 11 still lists him among the faithful. [Hebrews 11:32]

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? A flawed hero. His motives were his own. His mission was God's. [Judges 13:5, 14:4, 15:11; Hebrews 11:32]

## Value
- He killed thirty men to pay off a bet he lost at his own wedding [Judges 14:12–19]
- His wife was given to his best man; his revenge with 300 foxes got her and her father burned [Judges 14:20, 15:1–6]
- 3,000 Israelites tied him up and handed him to the Philistines [Judges 15:9–13]
- His own explanation for all of it: "As they did to me, so have I done to them" [Judges 15:11]
- Judges says the marriage that started it all "was from the LORD", who was "seeking an opportunity against the Philistines" [Judges 14:4]
- He judged Israel twenty years, and killed more Philistines at his death than in his whole life [Judges 15:20, 16:30]
- Hebrews 11 lists him among the faithful [Hebrews 11:32]

## Caption
Hero or villain? The answer is in Judges 13–16. Read it yourself.
Three more things most people miss:
1. He scraped honey out of the dead lion and fed it to his parents without telling them where it came from (Judges 14:8–9).
2. The number 3,000 shows up twice: the men of Judah who tied him up (Judges 15:11) and the people on the roof when he died (Judges 16:27).
3. Hebrews 11 lists him among those who through faith "were made strong out of weakness" (Hebrews 11:32–34).
