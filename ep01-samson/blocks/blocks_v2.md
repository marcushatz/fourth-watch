# Samson: building-block library v2 (fact-check round 1 applied; critic round 1 merged)

MESSAGE: Samson's God-given strength kept working while he did what was right in his own eyes, so its working was no proof he was living the way God wanted. A gift from God is not a verdict from God.

FACT NOTE: Is it factual that "the LORD had left him"? Yes. It is the exact ESV of Judges 16:20: "But he did not know that the LORD had left him." The text records it after he "told her all his heart" and the shaving (16:17-19). It never says his eyes were the reason, so the script states the events in order and never as cause and effect.

Rules:
- Samson only. David and Saul are out.
- ESV only, quoted word for word. State events in the text's order; never chain them into causes the text doesn't give.
- Never use the Spirit-driven feats (lion, Ashkelon, Lehi) as examples of power while living wrongly.
- Don't deny the hair-strength link: Samson states it (16:17) and the text records it (16:19). The hair was the sign; the strength was from the LORD.
- Bold claim at the start, bold claim at the end. Visuals carry the famous story; narration carries value.

## Setup: The gift (the montage shows the feats)
- S1 **[REC]** [verified; critic 9] "The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him" three times, more than on any other judge. A lion. Thirty men. A thousand. [14:6, 14:19, 15:14; cf. 3:10, 6:34, 11:29]
  - VISUAL: Montage, one beat per line (about 2 s each so first-timers can read it): the lion torn barehanded; thirty Philistine figures topple in a row at Ashkelon and their garments drop (no gore); ropes melt at Lehi and the jawbone rises over a field of fallen shields. Gold rushes in each time; the gold mark appears and stays lit.
  - NOTE: Quote, stat and a three-beat rhythm built for the montage. The count holds because it is tied to the "rushed upon" quote (13:25 "began to stir him" is a fourth Spirit line).
- S2 [verified; critic 5] His strength came from God. Three times, "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him." No other judge has the Spirit come on him that often. [14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
  - VISUAL: Same montage as S1.
  - NOTE: Same facts as S1, said less sharply.
- S3 [verified; critic 5] Before he was born, an angel said he would "begin to save Israel." And God's Spirit came on him like on no other judge. [13:5; 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
  - VISUAL: The angel ascends in the altar flame (13:20), then the feat montage.
  - NOTE: "Begin to save" is a strong detail, but it belongs to a different reel.

## Aftermath: Optional
- A1 [fixed; critic 6] Blind and a prisoner, that's the first time we see him ask God for strength: "strengthen me only this once." [16:21, 16:28]
  - VISUAL: Blind Samson at the mill, then at the two pillars, praying. Tags 16:21 · 16:28.
  - NOTE: Real value, but it adds a second lesson (prayer) right before the close. "For strength" matters: in 15:18 he called on the LORD for water.
- A2 [verified; critic 3] Blind, he finally asked: "strengthen me only this once." And God answered. [16:28-30]
  - VISUAL: The pillars give way; the house falls in stone and dust (no bodies).
  - NOTE: Critic: never pair it with this message. If the strength returns on prayer, the gift starts to look like a verdict again.
