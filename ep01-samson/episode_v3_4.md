# Was Samson a hero or a villain?
series: HERO OR VILLAIN?
target_seconds: 54
answer: A flawed hero of faith. His failures were his own. His strength was God's.

## Hook
VO: Was Samson a hero or a villain?
ON-SCREEN: WAS SAMSON A HERO OR A VILLAIN?
VISUAL: Blind Samson between two huge stone pillars, a hand on each; a crack climbs one pillar; crowds on the roof above.

## Stakes
VO: Most think his strength was in his hair. [Judges 16:17]
VISUAL: Close on Samson's seven long locks in silhouette, back-lit; slow push-in toward the hair. No gold yet.

## Story
VO: But three times, the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him. [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
VISUAL: Three gold rushes of light from the top left, one per cut: a young lion charges him roaring and he tears it apart with nothing in his hand; at Ashkelon a wave of gold light throws a line of Philistine silhouettes back (the city in neutral dusk light); the ropes on his arms fall apart like burnt flax and drop off his hands (no fire on him).
VO: His weakness? His eyes. [Judges 14:1, 14:7, 16:1]
VISUAL: Extreme close-up on Samson's eyes; an ember-red reflection enters them.
VO: He demanded a wife from Israel's enemies. "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." [Judges 13:1, 14:1–4]
VERSE: "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes." — Judges 14:3 (ESV)
VISUAL: First the sighting at Timnah: over his shoulder, a Philistine woman in natural dusk light, screen right; the ember glow is only in Samson's eyes as he looks. Then at the family home in Zorah, no woman present: Samson speaks to his father while his mother objects beside him; he gestures off screen right.
VO: Later, he told Delilah his hair marked him as set apart to God. [Judges 13:5, 16:4, 16:17]
VISUAL: Night, a lamp-lit room in the Valley of Sorek (clearly a different woman and place from Timnah): Delilah in the foreground as Samson tells her, touching his long hair; the lamp's ember light on his face.
VO: She had it shaved. The Lord had left him. [Judges 16:19–20]
VISUAL: He sleeps on Delilah's knees; a man shaves off the seven locks; as the last lock falls, the faint gold that has stayed on him since the lion fades out while he sleeps. He wakes and tries to shake himself free as at other times, in the dark, not knowing it is gone.
VO: The man who lived by his eyes lost his eyes. [Judges 16:21]
VISUAL: Philistine silhouettes close in from screen right; push in on his face; hard cut to black on "lost his eyes".
VO: Their god Dagon got the credit. Samson was the entertainment. [Judges 16:23–25]
VISUAL: An extreme wide of the packed house at the Philistines' celebration: the lords below, about 3,000 men and women on the roof, rejoicing. Then a young man leads blind Samson in by the hand; when the people see him, they raise their arms in praise of their god, and he is made to stand between the pillars.

## Turn
VO: He asked God for strength, to avenge his two eyes. [Judges 16:28]
VISUAL: Close on his face as he lifts it upward to pray, his hands feeling the pillars; the camera holds completely still; a faint gold touches his hands as the line ends.
VO: And the house fell. [Judges 16:30]
VISUAL: He grasps the two middle pillars, his right hand on one and his left on the other, and bows with all his strength, leaning, not shoving; the pillars give; the roof comes down; dust and darkness fill the frame. No gold on the collapse.
VO: The New Testament lists him among the faithful. [Hebrews 11:32]
VISUAL: Natural dawn light (pale, not the Spirit's gold): his brothers and family carry him from the ruins toward his father's tomb.

## Landing
VO: Hero or villain? A flawed hero of faith. His failures were his own. His strength was God's. [Judges 16:1, 16:17, 16:20, 16:28; Hebrews 11:32, 11:33, 11:39]
VISUAL: The opening frame again: blind Samson between the pillars. On "His failures were his own" an ember glow rises on his own face and hands; on "His strength was God's" a shaft of gold falls on him from above; it settles to the opening look as the title returns.

## Value
- His strength came from God, not his hair: three times "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him" [Judges 14:6, 14:19, 15:14]
- His hair marked him as set apart to God; when it was shaved, "the LORD had left him" [Judges 13:5, 16:17, 16:19–20]
- The man who lived by his eyes ("she is right in my eyes") lost his eyes, and his last prayer was to avenge them [Judges 14:3, 16:21, 16:28]
- His captors gave their god Dagon the credit and made him entertain them; there, he asked God for strength [Judges 16:23–28]
- The New Testament still lists him among the faithful [Hebrews 11:32, 11:39]

## Caption
Hero or villain? The answer is in Judges 13–16 and Hebrews 11:32–34. Read it yourself.
Two things most people miss:
1. Hebrews 11 lists him among those who through faith "were made strong out of weakness" (Hebrews 11:32–34).
2. The last verse of Judges echoes his words: "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (Judges 21:25).
Send this to someone who thinks Samson's strength was in his hair.
