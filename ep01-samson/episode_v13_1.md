# Delilah tried to hand Samson over three times. So why did he tell her his secret?
series: ASK THE TEXT
target_seconds: 85
answer: She framed guarding his secret as not loving her, and pressed him day after day until his soul was vexed to death. Ropes couldn't hold him; her words got through.

## Landing
VO: So why did Samson tell Delilah? She framed guarding his secret as not loving her, and she pressed him day after day. [Judges 16:15–17]
VISUAL: The Philistines lead him away; the prison mill at Gaza in cold light, Samson in bronze shackles, eyes closed, no gore (JUDGES 16:21).
VO: If someone makes guarding what God gave you feel like a betrayal, that isn't love. [Judges 16:5, 16:15–17]
VO: Bowstrings couldn't hold Samson. New ropes couldn't. Her words got through. [Judges 16:9, 16:12, 16:16–17]
VISUAL: Back to the opening view of Delilah's room, now empty: snapped bowstrings, snapped ropes and the loom on the floor beside seven locks. End card.

## Value
- Each Philistine lord offered Delilah 1,100 pieces of silver [Judges 16:5]
- While promised silver to hand him over, she accused him: "You have mocked me these three times" and "your heart is not with me" [Judges 16:5, 16:15]
- The text names the cause: she pressed him "day after day," "his soul was vexed to death," and "he told her all his heart" [Judges 16:16–17]
- The text says Samson loved Delilah; it never says she loved him [Judges 16:4]
- By his third lie he was already talking about his hair, and he still broke free [Judges 16:13–14]
- "When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart," she sent for the lords, who "brought the money in their hands"; but "he did not know that the LORD had left him" [Judges 16:18, 16:20]
- What we learn: if someone makes guarding what God gave you feel like a betrayal, that isn't love [Judges 16:15–17]
