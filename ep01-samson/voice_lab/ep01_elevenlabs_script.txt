If God is still using you, does that mean He's okay with how you're living?

Let's look at Samson, known as the strongest man in the Bible. His strength came from God. Yet he slept with a prostitute in Gaza. That same night, while men lay in wait to kill him, he pulled up the city gate, posts and all.

Later, he fell in love with Delilah, and the Philistine lords, Israel's enemies, offered her silver to find the secret of his strength.

Three times she said, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" And three times he broke free.

Then he told her his secret. While he slept, she had a man shave off his uncut hair, the sign he was set apart to God.

She called a fourth time. He woke up and said, "I will go out as at other times and shake myself free."

... "But he did not know that the Lord had left him." ...

Notice what he said: "as at other times." It had always worked before, so he assumed it would work again.

You can be gifted, lead, and help people. But God still using you doesn't mean He approves of how you're living.

Jesus warned that on judgment day, many will point to the mighty works they did in His name, and He will declare, "I never knew you."

But Samson's story doesn't end there. Blinded and brought out to entertain his captors, he prayed, "O Lord God, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once..." Even then, he asked for revenge.

His choices had cost him his eyes and his freedom. Earlier, he had assumed he would escape. Now, he asked God for strength.

Then he pushed against the pillars with all his strength, and the building fell on the Philistine lords and everyone inside. Samson died with them.

And the Bible adds: "So the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those whom he had killed during his life."

Samson's last act began with a prayer. And centuries later, Hebrews eleven names him in a list of people who, through faith, "were made strong out of weakness."

What stands out more to you about Samson: his strength, his failures, or his prayer at the end? Why?
