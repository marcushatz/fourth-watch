# Directing principles (compact). Sources: ClaudeAnimationBase/PDoomVideo (storyboard first; model the viewer; one read at a time); getkeyframes.com (each scene drawn for the words it sits under, changing on the exact word); our Lintel measurements (research/script_rules_from_hits.md, direction_rules.md); the pillars demo.

1. **Story before shots.** Answer the analysis (13 §2) first. A shot that can't say which question it answers is cut.
2. **One concept per sequence.** Pick a governing image and keep it: motifs develop, they aren't sprinkled.
3. **The verb is the picture.** Whatever the voice says happens is shown happening, on its word (anticipation 0.2-0.4 s before; the meaning held 0.5 s after).
4. **Model the viewer.** Every read has a start, an end and a path for the eye. One important read at a time. Fast actions, slow meanings.
5. **Word-locked change** (Keyframes): the picture changes on the words that carry meaning, not on a timer. A change about every 2 s; stillness only where it means something.
6. **Contrast carries meaning.** Small/huge, before/after, laughing/praying, high/low. The same frame twice says more than two frames.
7. **Motivated cuts.** Cut on action, on the noun that names the new thing, or into a match. Never just to "keep it moving".
8. **Light only on story beats,** and gold only where the text says God acted (the canon; the gate is `spec_check`).
9. **No invented events.** A picture may symbolise, but it may not claim what the text doesn't say. Attempts, departures "on the word", motives and answers to prayer all count.
10. **Pay off the opening.** The last image answers the first one.
11. **Reuse, then invent.** Look in the INDEX first. A new technique is built once as a primitive, and only when it proves itself in a render does it become a card.
12. **Passing checks is not the same as captivating.** Look at the frames and the motion; name the three worst problems; fix only those.
