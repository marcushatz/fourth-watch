**1. Question and answer**
The question was why Jonah ran from God. The answer was that it wasn't fear. He knew God was merciful and would spare Nineveh, and he didn't want that. Clarity: 4/5. It lost a point because nobody says why Jonah would hate Nineveh being spared. I don't know who Nineveh was, so he just looks petty.

**2. Where I'd swipe**
- First risk: line 2, "for their evil has come up before me." It sounds like a church Bible being read aloud. "It's easy to guess he was afraid. But Jonah gives his reason himself" saved it.
- Real swipe point: right after "Jonah ran because he knew God would show mercy" (about 0:50). The question is answered, so the plant part feels like an epilogue. "God appointed a worm" pulled me back.

**3. What confused me**
- "come up before me": I don't know what that means.
- "Yet forty days": does "yet" mean "still"?
- Who Nineveh is and why it matters.
- "120,000 persons": the number felt random.
- "and also much cattle": why cows?
- "slow to anger" is quoted at the end as if I'd heard it before. The Jonah 4:2 read stopped at "merciful."

**4. Repetitive, slow or preachy**
- "angry enough to die" comes right after "better for me to die than to live." It's the same beat twice.
- "evil" is said three times.
- The final "So why did Jonah run?" re-answers what was already answered at 0:50. It works as a bookend but leans sermon.
- Nothing felt judgmental.

**5. Strongest and weakest**
- Strongest: "So it wasn't fear. Jonah ran because he knew God would show mercy." Runner-up: "We never hear Jonah answer."
- Weakest: "More than 120,000 persons, 'and also much cattle.'" It's accidentally funny.

**6. New to me vs. retelling**
New:
- His whole message was one sentence.
- Jonah states his own reason.
- He wanted to die because the city was spared.
- The plant and the worm.
- The book ends on a question.

Retelling: running, storm, fish, Nineveh repents. "You know the next part" handled that well.

**7. The ending**
Mostly yes. "Slow enough to spare Nineveh, and to keep reasoning with Jonah" is a good turn. I'd tap JONAH and comment: "Jonah asked to die twice because God was too nice. Jonah, easy."

**8. Would I finish it, and would I share it?**
I'd watch to the end. I'd send it to my friend from middle-school youth group who always said Jonah was about obedience.

**9. Fixes**
- Opening quote: paraphrase it ("God told him to warn Nineveh") or keep the quote on screen only.
- "Yet forty days": say "Forty more days..." out loud. The counter already helps.
- Nineveh: add one line saying who they were to Jonah's people. That makes his anger make sense.
- Swipe point at 0:50: before the plant, add a bridge like "And the ending is stranger than the fish."
- Double death wish: cut "angry enough to die" or the earlier line.
- "slow to anger": read the full 4:2 phrase earlier ("gracious God and merciful, slow to anger") so the closing bookend pays off.
- Cattle: cut it, or own it ("God even counts the animals").
- "evil" three times: swap one for "wickedness" or drop it from the opening quote.
- Sermon-ish ending: drop the second "So why did Jonah run?" and go straight to "Because he knew God is slow to anger."
