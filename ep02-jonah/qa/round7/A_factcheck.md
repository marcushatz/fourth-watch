VERDICT: VERIFIED. No change is needed. One optional citation tweak is below.

(1) "Patient" is a fair plain-English paraphrase of 4:2's "slow to anger." The line leaves out the rest of the list ("gracious... merciful... abounding in steadfast love, and relenting from disaster"), but that is fine for a reel.

(2) Supported. 4:1 says "But it displeased Jonah exceedingly." The "it" points back to 3:10: "God relented of the disaster that he had said he would do to them, and he did not do it." So "that is exactly what displeased him" is accurate, and the line adds no motive the text doesn't give. Strictly, sparing Nineveh is "relenting from disaster" rather than "slow to anger." 4:2 lists the two together, so the line holds.

(3) Fair. God asks "Do you do well to be angry?" (4:4) and asks again in 4:9, then argues "should not I pity Nineveh...?" (4:10–11). The book ends on God's question. "The ending shows" signals that this is a reading of those verses, which is appropriate.

(4) Fair. 4:2 says "That is why I made haste to flee to Tarshish; for I knew..."
