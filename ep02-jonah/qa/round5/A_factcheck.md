**1. History line: OVERCLAIM (placement and timing). Needs rewording.**
(a) Fair. Nineveh was an old Ishtar cult center with royal palaces and temples from Shalmaneser I and Tiglath-pileser I, and building grew "from the time of Ashurnasirpal II (883–859 BC) onward." It became the capital only under Sennacherib (705–681, c. 700). So "one of Assyria's great cities" holds for Jonah's day.
(b) Correct. 2 Kgs 17:6 says "the king of Assyria captured Samaria, and he carried the Israelites away." The siege began under Shalmaneser V and Sargon II finished it, c. 722–720 BCE.
(c) Correct. World History Encyclopedia dates Jeroboam II to 786–746, so Samaria fell roughly 25–60 years later. But "later" is ambiguous: it could mean later than the repentance or later than Jonah.
(d) Yes, it implies a motive. Coming right before 4:2, the line suggests Jonah ran out of hatred of Assyria, and even that he ran because of a conquest that hadn't happened yet. Fix: "Historically, Nineveh was one of Assyria's great cities, and decades after Jonah, Assyria conquered Israel's northern kingdom. The book of Jonah never gives that as his reason."
(e) "Historically" is enough. The added last sentence makes it airtight.
Citations: Nahum 3:7 and 3:18 name Nineveh and the "king of Assyria" but say nothing about Israel. World History Encyclopedia does not mention Samaria's fall. Cite the conquest to 2 Kgs 17:6 and a history source such as Wikipedia, "Kingdom of Israel (Samaria)".

**2. Lead-in: Quote VERIFIED. "Only then" is a mild OVERCLAIM.**
The quote is exact ESV. The verse continues "merciful, slow to anger…", so on-screen text should end with an ellipsis, not a period. 4:2 opens "is not this what I said when I was yet in my country?", so Jonah says he gave this reason before. Fix: "Only then do we hear why he ran: …"

**3. Join: VERIFIED.**
4:6–7 is paraphrased accurately ("exceedingly glad", "when dawn came up the next day, God appointed a worm… so that it withered"). The 4:10–11 wording and ellipses match the ESV. "God said" claims no timing. Cutting 4:8–9 does lose the wind, Jonah's wish to die, and that God's words answer Jonah's anger over the plant. Optional bridge: "Jonah was angry enough to die over the plant. God said, …"
