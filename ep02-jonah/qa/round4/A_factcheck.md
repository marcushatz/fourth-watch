**ESV fact-check: Jonah 3–4 reel lines**

**1. VERIFIED.** The quote in 3:4 is exact: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" "One sentence, as the book records it" is a fair way to put it.

**2. VERIFIED.** "slow to anger" (4:2) and "it displeased Jonah exceedingly" (4:1) are exact. 4:2 says "That is why I made haste to flee to Tarshish; for I knew…", so "he says he ran because he knew" is fair. "Holds back disaster" is a fair unquoted paraphrase of "relenting from disaster," but "relents from disaster" is closer to the text, because the ESV word means changing course on a disaster already announced.

**3. CORRECTION.** "Sent a worm" is fair as an unquoted paraphrase of "appointed a worm that attacked the plant." Still, "appointed" is the text's own repeated verb (4:6, 7, 8), and "God appointed a worm" is closer. "I do well to be angry" is an exact run of consecutive words. Placing it right after the plant withers leaves out 4:8 (God appointed a scorching east wind, the sun beat on Jonah's head, and he asked to die). It also hides that Jonah is answering God's question. Fix: "God asked, 'Do you do well to be angry for the plant?' Jonah said, 'Yes, I do well to be angry, angry enough to die.'"

**4. OVERCLAIM (small).** "Patient" is a fair paraphrase of "slow to anger." 4:4–11 does show God reasoning with Jonah: two questions (4:4 and 4:9), then the argument in 4:10–11, which ends in a question. The problem is that, as written, the line can sound as if Jonah ran because he knew God would keep reasoning with *him*. That adds a motive the text doesn't give. Fix: "Because he knew how patient God is: patient enough to spare Nineveh. And the ending shows he's patient enough to keep reasoning with Jonah too."
