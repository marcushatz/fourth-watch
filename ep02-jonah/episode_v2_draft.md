# Why did Jonah run from God instead of going to Nineveh?
series: ASK THE TEXT
target_seconds: 105
answer: Jonah says it himself: it wasn't fear. He ran because he knew God is gracious and merciful and relents from disaster, and when God spared Nineveh, it displeased him exceedingly.

## Hook
VO: Why did Jonah run from God instead of going to Nineveh?
ON-SCREEN: WHY DID JONAH RUN FROM GOD INSTEAD OF GOING TO NINEVEH?
VISUAL: A harbour. Jonah's hand drops coins into a sailor's palm on "run"; the mooring rope slips free on "Nineveh" and the ship pulls away as the camera pulls back. Pill: TARSHISH.

## Stakes
VO: God told him to go to Nineveh, "for their evil has come up before me." Jonah found a ship going to Tarshish instead, and paid the fare. [Jonah 1:2-3]
VO: It's easy to guess he was afraid. But Jonah gives his reason himself. [Jonah 4:2]

## Story
VO: You know the next part: the storm, the sailors, the great fish. Then he went to Nineveh. [Jonah 1:4-17, 3:3]
VISUAL: A fast run, one shot per item (1.1–2.2 s each): the storm; the sailors hurl him into the sea (1:15); the fish; Jonah on dry land (2:10); then Jonah walking into the city.

## Turn
VO: But notice his message, as the book records it. One sentence: "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" [Jonah 3:4]
VISUAL: A counter on screen: 40 DAYS.
VO: And the people of Nineveh believed God. [Jonah 3:5]
VISUAL: The people put on sackcloth, "from the greatest of them to the least of them"; then the word reaches the king, who sits in ashes (3:5–6). No animals in sackcloth (only the decree mentions them, 3:8).
VO: When God saw them turn from their evil, He didn't bring the disaster. [Jonah 3:10]
VO: That's when we finally hear why Jonah ran. [Jonah 4:1-2]
VERSE: "That is why I made haste to flee to Tarshish; for I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful," — Jonah 4:2 (ESV)
VO: So it wasn't fear. Jonah ran because he knew God would show mercy. And when God spared Nineveh, "it displeased Jonah exceedingly." [Jonah 4:1-2]

## Landing
VO: Then he asked God to take his life: "it is better for me to die than to live." [Jonah 4:3]
VO: God made a plant grow to shade him, and Jonah was overjoyed. At dawn, God appointed a worm, and the plant withered. Jonah said he was "angry enough to die." [Jonah 4:6-9]
VO: God said, "You pity the plant, for which you did not labor… And should not I pity Nineveh, that great city…?" [Jonah 4:10-11]
VO: More than 120,000 persons, "and also much cattle." And the book ends right there, on God's question. We never hear Jonah answer. [Jonah 4:11]
VO: So why did Jonah run? Because he knew God is "slow to anger." Slow enough to spare Nineveh, and to keep reasoning with Jonah. [Jonah 4:2, 4:4-11]

## Question
VO: Who surprises you more in this story: Jonah, or the people of Nineveh? [Jonah 3:5, 4:1]

## Value
- Jonah gives his own reason for running, and it is God's mercy, not fear of Nineveh [Jonah 4:2]
- The book records one sentence of his message to Nineveh, and the city believed God [Jonah 3:4-5]
- The book ends on God's unanswered question about 120,000 persons "and also much cattle" [Jonah 4:11]

## Caption
Why did Jonah run? He gives the reason himself. Read Jonah 4:1–11, then chapter 3.
