Strongest candidates

  1. Hand-Drawn Ink. The most distinctive and the most adult of the ten. It reads at phone size, its restraint suits Scripture, and each frame takes seconds. The risk is that a simulated brush could look mechanical once it moves.
  2. Dark Cinematic. The closest to film: real light in real space, figures found by a single edge of light. The cost is render time.
  3. Monochrome Film. The shadow scene holds the single strongest idea in the study: the offer happens twice, once small and once huge on the rock. Black and white would also give the brand's gold one job, as the only colour on screen where the text says God acted. That pairing isn't tested here.
  4. 2.5D Atmospheric. The best balance of beauty and practicality. The layers are built for slow camera moves, and only the figure changes from frame to frame.

The decision is yours. These four are my reading of the pictures, not audience evidence.

What Claude Code could realistically animate

  • Straightforward: Premium Vector, Geometric Editorial, Minimalist Silhouette and Neon Noir. Every shape is a number, a frame renders in about a second, and poses can be eased between key positions.
  • Realistic with care: Hand-Drawn Ink, 2.5D Atmospheric and Painterly. Paint the world once per shot, redraw only the figure each frame with the same brush seeds so it doesn't flicker, and move the camera through the layers.
  • Not practical on this machine: Dark Cinematic, Monochrome Film and Stylized 3D. Posing them is easy, but a frame takes 1 to 4 minutes here. A 132-second film at 24 frames a second is about 3,200 frames, which is several days of rendering. A computer with a graphics card, or held shots with slow camera moves, would change that.

Notes

  • Silhouette figures and flat vector shapes were turned down in an earlier round. They are included because this brief asks for them.
  • Two ideas came up that aren't built: ink with a single touch of gold, and monochrome film with gold. Both follow the brand rule that gold marks where God acts.
  • Motion tests are the next real question for any of these. They need your approval first, because the production lock covers rendering video.
