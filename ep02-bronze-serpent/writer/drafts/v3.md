## Hook
VO: Moses destroyed a golden calf because Israel worshiped it. [Exodus 32:8, 32:20]
ON-SCREEN: HOW DID SOMETHING GOD COMMANDED BECOME AN IDOL?

## Stakes
VO: Centuries later, a faithful king, Hezekiah, destroyed a bronze serpent for the same reason. [2 Kings 18:3-4]
VO: The difference? The calf broke God's command. The serpent came from it. [Exodus 32:8; Numbers 21:8]
VO: And long after it was smashed, Jesus used it to explain eternal life, just before John 3:16. [John 3:14-16]

## Story
VO: In Numbers 21, God sent fiery serpents. His cure: "Make a fiery serpent and set it on a pole." Moses made it of bronze. Anyone bitten who looked at it lived. [Numbers 21:6-9]

## Turn
VO: Second Kings 18 lists it right beside the pagan objects Hezekiah destroyed: "the bronze serpent that Moses had made." [2 Kings 18:4]
VO: Because "the people of Israel had made offerings to it." [2 Kings 18:4]
VO: Opposite beginnings, same ending. But the life had come from God's promise, not the bronze. [Exodus 32:8; 2 Kings 18:4; Numbers 21:8]

## Landing
VO: So why would Jesus bring it up? [John 3:14]
VO: He told a Jewish leader, Nicodemus, "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up." [John 3:1, 3:14]
VERSE: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up" — John 3:14 (ESV)
VO: John 12 explains the phrase: it showed "what kind of death he was going to die." [John 12:32-33]
VO: Then comes John 3:16, which can be translated, "For this is how God loved the world." [John 3:16]
VO: How? He gave his only Son, lifted up like the serpent. Whoever looked, lived. Whoever believes in him "should not perish but have eternal life." [Numbers 21:8-9; John 3:14-16]
VO: So how did something God commanded become an idol? People held on to the bronze and brought offerings to it. Hezekiah destroyed the object. Jesus kept the meaning. [2 Kings 18:4; John 3:14-16]

## Value
- God never commanded the golden calf, but he did command the serpent; both ended with offerings made to them [Exodus 32:8; Numbers 21:8; 2 Kings 18:4]
- Second Kings lists Moses' serpent right beside the pagan objects Hezekiah destroyed; the life had come from God's promise, not the bronze [2 Kings 18:4; Numbers 21:8]
- John says "lifted up" pointed to how Jesus would die [John 12:32-33]
- John 3:16 begins with "For"; the ESV notes it can read "For this is how God loved the world", right after the serpent comparison [John 3:14-16]
