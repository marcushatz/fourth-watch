## The question people ask
- Search evidence: "Was Moses' Bronze Serpent an Idolatrous Graven Image?" (National Catholic Register), and related results for golden calf vs bronze serpent.
- The common answer: the object wasn't idolatrous because God commanded it; the sin was people offering to it. True, but it stops short. It doesn't ask what each object was a picture of, or why Jesus chose that one.

## Kept in reserve (true, strong, cut for length)
- **Paul sets both disasters three verses apart** (1 Cor 10:7, 9), and says those destroyed by serpents had put Christ to the test (some manuscripts read "the Lord"). Strong enough for its own episode.
- **Aaron dies one chapter before the serpent** (Num 20:28; 21:4).
- **The calves come back.** Jeroboam repeats Aaron's words (1 Kgs 12:28), and 2 Kgs 17:16 names the two calves among the reasons the north fell, one chapter before Hezekiah crushes the serpent.
- **They asked God to take the serpents away; he gave them one to look at** (Num 21:7-8).
- **Moses warned against the likeness of "anything that creeps on the ground"** (Deut 4:18).

## Traps
- Levites killed the three thousand (Ex 32:28), and the plague is separate (Ex 32:35). Say "died", not "God killed".
- The text doesn't say why people made offerings to the serpent, or when it began.
- John never says the serpent stood for sin. That reading comes from putting John 3 beside 2 Cor 5:21, so it has to be attributed.
- "Lifted up" means his death only through John 12:32-33. Tag it on screen.
- ESV main text of 3:16 is "For God so loved the world". "For this is how…" is the footnote, so say "can be translated".
- ESV has "broke in pieces" at 2 Kgs 18:4. "Crushed" is the gloss of the Hebrew verb.
