# The approved narration (Marcus, Nov 1 16:32). Do not change without his permission.

**Title: Why Did God Command Something That Later Had to Be Destroyed?**

God told Moses to make a bronze serpent. Centuries later, a faithful king smashed it to pieces. Then Jesus used it to explain eternal life.

Why?

In Numbers 21, deadly snakes were biting the Israelites. They admitted their sin and asked Moses to pray that God would remove the snakes.

But God gave a different answer.

He told Moses to raise a bronze serpent on a pole. Anyone bitten could look at it and live.

Now here's the problem.

Hadn't God forbidden images?

Exodus 20 forbade making images to bow down to and serve. The bronze serpent wasn't meant to be worshiped. It was a sign of God's provision.

But centuries later, something changed.

Second Kings 18 says the Israelites were burning incense to it. They called it Nehushtan—a name connected to the Hebrew words for serpent and bronze.

So King Hezekiah smashed it.

What once reminded them of God's mercy had become an object of worship.

But the strangest connection comes centuries later.

In John 3, just before John 3:16, Jesus tells Nicodemus:

"As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up."

In the wilderness, the dying looked to what God provided and lived. Jesus says those who believe in Him may have eternal life.

Now think about Hezekiah's decision.

He wasn't destroying God's miracle. He was destroying something people had put in God's place.

And that raises a question for us.

What good thing in your life could quietly become more important than the God it was supposed to point toward?
