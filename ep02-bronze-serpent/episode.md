# How could something God Himself commanded become an idol?
series: ASK THE TEXT
target_seconds: 100
answer: The serpent was made at God's command so the bitten could look and live; centuries later Israel made offerings to the object itself, so Hezekiah destroyed it, yet Jesus revealed the meaning that survived it: it was pointing toward Christ.

## Hook
VO: Moses destroyed a golden calf because Israel worshiped it. [Exodus 32:8, 32:20]
ON-SCREEN: HOW COULD SOMETHING GOD HIMSELF COMMANDED BECOME AN IDOL?
VO: Centuries later, King Hezekiah destroyed another object Israel had begun worshiping. [2 Kings 18:4]
VO: But this one had been made at God's command. [Numbers 21:8-9]
VO: How could something God Himself commanded become an idol? [Numbers 21:8; 2 Kings 18:4]

## Stakes
VO: In Numbers 21, deadly serpents were killing the Israelites. [Numbers 21:6]
VO: The people confessed their sin and begged Moses to ask God to take them away. [Numbers 21:7]
VO: But God's answer was unexpected. [Numbers 21:8]

## Story
VO: He commanded Moses to raise a serpent on a pole. Anyone who had been bitten could look at it and live. [Numbers 21:8-9]
VO: They weren't told to make offerings to it. They were told to look, and live. [Numbers 21:8-9]
VO: Yet centuries later, according to Second Kings 18, the Israelites were doing exactly what God had never commanded. [2 Kings 18:4]
VO: They were making offerings to the serpent itself. [2 Kings 18:4]
VO: So Hezekiah smashed it. [2 Kings 18:4]

## Turn
VO: Centuries after its destruction, Jesus brings up that very serpent while speaking to Nicodemus. [John 3:1, 3:14]
VO: "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up." [John 3:14]
VERSE: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up" — John 3:14 (ESV)
VO: He was speaking of His coming death on the cross. [John 12:32-33]
VO: In the wilderness, dying Israelites looked at what God provided and received life. [Numbers 21:9]
VO: Those who believe in Him may receive eternal life. [John 3:15]

## Landing
VO: And just two verses after Jesus mentions the serpent comes one of the most famous verses in Scripture. John 3:16. [John 3:14-16]
VO: It can be translated, "For this is how God loved the world." [John 3:16]
VO: Hezekiah destroyed the serpent. Yet Jesus revealed the meaning that survived its destruction. [2 Kings 18:4; John 3:14-16]
VO: Because the story was never ultimately about the serpent. It was pointing toward Christ. [John 3:14-16]

## Value
- God commanded the serpent, unlike the golden calf [Exodus 32:8; Numbers 21:8]
- Centuries later Israel made offerings to the serpent itself, so Hezekiah smashed it [2 Kings 18:4]
- Jesus brought up the destroyed serpent two verses before John 3:16, which can be translated "For this is how God loved the world" [John 3:14-16]
