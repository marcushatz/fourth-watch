## 2. The visual idea: one stone pedestal, three things on it
**The film's spine is a single plinth (the pedestal)**, always framed the same way: a low three-quarter view, upper-centre.
- In beat A, the golden calf stands on it, then the bronze serpent stands on the same plinth. The match cut *is* the comparison.
- In beat E, the serpent returns to that plinth for the reversal.
- In beat I, the plinth stands empty, and a gold line rises past it toward the cross.

The viewer *sees* that the calf and the serpent occupied the same place in Israel's worship, and that something else was meant to stand in that line of sight.

**Two visual vocabularies, used consistently:**
- **Light, for the origin.** God's command is the gold beam from the upper left (series canon: gold only where God acts). People's making is warm ember light from fires and hands. The calf only ever gets ember; the serpent's first appearance gets gold.
- **Gaze vs offering, for the purpose.** A *gaze line* (a fine gold line from people's eyes) passes up through the serpent: look and live. An *offering* is a bowl or incense set at its base, and the line stops there. Beat D shows both side by side in one frame.

**Shots I removed or never planned (the value test):**
- No separate "Hezekiah walks in" shot: the swing is in shot 2.
- No smash re-run in beat E: shot 12 is a new angle, 1.6 s long.
- No establishing shot of Jerusalem by day.

- No repeated Moses walks.

**Accuracy fences, carried over and updated:**
- God commanded "a fiery serpent"; Moses made it of bronze. The visuals show a bronze serpent, as Moses made it.
- **No prostration anywhere:** 2 Kgs 18:4 says "offerings", so people kneel holding bowls.
- The serpent's location in Hezekiah's day isn't stated: a generic stone court, never labeled "temple".
- The pole is straight, with no crossbar.
- The cross appears only inside the comparison panel (shot 15) and as the distant final image (shot 21). Both are **visual interpretation** grounded in John 12:32-33, never a scene in the conversation.
- The ghosted figure with the bowl in shot 9 is styled as a counterfactual (ghost grade plus an X), not as an event.
- The empty pole beside the cross in shot 21 is symbolic: a composite landscape at dawn, not a claim that the pole survived.
- Ex 32:4: Aaron "fashioned it with a graving tool". The hands in shot 3 are shown working gold; no face.
- Jesus follows the logo design: lit, coloured, no facial acting.

## 4. The most technically demanding shots
1. **Shot 1, the calf's burn into gold dust:** fire over a metallic mesh, then a dissolve into a particle mound.
2. **Shots 2 and 12, the serpent shattering:** pre-fractured pieces with analytic flight, plus the hammer swing.
3. **Shots 3 and 15, the panel compositor:** two live 3D renders per frame (the cost doubles).
4. **Shot 10, the time-lapse set morph:** walls rising, the sky racing.
5. **Shots 18-19, the John 3 page:** a 2D card with a camera tilt, a glow and a drawn link line. Cheap, but the typography must be excellent.
6. **Shot 13, Jesus as a PP2 character** from the approved logo design.
