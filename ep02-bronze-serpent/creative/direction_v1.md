## A. Narrative analysis

**What the viewer learns, and where their understanding changes**
| When | The viewer thought | Now they understand |
|---|---|---|
| 0-12 s (hook) | The bronze serpent is a wilderness story | The same object turns up in three eras with three opposite fates. That is the mystery. |
| 22-33 s | God answers prayers by removing the problem | They asked for *removal*; God gave a way to *live* while the snakes were still there |
| 35-48 s | God made a graven image? A contradiction | The command forbids images made *to bow down to and serve*. Looking at a sign is not bowing to an idol. |
| 48-69 s | It stayed a holy relic | Centuries later people made offerings to it and named it, so a faithful king smashed it |
| 72-93 s | It's an Old Testament curiosity | Jesus used it, just before John 3:16, as a picture of himself being "lifted up" (John 12:32-33: the cross) |
| 93-116 s | Hezekiah destroyed something God made | He destroyed what it had become: something standing in God's place. That is the principle. |

**System/13's answers**
- **Central mystery:** one object, a command, its destruction, and Jesus' use of it. Why?
- **Emphasis moments:** "look… and live" (the gesture), "smashed" (the impact, then the silence), "lifted up" (the comparison).
- **Feeling:** dread (the snakes) → relief (looking and living) → unease (the bowing) → shock (the smash) → quiet awe (night) → a reflective question (dawn).
- **What pays off the opening:** the opening's close-up composition returns at the end with the pole *empty*, and a figure looks past it, up to the light.

**Accuracy fences** (what no picture may claim)
- **Num 21:6-7:** the snakes bit and "many people… died". Show the bites with no gore, and show deaths only as the emptied place. The people came to Moses; **Moses** prayed.
- **Num 21:8-9:** God said "make a **fiery** serpent and set it on a **pole**"; Moses made it of bronze.
  - **A straight pole, no crossbar.** We must not make a cross shape in the wilderness.
  - **The snakes are still there after the pole goes up.** The text gives a remedy, not a removal.
- **2 Kgs 18:4 (ESV):** the people "had made offerings to it (it was called Nehushtan)". **The text doesn't say where it stood.** Show a generic stone court, never labeled "temple". The offerings are incense smoke and small bowls, which fits "made offerings" (ESV) and "burned incense" (NIV, KJV).
- **2 Kgs 18:7, "the LORD was with him":** Hezekiah may have the faint gold light (the gold rule).
- **John 3:2:** Nicodemus came "by night". The place isn't given, so show a lamp-lit courtyard, not a rooftop.
- **John 3:14 is quoted, and "lifted up" = the cross by John 12:32-33.** The cross appears **only inside a framed comparison panel**, as a distant silhouette, never as a live event in the conversation scene.
- **Jesus:** drawn lit and colored, with the approved logo design. No facial acting; dot eyes.
- **No picture implies that all images are forbidden.** The X goes on the act of *bowing to an image*, never on the serpent.

**Precision notes on the approved script** (raised, not changed; see `.claude/skills/fourth-watch-writer/reference/gold_bronze_serpent.md`)
1. Num 21:8-9: God said "fiery serpent", and Moses made it of bronze.
2. The ESV of 2 Kgs 18:4 says "made offerings", not "burning incense".
3. The ESV says "it was called Nehushtan", not "they called it".

The pictures stay accurate under either wording. Marcus decides whether to touch the words.

## B. Three treatments (they differ on image, space, camera grammar, light and transitions)

### T1 "The Mark" (RECOMMENDED)
**ONE recurring composition** (*the mark*): a low three-quarter view of the serpent atop its pole, upper centre, in the 22-45% band. Every era is a new world built around the same frame.
- **Image:** the object never moves. People, place and light change around it.
- **Space:** a high wide for each era first (the bird's-eye "where are we"), then the mark.
- **Camera grammar:**
  - pull out from the object to reveal;
  - a crane on time jumps;
  - locked on the mark for every rhyme.
- **Light:**
  - wilderness: dusk gold;
  - worship: amber haze with cool shadow;
  - the smash: hard morning;
  - Nicodemus: blue night with one warm lamp;
  - the principle: dawn.
- **Transitions:** match-dissolves *on the serpent* between eras, plus one time-lapse where the walls rise around it.
- **The gaze-line motif** (from T3): a fine gold line from people's eyes.
  - In the wilderness it passes *through* the serpent, up to God's light: a sign points beyond itself.
  - In the idol era it *stops* at the serpent.
  - At the end it rises past the empty pole.
- **Risk:** the repeated framing can feel static. The fix is that every return changes the place or the people, never just the caption.

### T2 "The Case File"
The spine is an evidence board: a parchment timeline (Numbers → Exodus → 2 Kings → John), with the serpent pinned as Exhibit A.
- **Image:** cards and exhibits.
- **Space:** the board, flying into 3D dioramas.
- **Camera grammar:** a push along the timeline, a dive into each exhibit.
- **Light:** a neutral board, with each exhibit lit for its era.
- **Transitions:** pushes and whips along the board.
- **Risk:** the clearest logic but the weakest emotion. It slides toward the slideshow/diagram look Marcus rejected, and the smash and the night scene lose scale.

### T3 "Eyes Up, Eyes Down"
Told by the **direction people look**:
- the wilderness from a worm's-eye view (looking up past the serpent to the sky);
- the worship from directly above (bowed backs, eyes down);
- the smash from the serpent's point of view (the hammer coming down);
- Nicodemus at eye level (looking at Jesus);
- the end: eyes up again.

**Risks:**
- The POV shots hide *where we are*, so they fail a muted test more often.
- It needs the most new poses.
- A worm's-eye of a pole risks a cross-like silhouette in the wilderness.

### The pick: T1 with T3's gaze as the motif
**The concept in one line:** *the object stays; the eyes move.*
- T1 gives the "same object, same framing, new relationship" that sequences A, D and G demand.
- T3's look-up vs bow-down gives a visual vocabulary that reads without sound.
- T2's timeline survives only as small era pills ("NUMBERS 21", "2 KINGS 18", "JOHN 3") on the time jumps.

## C. Concept: motifs, emotion keys, transition grammar

**Motifs**
| Motif | First | Develops | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The mark** (the serpent on its pole, low 3/4) | S1, close up | S9 raised; S17 the same frame, people bowing; S19 the same frame, smashed | S30: the same frame, the pole empty at dawn |
| **Look up vs bow down** | S10, a bitten man lifts his head and lives | S14 the crowd looks, nobody bows; S17 prostrate worshippers | S25 Nicodemus looks up at Jesus; S30 a figure looks up past the pole |
| **The gaze line** | S10 through the serpent to God's light | S15 many lines, all passing upward | S17 the lines stop at the serpent; S28 the serpent is shown sitting where the light belongs; S30 one line rises past the empty pole |
| **Smoke vs light** | S8 God's gold light | S17 incense smoke rises and *pools around* the object | S20 the smoke breaks up after the smash; S28 the gold light falls through the emptied space onto Hezekiah (18:7) |

**Transition grammar**
- **Match-dissolve on the serpent:** era changes (S2, S3, S16).
- **Hard cut on the verb:** actions (bite, raise, smash).
- **Crane up or down:** time and place jumps (S5, S21, S29).
- **Fade:** the memory panels (S23, S24, S27).
- No whip pans: this film is reverent and inquisitive. The whip is saved for an episode with travel.
