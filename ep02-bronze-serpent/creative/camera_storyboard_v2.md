# The Bronze Serpent: camera storyboard v2 (Nov 2)

The art style is approved and unchanged. This document is about the camera only.

**Rule:** every camera decision has a narrative job. If a move can't say what it reveals or emphasises, the shot is locked.

## 1. What the Lintel camera study shows
Source: `research/lintel_ref/yt/_patterns.md`, 12 Shorts, 265 shots, measured.

- **About 60% of their shots are effectively locked.** A measurable move (5% or more) happens in 41% of shots; a big move (15% or more) in only 14%. Movement is rationed, which is why it lands when it comes.
- **Big moves only do four jobs:**
  1. A hook reveal: a pull-out from one character to the world (Tree, −42% in 1.2 s; Never heard, −64%).
  2. Travel that links scenes or times (Cain: people → garden → cross in one move).
  3. Following a walker.
  4. A push into a revelation (Never heard: the Athens altar, +22%; Job: through the opening door, +37%).
- **Shot length:** median 3.8 s, one clause per shot. Lists and parallel items cut short (1.2–2.5 s); travels and held diagrams run long (6–10 s).
- **One action per clause, on the verb.** The cut or the gesture lands on the verb.
- **Keep the set, change the moment.** A story stays on one set and changes framing, light or time, not location.
- **The match dissolve.** Their strongest single transition is tree → cross (Tree 69.8 s): the same shape carries the meaning across.
- **God's action is a beam from above,** in all 12 videos.

Where Lintel is weaker, and what we take further:
- Their default is one elevated isometric wide (60–70% of shots), so their camera rarely changes angle.
- We keep their discipline (rationed, motivated moves) but add the full range: extreme close, low hero, aerial, the subject's own view, and match cuts that carry an idea.

## 2. Fourth Watch camera language
- **Distance carries meaning.**
  - Extreme wide or aerial = scale, consequence, God's view.
  - Wide = where everyone stands.
  - Medium = an action.
  - Close = one person's moment, or an object's identity.
  - No two shots in a row share the same distance and angle.
- **Angle carries meaning.**
  - Low = authority or the monumental (Moses commanding, the raised serpent, the king's blow).
  - High = vulnerability, a multitude (the bitten).
  - Aerial = scale, or heaven's view.
  - The subject's own view (over the serpent's head) = what the people are looking at.
  - Eye level = the human moment.
- **Movement starts on the motivating word and settles on a word.** The allowed jobs:
  - reveal (pull-out or crane);
  - isolate (push-in);
  - follow an action (tilt or crane with an object, pan with a falling object, track with a walker);
  - travel (between places or eras);
  - carry an idea (a match dissolve).
  - No drift-zoom "for life"; a held frame with acting in it is better.
- **Composition in 9:16:**
  - stack depth vertically (foreground low, subject in the middle third, background high);
  - keep the top quarter for text;
  - put the subject on a third, or dead centre only when everything converges on it (the over-the-serpent shot);
  - use a foreground element in at least every other shot.
- **Transitions:**
  - a hard cut on the verb by default;
  - a dissolve only between eras;
  - a match dissolve when two images share a shape and an idea (pole → cross, look → look).

Variety in 10 seconds:
- **Distances:** medium, extreme wide, medium-low, high wide, close-up.
- **Angles:** eye level, aerial, low, the subject's view, low close.
- **Moves:** a crane up with a reveal, a tilt following an action, a near-lock, a push-in.
- **Compositions:**
  - a centred figure against the sky;
  - a figure lost in the field;
  - a diagonal rising across the frame;
  - a radial crowd around a centre;
  - a face on the middle line with the object above.

## 4. The whole episode, shot by shot (camera-first)
Times are from the recorded voice. Fences:
- God commanded a "fiery serpent"; Moses made it of bronze.
- 2 Kings 18:4 says "made offerings": no prostration.
- The court is unnamed.
- The pole is straight, with no crossbar.
- The cross appears only in the comparison panel and the final image.
- Gold light appears only where God acts.
- Jesus is lit, in logo design, with no facial acting.

### Line 0: "Moses destroyed a golden calf because Israel worshiped it." (0–3.9), after a 0.9 s cold open
- **S0 (cold open, title):**
  - Start: extreme close-up, low, on the bronze serpent's coil.
  - End: its head against the dusk sky.
  - Move: a tilt up the coil, 0.9 s.
  - Reveals: the object of the question before any word is spoken.
  - Why: the title asks about this object, so the eye climbs to it.
- **S1 "Moses destroyed a":**
  - Start: Moses in a medium close-up, eye level, on the left third, the mountain behind him, looking right.
  - End: the same frame.
  - Move: locked, 1.1 s.
  - Reveals: who he is and what he feels (anger, Ex 32:19).
  - Why: a face that is looking off screen makes us want the next shot.
- **S2 "golden calf":**
  - Start: his view, an elevated wide over the camp.
  - End: the same frame, with Moses starting to stride in.
  - Move: locked, 1.2 s.
  - Reveals: the calf and the people worshiping it, and where Moses stands relative to them.
  - Why: the geography is clear in one picture; his stride gives us the cut.
- **S3 "because Israel worshiped it":**
  - Start: a low medium; Moses strikes the calf off its pedestal into the fire.
  - End: the fire and the people reacting, then a tilt up with the smoke.
  - Move: a whip-pan with the falling calf (0.45 s), then a tilt up (0.9 s).
  - Reveals: cause and effect, and the smoke that carries us across centuries.
  - Why: the camera follows the action rather than watching it.

### Line 1: "Centuries later, King Hezekiah destroyed another object Israel had begun worshiping." (4.3–10.4)
- **S4 "Centuries later":**
  - Start: a dissolve from the calf's smoke into incense smoke past the serpent's head, in close-up.
  - End: an elevated wide of the court.
  - Move: a crane up and back, 1.3 s.
  - Reveals: the same serpent from S0, now with people bringing offerings to it.
  - Why: the move reveals the twist; a cut would only state it.
- **S5 "King Hezekiah":**
  - Start: a low hero medium; the king on the left third, the serpent on the right third.
  - End: he lifts the hammer.
  - Move: locked, with a slight push in on the windup, 0.8 s.
  - Reveals: intent (anticipation).
  - Why: the low angle gives him authority; the stillness holds the breath.
- **S6 "destroyed":**
  - Start: a close-up, low, on the serpent's head (the S0 frame).
  - End: it shatters toward the lens.
  - Move: one shake, 0.6 s.
  - Reveals: the impact.
  - Why: cutting on the blow to the opening image destroys what we first saw.
- **S7 "another object…worshiping":**
  - Start: high, on the broken pole.
  - End: a high wide with two rows of offerers still holding bowls to nothing.
  - Move: a crane up and back from "object", 2.2 s.
  - Reveals: how many had worshiped it.
  - Why: a pull-out from the evidence to the extent of the worship.

### Line 2: "But this one had been made at God's command." (10.8–13.1)
- **S8:**
  - Start: a wide, eye level, the two objects side by side: the serpent screen left, the calf screen right in ember light.
  - End: a medium on the serpent as gold light falls on it alone on "God's".
  - Move: a lateral track left with a 10% push, 2.3 s.
  - Reveals: the difference (commanded, Num 21:8; made by people, Ex 32:4).
  - Why: the track physically leaves the calf behind and arrives at the commanded object.

### Line 3: "How could something God Himself commanded become an idol?" (13.5–16.7)
- **S9:**
  - Start: the serpent alone in gold light, in a medium.
  - End: high overhead. As the camera rises, a ring of offering bowls and kneeling figures dissolves in around it.
  - Move: a crane straight up, 3.2 s.
  - Reveals: the question in one image: the object stays the same while the worship forms around it.
  - Why: a vertical move matches the vertical idea ("lifted up", then worshiped). Done static, it would be a picture with a caption.

### Line 4: "In Numbers 21, deadly serpents were killing the Israelites." (17.3–21.6)
- **S10:**
  - Start: an extreme close-up at ground level: a red-brown snake slides across the sand toward a fallen hand.
  - End: a low wide of the camp at dusk, sick people on the ground, more snakes.
  - Move: a rising pull-back, 4.2 s.
  - Reveals: the threat first, then its reach.
  - Why: the hook reveal (Lintel's job 1) on a new set.

### Line 5: "The people confessed their sin and begged Moses to ask God to take them away." (22.0–26.9)
- **S11 "The people confessed their sin":**
  - Start: a tracking shot at eye level, following two people supporting a third toward Moses' tent.
  - End: the tent opening.
  - Move: track, 2.0 s (Lintel's job 3, following a walker).
  - Reveals: who comes, and where to.
  - Why: the walk is the confession, made visible.
- **S12 "and begged Moses…away":**
  - Start: a high angle over kneeling people.
  - End: the same frame; Moses in the doorway, small at the top.
  - Move: locked, 2.9 s.
  - Reveals: the begging, with Moses above them.
  - Why: the high angle makes them small; holding lets their gestures carry it.

### Lines 6–7: the camera test, T1–T4 above (27.3–36.0)

### Line 8: "They weren't told to make offerings to it. They were told to look, and live." (36.5–41.4)
- **S13 "They weren't told to make offerings to it":**
  - Start: a ground-level close-up of an untouched offering bowl in the sand (foreground, soft); the crowd beyond.
  - End: a rack focus past the bowl to the faces looking up.
  - Move: a slow push past the bowl, 2.4 s.
  - Reveals: what wasn't commanded (the bowl lies unused) and what was (looking).
  - Why: the foreground object carries the negative; the focus change carries the turn.
- **S14 "They were told to look, and live":**
  - Start: a lateral track at eye level along a line of faces turned up.
  - End: the healed man from T4 in frame.
  - Move: track right, 2.5 s.
  - Reveals: the same act repeated person after person.
  - Why: the track turns one example into a rule.

### Line 9: "Yet centuries later, according to 2 Kings 18, the Israelites were doing exactly what God had never commanded." (41.8–49.8)
- **S15 (travel, 8 s):**
  - Start: the wilderness serpent at sunset, in a low wide.
  - End: the same serpent on a stone plinth in a court, crowded with offerers.
  - Move: a crane up into the sky (2.5 s); the sky cycles through days (a time-lapse colour shift); the camera descends into the court (3 s), settling on a high three-quarter view.
  - Reveals: time passing, and what changed (the place and the worship) and what didn't (the object).
  - Why: Lintel's job 2. One continuous move makes the centuries feel like a single line of events, which is the episode's argument.

### Line 10: "They were making offerings to the serpent itself." (50.3–52.8)
- **S16:**
  - Start: a medium close-up of a bowl lifted toward the camera, incense smoke rising.
  - End: focus racks to the serpent's head behind it.
  - Move: rack focus plus a small tilt up, 2.5 s.
  - Reveals: the bowl is offered to the bronze ("itself").
  - Why: the focus pull draws the line from the offering to its target.

### Line 11: "So Hezekiah smashed it." (53.2–54.7)
- **S17:**
  - Start: top-down, directly overhead of the plinth.
  - End: the hammer head enters frame top and bronze pieces scatter outward across the floor.
  - Move: locked, 1.5 s.
  - Reveals: the destruction as a pattern of pieces.
  - Why: a new angle on an event we've already seen in line 1: decisive and final, not a repeat.

### Line 12: "Centuries after its destruction, Jesus brings up that very serpent while speaking to Nicodemus." (55.5–62.2)
- **S18 "Centuries after its destruction":**
  - Start: the same top-down on the pieces.
  - End: the light shifts to night and the stones dissolve into a lamp-lit rooftop.
  - Move: locked, then a cross-dissolve, 2.5 s.
  - Reveals: time, and a new place, on the same composition.
  - Why: a match dissolve on the frame keeps the serpent's story continuous.
- **S19 "Jesus brings up… Nicodemus":**
  - Start: a crane down from above the roof.
  - End: an eye-level two-shot, Jesus (lit, logo design) and Nicodemus seated, a lamp between them (John 3:2, "by night").
  - Move: crane down, 4.2 s.
  - Reveals: who is speaking and when.
  - Why: descending from God's-view height into a private night conversation.

### Line 13: "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up." (62.6–68.3)
- **S20 "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness":**
  - Start: the two-shot.
  - End: the night sky above them.
  - Move: a tilt up as Jesus' hand rises, 1.8 s. Then a match dissolve to the wilderness serpent against the evening sky (the T2 end frame), 1.4 s.
  - Reveals: the image Jesus is pointing to.
  - Why: a tilt up plus a match dissolve on the vertical shape.
- **S21 "so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (the comparison panel):**
  - Start: the serpent full frame.
  - End: a split frame: the serpent on its pole on the left, a cross on a hill on the right, both lifted against the same sky.
  - Move: a pull-back that opens the split, 2.6 s.
  - Reveals: the parallel Jesus draws (John 3:14).
  - Why: the move builds the comparison in front of the viewer.

### Line 14: "He was speaking of His coming death on the cross." (68.7–71.2)
- **S22:**
  - Start: the split frame.
  - End: the cross half fills the frame, in silhouette with dawn light behind it.
  - Move: a push into the right panel; the serpent panel slides out; 2.5 s.
  - Reveals: the meaning of "lifted up" (John 12:32–33).
  - Why: a push into a revelation (Lintel's job 4).

### Line 15: "In the wilderness, dying Israelites looked at what God provided and received life." (71.6–77.2)
- **S23:**
  - Start: the over-the-serpent high angle (the T3 frame), crowd faces turned up.
  - End: the same frame as gold light sweeps across the crowd and figures rise.
  - Move: locked, 5.6 s; the light moves, not the camera.
  - Reveals: looking and life, for many.
  - Why: a callback to T3. Holding lets the light do the healing.

### Line 16: "Those who believe in Him may receive eternal life." (77.6–80.6)
- **S24:**
  - Start: a close-up of the healed man's lifted face (the T4 end frame).
  - End: a match cut to Nicodemus' face lifted toward Jesus in the lamp light, the same framing.
  - Move: a match cut with matching slow push-ins, 3.0 s.
  - Reveals: "look and live" becomes "believe and live".
  - Why: the match cut is the visual explanation. Two faces in one composition across 1,000+ years.

### Line 17: "And just two verses after Jesus mentions the serpent comes one of the most famous verses in Scripture. John 3:16." (81.2–90.2)
- **S25:**
  - Start: a close-up on a parchment column at John 3:14 (highlighted).
  - End: two verses down, John 3:16, highlighted.
  - Move: a vertical track down the text, 6.0 s, landing on "3:16" as it is spoken.
  - Reveals: the distance is literally two verses.
  - Why: tracking the column shows the proximity.

### Line 18: "It can be translated, 'For this is how God loved the world.'" (90.6–94.6)
- **S26:**
  - Start: the 3:16 line.
  - End: "this is how" isolated, with warm light.
  - Move: a push-in, 3.9 s.
  - Reveals: the word that points back to the serpent (*houtōs*, "in this way").
  - Why: a push into the revelation the episode has been building.

### Line 19: "Hezekiah destroyed the serpent. Yet Jesus revealed the meaning that survived its destruction." (95.0–101.7)
- **S27 "Hezekiah destroyed the serpent":**
  - Start: the top-down on the pieces in the court (the S17 frame), cold light.
  - End: the same frame.
  - Move: locked, 2.5 s.
  - Reveals: a callback to the destruction.
  - Why: stillness = finality.
- **S28 "Yet Jesus revealed the meaning that survived":**
  - Start: the same top-down.
  - End: a crane up and away until the pieces are small; warm light grows from the top of frame.
  - Move: crane up, 4.2 s.
  - Reveals: the object is gone; what remains is above it.
  - Why: the rise separates the meaning from the metal.

### Line 20: "Because the story was never ultimately about the serpent. It was pointing toward Christ." (102.1–107.9), the final image
- **S29:**
  - Start: the wilderness serpent at sunset, a low wide; its long shadow falls across the sand.
  - End: the shadow leads to a cross on a far hill (the final symbolic image); the serpent pole is in the foreground, the cross on the horizon.
  - Move: a track along the shadow toward the horizon (3.5 s), then a slow pull-up to a held wide (2.3 s).
  - Reveals: the serpent literally points (by its shadow) toward the cross.
  - Why: the camera travels the exact line of the argument and ends on the image that answers the title. The last frame mirrors the cold open's vertical (serpent → cross).
