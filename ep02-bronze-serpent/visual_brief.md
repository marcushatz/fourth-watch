# Bronze Serpent: visual brief

**Status:** draft for the pilot. The narration is candidate r5, which isn't locked.

## The film's one image

Two metal animals in the dark, then a pole beside an empty line. Everything else serves those two pictures.

## What only this film does

It keeps accounts. A simple cost ledger starts as a comparison, waits with one empty row, and is completed by John 3:16.

## What returns, and how it changes

The serpent on its pole. It heals, gathers smoke, breaks, and finally stands beside a second line rising at the same height.

## Colour story

Gold light appears only where the text says God acted: on the serpent when God orders it, and on the last line of the ledger. The calf is gold metal, but it never receives that light. Ember belongs to judgement. Parchment belongs to Scripture.

## How the visual language changes

- **Real objects stay physical.** The calf, serpent and broken bronze have weight and light.
- **People live in painted layers.** Their movement carries the story.
- **The argument leaves the world.** When the script compares, the scene falls away and the ledger builds.
- **At the climax, the words become the picture.** The exact verse folds into two columns, and the lines rise from them.

## Boldest shot

The verse divides on "As" and "so", with "lifted up" aligned across both columns. A pole rises on one side and an empty line on the other. The crossbar arrives only on "his cross".

## What this film refuses

- Depicting Jesus.
- Gore, or showing how the men died.
- Anyone bowing before the serpent.
- A crossbar on the pole.
- A sun disc behind a centred subject.
- Pictures that only repeat the narration.
