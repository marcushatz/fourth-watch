## Scene block (identical in both)

The scene is a rocky wilderness at dusk. A single bronze serpent with a wedge-shaped head and a hammered, dark-patinated surface winds twice around the top of one straight wooden pole. The pole has no crossbar. It stands in a low mound of stones on the right third of the frame, rising above a camp of low black goat-hair tents. In the near foreground at lower left, a weakened, weathered ancient Near Eastern man in a plain undyed wool robe lies on the stony ground, propped on one arm. He lifts his head and looks up at the bronze serpent, seen in three-quarter profile, with quiet, desperate hope. There are no wounds and no blood. Behind him a few small figures support one another, and some turn their faces toward the pole. Two small dark snakes slide between the stones near his hand. The camera is very low, close to the ground, with a 35mm lens looking up past the man's shoulder toward the serpent. This creates one strong diagonal from lower left to upper right. There are five depth layers: blurred foreground stones, the man, the camp, distant ridges and open sky. The camp has fallen into cool blue-umber shadow. One narrow beam of warm gold light comes from the upper left and falls only on the bronze serpent, with a faint gold reflection on the man's lifted face. Nothing else in the scene is gold. The palette is night umber, clay, ash grey and deep dusk blue, with warm gold as the only bright accent. Vertical 9:16 composition. The top quarter of the frame is calm, empty sky, with the serpent just below it. No text, letters, logos, halos or symbols.

## Why these models

Evidence is limited. Neither model has been tested on our art.

- **GPT Image 2.5 for A:** a hands-on review found GPT Image 2 strongest on classical painting. It's an earlier version, and the same review saw its depth collapse in places, which the five-layer brief tests.
- **Nano Banana Pro for B:** the same review found Nano Banana 2 stronger on spatial layout. Nano Banana Pro also works with Higgsfield Elements, which matters for repeating 3D characters. The catalog tags it as photorealistic, so it may drift away from stylized.
- **Higgsfield's model guide** gives no recommendation for illustration or 3D.
