# Marcus's v4 (Nov 1, 17:54), cuts only: his own words, plus one factual fix (Num 21:7), about 246 words, about 95 s
Moses destroyed a golden calf because Israel worshiped it.
Centuries later, King Hezekiah destroyed another object Israel had begun worshiping.
But this one had been made at God's command.
How could something God Himself commanded become an idol?
In Numbers 21, deadly serpents were killing the Israelites.
The people confessed their sin and begged Moses to ask God to take them away.
But God's answer was unexpected.
He commanded Moses to raise a serpent on a pole. Anyone who had been bitten could look at it and live.
They weren't told to make offerings to it. They were told to look, and live.
Yet centuries later, according to Second Kings 18, the Israelites were doing exactly what God had never commanded.
They were making offerings to the serpent itself.
So Hezekiah smashed it.
Centuries after its destruction, Jesus brings up that very serpent while speaking to Nicodemus.
"As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up."
He was speaking of His coming death on the cross.
In the wilderness, dying Israelites looked at what God provided and received life.
Those who believe in Him may receive eternal life.
And just two verses after Jesus mentions the serpent comes one of the most famous verses in Scripture. John 3:16.
Hezekiah destroyed the serpent. Yet Jesus revealed the meaning that survived its destruction.
Because the story was never ultimately about the serpent. It was pointing toward Christ.
