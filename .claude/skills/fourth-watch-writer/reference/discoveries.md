# What counts as a discovery (Stage 2)

A discovery is a true thing from the text that **changes what the viewer believed**. Write each one with a before and an after. If the after equals the before, it is not a discovery.

## The kinds that work (with real examples)
| Kind | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The overlooked detail** | A clause most readers skip | They asked God to *remove* the snakes; God gave a way to *live* instead (Num 21:7-8) |
| **The apparent contradiction** | Two texts that seem to clash, resolved by reading precisely | A serpent image vs "you shall not make… bow down… serve" (Ex 20:4-5) |
| **The later look-back** | A later book names or quotes the earlier event | Hezekiah breaks Moses' serpent (2 Kgs 18:4); Jesus names it (John 3:14) |
| **The same object, person or phrase, changed** | It returns, and its meaning has shifted | The serpent: rescue → idol → rubble → Jesus' comparison |
| **The word on the page** | A meaning given by the ESV text or its footnote, or a standard lexicon | Nehushtan "sounds like the Hebrew words for both 'bronze' and 'serpent'" (ESV note) |
| **The reversal of the common answer** | What people usually say, shown wrong or incomplete by the text | "Cain's offering failed because it wasn't blood": Leviticus 2 has grain offerings that God accepts |
| **The speaker's own words, turned back** | A character's words that echo or invert earlier ones | Jacob deceived his father in the dark; Laban says "the younger before the firstborn" (Gen 29:26) |
| **Context the text assumes** | History or a custom, from a named source, labeled as such | Labeled HISTORY in the dossier, and said as history in the script ("Historians…"), never as Scripture |
| **What the text doesn't say** | Correcting an embellishment | Genesis 33 never says "forgave"; Exodus never says the Pharaoh drowned |

## What does NOT count (cut on sight)
- **Trivia that doesn't move the argument.** "Methuselah lived 969 years" is a number, not a discovery, unless the argument needs it.
- **Look-alike links.** "Both have twelve…", "both on a hill…". It must pass the **link test**: the later text names or quotes the earlier one, or a mainstream reading links them *and the script says it is a reading*.
- **Folk etymology, gematria, "hidden messages" in names or letters.** The R3alism "secret message" genre (research/transcripts.md, #11) is the warning.
- **A scholar carrying the claim alone.** Scripture carries the argument (Lintel hits: 0 outside authorities). History may be cited as history, with its source.
- **Speculation about motives or feelings** the text doesn't give ("Jacob was furious", "it was crushing her").
- **The famous fact restated** ("Jonah was swallowed by a great fish").

## Strength test
Answer these in words before arguing. No scores.

- **Surprise:** do most churchgoers already know it?
- **Load-bearing:** does the story break without it?
- **Visual:** can it be shown as an object, an action or a place?

Drop anything that doesn't change the story.
