# Why our earlier scripts were weak, and the Bronze Serpent wasn't

Every example below is real, with Marcus's verdict in his words. Read this before drafting.

---

## Failure 1: the retelling (Ep 1 Samson, v57)
**Question:** "If God is still using you, does that mean He's okay with how you're living?"

**What the script spends its words on** (lines from `ep01-samson/episode.md`):
> "Let's look at Samson, known as the strongest man in the Bible. His strength came from God. Yet he slept with a prostitute in Gaza. That same night… he pulled up the city gate, posts and all."
> "Later, he fell in love with Delilah… Three times she said… And three times he broke free. Then he told her his secret…"

**Measured:** 49% of the runtime retells the story (the Lintel hits: 0-5%). The turn comes at 53 s (the hits: 14-25 s). 379 words.

**Marcus:** "disappointed… simply not good enough" (Round 15). "the storytelling is taking too much presence rather than giving value" (Round 8).

**Why it's weak:**
- The viewer already knows Delilah, the hair and the pillars.
- The few real discoveries ("as at other times", Hebrews 11's "made strong out of weakness") are buried after 50 s of retelling.

**The fix in gold-standard terms:** enter at the scene that holds the first discovery, and spend one line on what everyone knows.

## Failure 2: the fact pile (Ep 4 Esau and Jacob, v6)
**Question:** "Why did God 'hate' Esau and love Jacob, the liar?"

Its lines hop through 7 books:
- Genesis 27;
- Malachi 1;
- Genesis 29;
- Deuteronomy 23;
- Obadiah;
- Romans 9;
- Genesis 28, then back to Genesis 33.

> "And Esau's people? God told Israel, 'You shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother.'"
> "When Edom later turned on Jacob's people, God condemned it 'because of the violence done to your brother Jacob.'"
> "Paul quotes 'Esau I hated' while writing about the twins…"

**Marcus:** "an actual piece of dogshit, it makes no sense, i dont understand what the clear message is, there is so much that you left unanswered" (Round 26).

**Why it's weak:**
- No single thread. Each fact answered a complaint from an AI test viewer, so the script grew by patching.
- It gave five partial answers (nations, "loved less", election, the promise, Esau the good brother), and none of them was *the* answer.
- The facts were true, but they didn't transform each other. They could be shuffled without loss.

**Contrast:** the gold standard has 4 books, all about one object, and each era changes the object's meaning.

## Failure 3: the famous story with verse dumps (the Leah options)
> "When the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb." Then the names: "now my husband will love me"… and then "This time I will praise the LORD."

**Marcus:** "what up with the incorrect grammar weird way of talking… these stories are full of parts that are so wasteful and non intriguing" (Nov 1).

**Why it's weak:**
- The beats were the famous story (the bride swap, the rivalry).
- It read archaic ESV phrases aloud as if they were the content.
- The outline was written in note fragments ("25-60s THE DIARY: each name pops…").

**What would have worked:** discoveries the viewer doesn't have. For example, Jacob tells Leah's own sons "my wife bore me two sons" (Gen 44:27); the birthright went to Rachel's son while the crown went to Leah's son (1 Chr 5:1-2, Gen 49:10). Write them in plain modern sentences.

---

## Failure 4: the soft answer (Ep 3 early drafts)
The early drafts of "What is the unforgivable sin?" defined it, but never said *why* it can't be forgiven. That was the question every viewer had next.

**Marcus:** "i stilll dont know if it provides enough value… im just disappointed u missed all this" (Round 21).

**Lesson:** list the viewer's next questions before drafting, and answer every one.

## The success: the Bronze Serpent (approved)
| Test | Samson v57 | Esau v6 | Leah options | Bronze Serpent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Familiar retelling | 49% | ~15%, but scattered | ~70% | ~15%, in one place |
| One thread | yes (Samson), but no investigation | no (7 books, 5 answers) | yes, but famous | **yes: one object** |
| Each discovery transforms the last | no | no (shuffleable) | no | **yes: sign → idol → rubble → Jesus' picture of himself** |
| Contradiction raised and resolved | no | partly | no | **yes: "Hadn't God forbidden images?"** |
| The viewer's next questions answered | partly | no | no | **yes: why destroy it? weren't images banned? why would Jesus use it?** |
| Plain spoken English | mostly | yes | no (archaic dumps) | **yes** |
| The principle follows from the evidence | weakly | no | — | **yes: "the object didn't change; their use of it did"** |

**The pattern to copy** (not the content):
1. Three facts that don't fit, in the hook.
2. "Why?"
3. Enter the story at the discovery.
4. Voice the objection.
5. Resolve it precisely.
6. Time-jump to a later text.
7. A link the text itself makes.
8. Re-read an earlier fact in that light.
9. One principle, asked as a question that accuses no one.
