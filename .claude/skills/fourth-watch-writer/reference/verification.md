# Verification: labels, sources and the traps we've fallen into

Scripture integrity is nonnegotiable (`EDITORIAL.md`). It doesn't mean avoiding interpretation or hard passages. It means the viewer can always tell what Scripture says from what we conclude. This file merges the writer's and the story skill's rules, so there is one set of labels.

## Label every consequential claim

Keep this as the claim table in `writer/claims.md`, one row per claim the script makes.

| Label | Meaning | How the narration may say it |
|---|---|---|
| **TEXT** | The ESV says it explicitly | As fact, or quoted word for word |
| **INFERRED** | A fair inference from the text | "It seems," "this suggests," or a line that shows the reasoning |
| **INTERPRETATION** | A reading mainstream interpreters hold, which others may not | Attributed: "Many Christians read this as..." If Christians differ, say so in a line or in the caption. It adds depth but never carries the episode alone |
| **HISTORY** | An outside fact from a named, reputable source | As history ("Historians...", "In that culture..."), never as Scripture. The source goes in the table |
| **VISUAL** | A picture or symbol, not a claim about events | Note in the plan how it could mislead |
| **UNSUPPORTED** | A motive, thought, feeling or detail the text doesn't give | Not in the script. Cut or rewrite |

## Sources, in order

1. **The ESV text,** fetched in this session. Try esv.org first, then bible.com (version 59) or biblehub's ESV pages. Never quote from memory. The fetch tool won't return long ESV passages, so fetch short verses one at a time for exact wording. biblegateway is often blocked.
2. **ESV footnotes,** for word meanings and alternative readings.
3. **Mainstream study resources,** for what a reading *is*, not for what the text says: ESV Study Bible notes, Got Questions, The Gospel Coalition, BibleProject and standard commentaries.
4. **History and archaeology:** encyclopedias, university or museum pages, and peer-reviewed summaries, cited in the table.

A WebSearch first puts the URL in provenance, so WebFetch can then read it.

## Rules

- **Quotations are exact ESV,** and quoted verbs match. A shortened quote must still say what the verse is saying (Round 56).
- **Name the writer when it matters,** and don't blend Gospels into a voice none of them used.
- **Report what people say and do.** Don't invent inner life, private thoughts or God's motives. When the text gives a human reason and God's reason, say both.
- **Never invent dialogue.** A contrast like "not 'How could you?'" must be plainly ours, never sound like a quote.
- **Check the order of events verse by verse (Round 56).** A retelling that swaps two actions is an error, even when each action is true.
- **Check who the text addresses.** "Called to the man" is not "called to them". A collective description isn't applied to one person.
- **Never say "the only reason" without checking the whole Bible (Round 56).** Say what isn't needed, not what isn't true.
- **Keep the judgment in (Round 56).** If a story shows mercy, it also states what the sin cost.
- **Open questions are allowed.** Say what's known and what isn't, without implying a hidden answer.
- **Original-language claims need a lexicon or a commentary.** A similar-looking word proves nothing.
- **A disputed reading is never the discovery,** and the story must stand without it.
- **Confidence on screen can't exceed the support.** A dramatic verse card can make a guess look proven, and staging can imply events the text never records.
- **Never promise what the text doesn't promise,** especially around grief, abuse, illness and despair. Don't turn someone's pain into a lesson. Have a person with pastoral experience read scripts that touch these.
- **A command given to one person isn't general advice,** and a parable isn't a recorded event. Say which it is.

## Traps we've already fallen into

| Trap | Real case | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| A quoted verb that isn't the ESV's | "came upon him" for "rushed upon him"; Malachi 1:3 is "I **have** hated" | Quoted words match the ESV exactly, in the book you name |
| An implied event stated as fact | "God answered" (Samson); "he forgave" (Genesis 33 has no "forgave") | Show what the text records, and let the viewer infer |
| A future announcement stated as done | "God judged Edom" from Obadiah, which says "shall" | Match the tense ("condemned", "warned") |
| A flat definition | "'Hated' means loved less" | "can mean", with the counter-evidence named |
| One side of a debate taken silently | "God chose Jacob's line" (the Romans 9 debate) | Neutral wording, or name both views |
| A character's words stated by the narrator as fact | "whose face was like seeing the face of God" | "whose face Jacob said was..." |
| An action overstated | "the liar got lied to" (Laban deceived by action, never stated a lie) | Use the text's own verb |
| "Last", "only", "never", "all" | "the last time..." | Only if the text makes it so |
| History presented as Scripture | Nineveh's size (Jonah) | Say it as history, with its source |
| Another translation's word used as the ESV's | "burning incense" (NIV) where the ESV has "made offerings" | Paraphrase freely, but don't say "the Bible says X" when the ESV says Y |
| God's motive stated as fact | "He didn't need an answer. He wanted them." (Genesis v11) | Say what the question shows, and attribute the reason as our reading |

## Known traps by passage

| Passage | Trap |
|---|---|
| Genesis 3 | They hear God, then hide (3:8). God calls "to the man" (3:9), and Adam answers that he was afraid (3:10). The fruit isn't called an apple. "Where are you?" is also where judgment begins, so it isn't only tender. The clothes (3:21) come before they are sent out (3:23) |
| Why God made people | Scripture gives more than one purpose: his glory (Isaiah 43:7), "for him" (Colossians 1:16), and filling and ruling the earth (Genesis 1:28). "By your will" is Revelation 4:11. Saying he didn't need us doesn't deny these |
| Storm | It isn't the Mark 4 storm, and no boat is said to be sinking. No Gospel says why he came when he did. "Meant to pass by" is debated |
| Hagar | Ishmael isn't a toddler in Genesis 21. The well isn't said to appear from nowhere: her eyes were opened |
| Mary at the tomb | John doesn't say why Jesus appeared to her first. "Still dark" doesn't place it in the fourth watch |
| Peter's charcoal fires | Both are in John, but no text says Jesus chose the fire to remind him. The switch between words for love is disputed |
| Naomi | Her grief is real. Don't say she didn't appreciate Ruth |
| Elijah, 1 Kings 19 | Don't diagnose him. His wish to die (19:4) needs care and a pastoral read. The ESV has "the sound of a low whisper" (19:12), not "still small voice". "What are you doing here?" (19:9, 13) is open to readings, so don't state that it is a rebuke |
| The prodigal son | It's a parable. "A running father was shameful in that culture" is a debated historical claim, not text |

More are in `research/curated_archive/04_scripture_story_dossiers.md`.

## The fact-check brief (step 6, with Marcus's OK)

Use the ESV fact-checker brief in `prompts/`, plus these lines:
- "Label each line TEXT / INFERRED / INTERPRETATION / HISTORY, and say whether the script presents it at the right level."
- "Rule on every cross-reference: does the later text name or quote the earlier one, or is it a reading? If it's a reading, does the line say so?"
- "Flag any motive, thought or feeling given to God or a character that the text doesn't state."
