# Directing

How to turn a story into shots. Full evidence is in `research/direction_rules.md`, from 24 videos on three channels. Camera detail is in `system/06_camera_and_angles.md` and `system/06c_shot_recipes.md`.

## Principles

1. **Story before shots.** A shot that can't say which question it answers gets cut.
2. **One concept per sequence.** Pick a governing image and develop it.
3. **The verb is the picture.** What the voice says happens is shown happening, on its word.
4. **One important read at a time.** Fast actions, slow meanings.
5. **The picture changes on meaning, not on a timer.** Stillness only where it means something.
6. **Contrast carries meaning.** Small and huge, before and after, the same frame twice.
7. **Cut for a reason.** On action, on the noun that names the new thing, or into a match.
8. **Light moves on story beats.** Gold only where the text says God acted.
9. **No invented events.** A picture may symbolize, but it can't claim what the text doesn't say.
10. **Pay off the opening.** The last image answers the first.

## Reads

A read is one thing the viewer must find and understand. Give each one a start, an end and a way for the eye to get there.

| What must be read | Minimum time |
|---|---|
| A new place | 0.8 s, or 0.5 s for the same set |
| A new person or prop | 0.5 s, or 0.3 s if a look, motion or light leads the eye |
| An action on its word | The move can take 0.2 to 0.6 s, then hold its meaning 0.5 s |
| Text the narrator isn't reading | 0.35 s plus 0.25 s per word |
| A light change that carries meaning | 0.8 s |
| The turn or payoff image | At least 1.2 s, camera locked or barely moving |

The picture for a named thing lands within 0.15 s of its word.

## Pace

- Something visibly changes about every 2 seconds. A person acts, something enters or changes, a label lands, the light shifts, or the camera arrives somewhere new. Captions don't count.
- Median shot 3 to 5 seconds, and nothing under 1 second except a true transition.
- A set can hold 10 to 12 seconds if things keep changing inside it.
- Spend the longest hold on the turn.

## Camera

Camera amount didn't separate hits from lows, but every big move has one of five jobs:

| Job | Example |
|---|---|
| Hook reveal | Pull out from a person to the whole world |
| Travel | Link scenes or time |
| Follow | Stay with a walker |
| Reveal | Push into a revelation |
| Carry | Move one picture into the next |

Use high wide shots for the bird's-eye story and low angles for scale or awe. Put a foreground layer in at least every other shot.

## Pop-ups

Labels, icons, arrows, rings and numbers keep things moving. Time them to words. Use one at a time, about every 1.5 to 3 seconds at most, never over a face, and never two text elements at once.

## Recipes

These worked on real shots.

| Job | How | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| A verb lands on its word | Lock the camera. Dip the other way 0.25 s before, drive 0.2 s past the target, settle 0.25 s, hold 0.5 s | A move too small for the shot size reads as nothing |
| Show who caused it | Keep cause and first effect in one frame, then cut wide | Cutting away between them breaks the link |
| Show how big something is | Cut from close to extreme wide on the word that names it, with the person small and central | Cut 0.5 s early so the place can be read |
| Follow a force | Start low at the point of contact, then crane up over 1.1 s | Keep the person in frame |
| A big consequence | Cause close, wide for the event, then the aftermath. Shake at most 12 px over 0.45 s | Keep the person a readable size |
| A loss with no gore | Show the emptied place afterward, with a slow 3% push, for at least 1.2 s | A top-down view may not read |
| Irony | Keep the person sharp, with the oblivious crowd soft behind them | Only if the text never says they stopped |
| A prayer the text doesn't answer | High angle, head lifts on the verb, no light change | Pair it with a size change |
| Pay off the opening | Repeat the hook's shape, size and height in frame | Rhyme the composition as well as the action |

## Transitions

Carry motion through the cut where you can: a whoosh, a shape that becomes the next scene, a light wipe, or one match-dissolve for a symbolic link. Keep flashes for true jumps in place or time.
