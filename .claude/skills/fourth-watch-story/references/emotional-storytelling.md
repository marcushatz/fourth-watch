# Emotional storytelling

Marcus: "It should discover compelling human stakes within Scripture and determine what emotional experience the story can genuinely deliver." A viewer feels something because of what happens to someone, not because a line says it's moving.

## How feeling is built

1. **Establish what the person wants.** Without a want, nothing can be lost.
2. **Make them specific.** One named person in one place beats "the people." One small concrete fact beats a summary.
3. **Put something in the way.** Let the obstacle grow, or let hope rise before it falls.
4. **Let the outcome stay uncertain.** Suspense needs a meaningful future in doubt. Delay alone isn't suspense.
5. **Let an event turn the feeling.** A shift needs a cause the viewer sees.
6. **Hold the moment.** After the peak, say less. Explaining a climax weakens it.

## Emotional progression

Plan a path, not a category. "Sad" tells you nothing about what to write. A path tells you what each beat must do.

Write it with the event that causes each change. This example applies Marcus's progression to Hagar in Genesis 21. The feelings are the viewer's, because the text doesn't report hers:

| Feeling | Caused by |
|---|---|
| Helplessness | Water runs out and she can do nothing |
| Unexpected intervention | God hears, and her eyes are opened |
| Relief | She sees the well |
| Gratitude | The boy lives, and his name means God hears |

Other paths the text might offer:

- Unease → curiosity → understanding → warmth → awe
- Grief → confusion → recognition → joy
- Pride → humiliation → obedience → astonishment
- Fear → reassurance → failure → rescue → worship

These are examples. Find the path in the passage. If the text offers a quiet, steady feeling instead, don't force a swing.

## The understanding path

Plan the viewer's understanding the same way: what they assume at the start, what complicates it, and what they understand by the end. The two paths should strengthen each other. The best moments change both at once.

## Honest sources and fake versions

| Experience | Honest source | How it gets faked |
|---|---|---|
| Awe and wonder | Scale, or something larger than expected | Calling it "incredible" |
| Heartbreak | A specific person's loss, told plainly | Sad music over a general statement |
| Joy and relief | A real reversal the story has earned | Promises the text doesn't make |
| Encouragement | Someone ordinary acting while afraid | Telling viewers they can do it |
| Motivation | A person choosing at real cost | Commands and slogans |
| Astonishment | A plain fact that overturns an assumption | Calling it a secret or code |
| Conviction | Seeing yourself in the story | Accusing the viewer |
| Finally understanding | A hard question solved by evidence | A confident answer with no evidence |

## What the research supports

These are general findings, not rules for Bible Shorts.

- Stories that absorb people can shape beliefs, and imagery and identification matter (Green & Brock, 2000).
- Shifts in emotion may matter beyond intensity, and may keep attention through a story (Nabi & Green, 2015). One later study linked emotional shifts to absorption and intent to share, while effects on attitudes were mixed (Winkler et al.).
- Suspense comes from conflict and uncertainty around a meaningful outcome (Lehne & Koelsch, 2015).
- Awe, anger and anxiety were linked to sharing news articles, and sadness to less sharing (Berger & Milkman, 2012). That was news, not video, so it doesn't mean sad stories are bad.

From our own small comment sample: viewers talk about themselves ("describing my life in 71 seconds"), about people, and about the final line ("goosebumps"). They rarely praise a clever fact. Treat this as a clue, because the sample is eight videos.

## Limits

- Never invent a feeling, motive or thought the text doesn't give. Describe what happened and let the viewer feel it.
- Never tell viewers what to feel, and never describe their spiritual state.
- Be careful with grief, abuse and illness. Don't turn someone's pain into a lesson.
- A promise the text doesn't make can hurt someone who cried out and wasn't rescued.
- Hope should be honest, so don't resolve what Scripture leaves unresolved.
