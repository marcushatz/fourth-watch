# Annotated scripts

Three strong scripts and three weak ones, analyzed sentence by sentence, plus what actually happened on screen. This is the core of the skill.

**Read sections 1 and 2 every time.** Open a script's table when your story resembles it.

**How to use this file**

- Learn the moves. Never reuse, paraphrase or adapt these scripts, their questions or their topics.
- Notes say what each sentence does, not what it says. Sentence numbers follow the lines in `research/lintel_transcripts_all.md`. Elijah follows the shot table in `research/other_ref/bibleproject/rlFAnG_VR20.md`. Storm v3 is `ep10-fourth-watch/writer/script_v3.md`, numbered in reading order.
- "On screen" comes from the shot studies in `research/lintel_ref/yt/`, which give the exact timing.
- **[O]** is observed in the script or data. **[H]** is our hypothesis about why. The Viewer column is our reading, so treat all of it as [H].
- Views are snapshots: Instagram for Lintel, YouTube for BibleProject. Channel, age and topic are uncontrolled, so they can't be compared across channels. Popularity isn't proof of quality.

## 1. What the six scripts teach

### Find the four parts first

| Script | Desire | Obstacle that grows | What can't be undone | Unexpected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tree | The viewer wants to trust God | He knew, then every war, then every law | The choice in the garden | The obvious fix would empty the world of choice |
| Never heard | The viewer wants the stranger to have a chance | One way, and a man who died without hearing | His death | The question lands on the viewer |
| Elijah | God wants his people back | A dead boy, then a nation gone astray, then a rival god | Elijah soaks his own altar before everyone | Fire falls, and later he is taken up alive |
| Show himself | The viewer wants proof | It doesn't grow. Sources change instead | Nothing | The calf is one, and it passes in seconds |
| Doorway | A family wants to live through the night | Told as a summary of history | The door is painted in 3.8 s by a man we just met | None. The surprise offered is a word |
| Storm v3 | Never stated clearly | Wind and fear, in sequence | Peter steps out, as one event among many | "They." isn't an outcome |

[H] In both strong arguments the viewer is the character. Every weak script is missing a part or rushes it.

When a story has no character, such as Genesis 1, the viewer's own question has to carry the desire.

### Nine principles and how they reach the screen

**1. One person carries the idea from start to finish.**
Never heard opens on one lit man, returns to him at 44.9 s, and ends with another man bringing a lamp to his door. Show himself opens on a tiny person who never returns. Doorway reaches a family at 46 s of 60. [O]

- Narration: name one person before any doctrine.
- Framing: a wide shot that leaves them small can state the problem by itself.
- Timing: plan where they come back.
- Acting: one gesture we can read, such as a raised lamp.

**2. Raise the scale on one thread.**
Tree: one fruit, every war, every law, the cross. Elijah: one boy, one nation, the sky. Never heard: one man, every nation, you. Show himself changes source instead of scale: a philosopher, Exodus, Jesus, a parable, Pascal. Doorway moves through years, not stakes. [O]

- Narration: each section should answer "what just got bigger?"
- Framing: size follows scale. Elijah goes from a kneeling man to crowds to the sky.

**3. Let the viewer watch the easy answer fail.**
Tree proposes the fix in sentence 4 and tests it twice. The viewer reaches the conclusion a moment before the script does. [H]

- Narration: propose the fix, then follow its consequences.
- Framing: act out each "if" literally. The tree vanishes, market goods vanish, a village empties. [O]
- Acting: turn the abstract result into bodies. People jerk on strings. [O]

**4. Give the idea early when events can deepen it, and save only what's new.**
Elijah states its idea at 15% and Never heard gives its first answer at 33%. Doorway answers at 77%, and Storm v3 in its last line. Early isn't enough on its own: Show himself lands its best idea at 25% and goes nowhere with it. [O]

**5. Spend the longest hold on the turn.**
Tree gives 8.5 s to people becoming puppets, and 1.7 s and 1.5 s to war and sickness. Never heard holds 17.9 s on honest disagreement. Show himself's longest shots are its end card and a Pascal quote. Doorway gives its story's central act 3.8 s. [O]

- Timing: shot length follows importance, not a clock.

**6. Retelling works when each event does a new job.**
Elijah tells three events, and each proves the same idea at a larger scale. Doorway's sentences 2 to 10 are a summary on the way to an answer. Storm v3 retells for 23 of its 29 lines. [O]

**7. One object returns with a new meaning.**
The tree dissolves into the cross at 69.8 s. The lamp in the dark becomes the lamp someone carries to the door. In Elijah, God's action is light: fire, then a beam. Doorway's door returns as a logo. [O]

- Framing: reuse the opening composition. A callback shows the change without a word.

**8. One readable action carries each decision.**
Elijah kneels holding the boy, then pours water on the altar. The village man lifts his small lamp. In Show himself, people turn and walk away. [O] Doorway does this too, so it can't rescue a script alone.

**9. End on the meaning, then leave.**
Elijah's last line completes the first idea, with a 1.5 s logo and no ask. Tree re-asks its title and answers in one line. Never heard makes one warm, specific request. Show himself runs 14.7 s of calls to action. Doorway ends on a word origin, and Storm v3 on a pronoun. [O]

### What did not separate strong from weak

These appear on both sides, so don't credit them. [O]

- **Question hooks.** Tree, Never heard, Show himself and Doorway all open with one.
- **Fast cutting.** Doorway is the fastest in the Lintel set (median 1.9 s), and Never heard among the slowest (4.8 s).
- **Camera movement.** Doorway moved in 21 of 26 shots and Never heard made 3 clear moves. Elijah always moves.
- **Visual metaphors.** Show himself has a curtain, a candle and a lit path.
- **Length.** A 60-second script appears on each side, and a 138-second script worked.

## 3. Strong: Why didn't God remove the tree?

Lintel, 7.6M, 89 s, 269 words.

- **Concern:** if God knew, is he to blame for all this suffering?
- **Promise:** a straight answer to the objection itself.
- **Stakes:** all suffering, and whether God can be trusted.
- **Feeling path:** unease, curiosity, realization, warmth, awe.
- **How it advances:** a test that keeps failing, not a list of facts.
- **Payoff:** the question is asked again and answered in one line.
- **Why share [H]:** it answers something people hear from others.
- **Comments:** unavailable.
- **Don't copy:** the free-will explanation is a theological reading. Genesis doesn't state God's motive.

## 8. Weak: How did Peter get back to the boat?

Fourth Watch storm v3, rejected. Marcus: "The ending feels small because the insight is small."

- **Concern:** none. Nobody asks this.
- **Promise:** a small puzzle.
- **Stakes:** present in the events, but never felt.
- **Feeling path:** mild curiosity, recognition, a shrug.
- **How it advances:** by retelling, until a detail at the end.
- **Payoff:** a pronoun.
- **Why share [H]:** nothing to send.
- **Comments:** never published.

| 21-23 | The rebuke, the calm, the worship | The real ending, used as a stepping stone |

**Failure:** it found a detail and built a video around it. The men never become people, and Mark 6:52 is dropped.

**Lesson:** say what the ending means in plain words. If the meaning is small, go back to the stakes.

## 9. Worked example: Peter steps out

Our own example from storm v4, showing a story decision carried through to the screen. **Untested, and not a storyboard.** The feelings listed are what we're aiming for.

| Part | Story decision | Aim | Narration | Framing | Timing | Acting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desire | He wants to reach Jesus, not to be rescued | Curiosity | He doesn't ask for the wind to stop | Close on his face, a small figure past his shoulder | Rest on "if" | Hands grip the side of the boat |
| Obstacle grows | No Jesus, dark, wind, then a figure they fear | Ache, dread | Short lines, each adding one thing | The boat stays small in very wide frames | Long, slow holds | The rowers strain and gain nothing |
| Can't be undone | He leaves the boat | Held breath | The sentence names the dark and the same wind | Low at the water, one foot | A beat of quiet before the foot lands | The foot tests the surface, then takes his weight |
| Unexpected outcome | He walks, sinks, and help comes at once | Shock, relief | Two matching sentences about time | Wide on the distance, then close on two hands | Hold the wide through its line, then cut on "no time at all" | The hand moves fast and doesn't slow |
