# Marcus's standing rules

These bind every script and every plan. Numbers are Rounds in `research/feedback_rules.md`, and his newest words win. Evidence from other creators never overrides this file.

This file is a checklist. The full rules live in:

- `system/02_story_and_script.md` and `production/03_script_and_narration.md` for scripts
- `research/direction_rules.md` and `production/05_motion_and_cinematography.md` for the screen

## Part 1: the script

**Emotion**

- Every video aims for extraordinary value and a clear emotional response: impressed, saddened, joyful, encouraged, motivated or genuinely educated (46).
- Choose stories for their emotional pull, not for a small textual detail (46).
- Find the human stakes in Scripture first, then let the story decide what feeling it can truly deliver. Never pick an emotion and go looking for a story (47).
- Every episode has a deliberate emotional destination (39).
- Plan a progression, not a category, with an event causing each change (47).
- Intellectual depth serves the emotional experience. It doesn't replace it (39).
- Show what the person wants, fears and faces. Don't label feelings (41).
- Never manufacture emotion, invent details or exaggerate (39).
- Emotional insight comes out of the events of the story. It never interrupts them (50).
- Let the viewer experience it before explaining what it means (50).
- The ending carries human meaning, not only an explanation (50).

**Value**

- Start from a meaningful human question, conflict or struggle (39).
- Write the one-sentence message first and show it with the script. Every line serves it (26).
- Reveal something real: a connection or detail the viewer would probably never find alone, and that the text supports (28, 34). Cool connection or cut it (13).
- Keep the famous story short. Condense it rather than skip it (4, 5), and open a famous scene on what people don't know.
- Fast-forward the story. Skip minor detail, keep moving, and go further into it (50).
- Don't stop short of the famous part. If viewers expect it, it belongs (50).
- When a story runs past its opening question, make the link between the two halves unmistakable (51).
- Two or three examples set a pattern (50).
- Deliver something new about every 10 to 15 seconds: a discovery, a comparison or a change in understanding (28). See the open decisions below.
- No recaps, and never re-explain what the hook revealed (28).
- Every section pays, then gives a new reason to stay (38).
- List the questions the hook raises before drafting, and answer every one (21).
- Include one line people would repeat, and one concrete proof from the text (21).

**Structure**

- Each line answers the question the last one raised. Ask them out loud where it helps (26).
- Stay on the subject, with no side characters or tangents (6). Use about three books plus one passing fact (26).
- A question hook must be one people actually ask (4, 12).
- An opening number needs its cause, and the central verse gets room (36).
- Plant someone early if their return is the payoff (39).
- Three points is one format among many (11). Numbered points are the same type and useful to the viewer's own life (11).

**Lessons and endings**

- A lesson comes from the passage, adds something the answer didn't say, and gives the why (Oct 28, 8).
- Use at most two lessons, with no numbered sermon outline (Samson viewer tests).
- Never end on an unanswered question (Oct 28). If Scripture leaves something open, say so and land on what it does tell us (42).
- Bring back the hook's own words and answer in one line (12, 14).
- The emotional climax can be the ending (41).
- A hope line fits every viewer and uses "we" (14).
- No follow or community ask at the end (`research/direction_rules.md`, section 9).
- The ask must grow out of the message, not sit beside it (59).
- If one line is the best in the video, build the ending around it. Hold it back if you can, so it lands there for the first time (59).
- After the key moment, explain what it means, say how it applies to our lives, then conclude (60). Keep it short, and add no new verse, fact or idea after that moment. Anything the ending needs must be planted before it (61).
- A closing question must leave the viewer happy and encouraged. It can't make anyone feel judged, exposed or put on the spot. The best one has only one answer, and that answer is good news (60).

**Language**

- Plain spoken words. Say what a phrase means (13).
- Read every line aloud. If it sounds like an essay or a clever note, rewrite it. Check that no word carries a slang meaning you don't intend (57).
- When two verses say the same thing, use the one people already know (57).
- If the Bible says something that will sound strange, name the passage aloud and show it on screen, so it comes from Scripture and not from us (57, 58).
- Clear beats clever. Every line must make sense on one listen to someone who has never opened a Bible (58).
- Never speak a verse whose wording needs explaining. Say it in plain words, and put the exact verse on screen (58).
- Every major claim about God is backed by Scripture: the narrator names the passage, and the exact verse is on screen (58).
- State what a first-time viewer doesn't know before relying on it (58).
- Use the names people know, such as Adam and Eve (57).
- Write for pictures: a person doing something, or an object something happens to. One clause, one picture (`system/02_story_and_script.md`).
- Follow a quote with a plain line saying what it proves (`system/02_story_and_script.md`).
- Name the iconic detail in the narration, not only the picture (Samson viewer tests).
- Say how a climax happens in plain words before a long verse card (Samson viewer tests).
- When the text gives a human reason and God's reason, say both (Samson viewer tests).
- Name New Testament sources both ways: "In the New Testament, Hebrews 11" (Samson viewer tests).
- Never mention something the video doesn't show (13).
- Never assume the viewer's spiritual state. No churchy stock phrases (12, 13).
- At most three "you" lines (`system/02_story_and_script.md`).
- A modern example is optional (12).

**Accuracy** (more in `scripture-integrity.md`)

- Never chain facts into causes the text doesn't state (6), and never give God a motive the text doesn't state (51).
- Don't merge separate actions for rhythm (51).
- Show what the text only implies rather than stating it (13).
- Nothing is "last" unless the text makes it last, and a count stands only if every item matches (13).
- A hard verse needs its resolution in the reel (9).
- A disputed reading is never a discovery (35).
- History is labeled as history, with its source (17).
- Cut any line that raises a side issue the video won't answer (50).
- Keep clear what a verse evaluates, and don't imply singleness is incomplete (50).

**Working with Marcus**

- When the choice is open, show only options you've concluded are great, and let him choose (5, 27).
- Hand over one tight script, not a library of options (7).
- Show exact wording for approval, with word count and runtime (17).
- Say what the message is and whether every line serves it. An AI score isn't a verdict (21, 26).
- Spend effort on the viewer. One fact-check on the final candidate, changed lines only (20).
- Fix the spine rather than patching line by line (26).

## Part 2: the screen

Use these when filling in framing, timing and acting.

**Picture**

- Every spoken clause gets a picture of it happening, on its word (15, `research/direction_rules.md`, section 0).
- Every scene advances understanding. If a picture only repeats the words, redesign it (29, 30).
- Show relationships with comparisons, match cuts, spatial relationships and symbolic transitions (29).
- No figure just standing (Oct 29, 15). No repetitive movement, needless establishing shots or long static scenes (29).
- One universal style, set by the first video (48). Inside it, choose what each moment needs (37).
- A symbolic picture must not read as a recorded event (34).
- Bright enough to read on a phone (Oct 29).
- No photorealism, gore or door imagery, and no text inside generated images.
- A verse card shows the exact ESV words and reference at the moment the narrator names the passage, and stays up long enough to read (58).

**Pace**

- Something visibly changes about every 2 seconds. A long shot needs changes inside it (`research/direction_rules.md`, section 1).
- Median shot 3 to 5 seconds, and nothing under 1 second except a true transition (`production/05_motion_and_cinematography.md`).
- Impacts land on their words (Oct 29).
- Pop-ups are timed to words: one at a time, at most about every 1.5 to 3 seconds, never over a face, never two text elements at once (23).

**Camera**

- Vary distance, angle, movement and composition. Every move has a story reason (`production/05_motion_and_cinematography.md`).
- Use a bird's-eye view of the story, changing pace and setting (23).
- Don't add camera motion for its own sake (`research/direction_rules.md`, section 7).

**Characters**

- Simple and well designed: consistent proportions, strong silhouettes, expressive poses (34).
- They inhabit the world: real walking, stopping, reaching and ground contact (34, 35).
- Motion and staging come before more illustration detail (34).
- Jesus is lit and colored, never an all-black silhouette (14).

**Ending**

- The final image is the answer as a picture, and it calls back the hook (`research/direction_rules.md`, section 9).
- End within about 4.5 seconds of the last word (`research/direction_rules.md`, section 9).

**Order of work**

- The plan becomes the storyboard in `production/05_motion_and_cinematography.md` after the voice is recorded.
- Nothing is animated until he approves the storyboard (30).
- Every video is built in Higgsfield with Higgsedit (48).
- Movement rules go in the plan and Higgsfield carries them out. There is no separate movement test (48).
- Judge motion from low-res video drafts, not stills (15), and only render with his approval.
