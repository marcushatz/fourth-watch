# Blueprints

A blueprint is a successful video's structure with its words removed: the order of its beats, how long each runs, and what each one does to the viewer. We keep the structure and bring our own topic and words (Round 52).

## How to use one

1. **Pick a proven video** that's the same kind of story as ours.
2. **Map its beats.** Write each beat's job, its share of the runtime and the feeling it creates. Never copy its sentences into the map.
3. **Bring our own topic and question.** They can't be that video's topic or question.
4. **Fill each beat with our own material.** Every beat needs real Scripture behind it. If a beat doesn't fit the text, change the beat.
5. **Write the script fresh,** with the source closed.
6. **Run the check:** `python3 .claude/skills/fourth-watch-story/scripts/check_overlap.py <script> <source transcript>`.

## The line

| We reuse | We never reuse |
|---|---|
| The order of the beats | Their sentences, even reworded one at a time |
| Timing and length | Their topic or question |
| The kind of opening and ending | Their distinctive lines or images |
| How tension builds and turns | Their artwork |

Scripture always beats the blueprint.

## A blueprint isn't a guarantee

Lintel made three videos from the same garden. Its video on why God left the tree got 7.6M views, its video on hiding after sin got 48K, and its video on what would have happened if they never ate got 35K. The setting didn't decide it. The question and how it was built matter more, though channel timing and luck are uncontrolled here.

## 1. The fix that fails

From Lintel's tree video (7.6M, 89 s). Use it for a hard objection people already hold.

| Runtime | Job | Viewer feels |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 10% | State the objection in the viewer's own words | Uneasy, taken seriously |
| 10 to 18% | Scale it up to everything it touches | Morally uncomfortable |
| 18 to 45% | Try the obvious fix, and test it twice on familiar examples | Curious, reasoning along |
| 45 to 58% | Show what the fix would destroy, then the true reason | Understanding shifts, warmth |
| 58 to 71% | Give one verse a job as evidence, and make the choice concrete | Trust |
| 71 to 82% | Answer the comeback before it's asked, and widen the scale | Relief, awe |
| 82 to 92% | Ask the opening question again and answer it in one line | Satisfaction |
| 92 to 100% | One small ask and a logo | Prompted to reply |

**The longest hold** sits on the moment the fix breaks.

## 2. One person, three answers, then you

From Lintel's video on people who never heard (1.6M, 138 s). Use it for a worry about someone specific.

| Runtime | Job | Viewer feels |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 8% | Ask the worry, then make it one person | Concern |
| 8 to 12% | Show real people ask this, and promise the answer's source | Not alone, trust |
| 12 to 33% | Tell one true story that speaks to the worry | Hope rises |
| 33 to 39% | First answer, stated plainly, back on the opening person | Relief |
| 39 to 54% | Second answer, with evidence | Surprise, wonder |
| 54 to 71% | Third answer, with evidence | Reassurance |
| 71 to 84% | Admit the honest limit, and hold on it | Trust grows |
| 84 to 94% | Turn the question onto the viewer, and resolve the opening image | Responsible |
| 94 to 100% | One warm, specific action and a logo | Gratitude |

**The longest hold** sits on the honest limit.

## 3. The idea first, then rising proof

From "The Lesson Elijah Teaches You" (1.0M, 60 s). Use it for one person's life or a run of events.

| Runtime | Job | Viewer feels |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 8% | A person and a meaning, no question | Intrigue |
| 8 to 14% | A first event, stated at once | Grief to relief |
| 14 to 28% | Name the idea | Clarity |
| 28 to 52% | New stakes on a bigger scale, and set the contest | Tension |
| 52 to 71% | The person makes it harder, then the event lands | Suspense, awe |
| 71 to 80% | Everyone sees the result | Triumph |
| 80 to 86% | A moment of calm | Calm |
| 86 to 97% | The last event, and a line that completes the idea | Goosebumps |
| 97 to 100% | Logo, no ask | Quiet |

**The shape:** every event proves the same idea at a larger scale.
