# Scripture integrity

Biblical accuracy is non-negotiable. It doesn't mean avoiding interpretation or hard passages. It means the viewer can always tell what Scripture says from what we're concluding.

## Label every consequential claim

| Label | Meaning | How the script may say it |
|---|---|---|
| TEXT | The passage says it | Plainly, naming the book when it matters |
| INFERRED | A fair inference | "Seems to," "suggests," or a line that shows the reasoning |
| INTERPRETATION | Interpreters propose it, and others differ | Attribute it. It can add depth but shouldn't carry the climax alone |
| HISTORICAL | Outside fact | State it, with an independent source |
| VISUAL_SYMBOL | A picture or symbol, not a historical claim | Note how it could mislead |
| UNSUPPORTED | Motive, thought, feeling or detail not in the text | Cut or rewrite |

Keep this as a table beside the script.

## Rules

- **Quotations are exact ESV,** checked against the source in this session. Never quote from memory.
- **Name the writer when it matters.** Don't blend Gospels into a voice none of them used.
- **Don't invent inner life.** Report what people say and do.
- **Open questions are allowed.** The script says what's known and what isn't, and it can't imply a hidden answer.
- **Remove-test every source.** If removing it changes nothing, cut it.
- **Original-language claims need real support.** Check a lexicon or a commentary. A similar-looking word proves nothing.
- **Disputed readings stay labeled,** and the story must still stand without them.
- **Confidence on screen can't exceed the support.** A dramatic verse card can make a guess look proven.
- **Pictures make claims too.** Staging can imply events the text never says happened. Flag this in the creative plan.
- **Never promise what the text doesn't promise.** Be careful with lines implying that crying out guarantees rescue.
- **Check the order of events verse by verse (Round 56).** A retelling that reverses two actions is an error, even when each action is true.
- **Never say "the only reason" without checking the whole Bible (Round 56).** Before ruling out an answer, search for verses that give it. Say what isn't needed, not what isn't true.
- **Keep the judgment in (Round 56).** If a story shows mercy, it also states what the sin cost.
- **Don't let a quote stop before its point (Round 56).** A shortened quote must keep what the verse is saying.
- **A figure of speech can still claim a time (Round 56).** "That day" implies one day. Don't use it unless the text gives one.

## Hard passages

Don't skip a hard verse to keep a story clean. Quote it, keep it in the story, and be clear about what it does and doesn't tell people today. A command given to one person isn't general advice to someone in danger. Have a person with pastoral experience read scripts touching abuse or grief.

## Known traps

| Passage | Trap |
|---|---|
| Storm | It isn't the Mark 4 storm, and no boat is said to be sinking. No Gospel says why he came when he did. No exact hours are given. Mark doesn't spell out what they should have understood about the loaves |
| Storm, "meant to pass by" | A debated line. Don't make it the central revelation |
| Hagar | Her name appears in the narrator's voice, even though Abram and Sarai don't use it in their speech. Ishmael is not a toddler in Genesis 21. The well isn't said to appear from nowhere: her eyes were opened |
| Mary at the tomb | John doesn't say why Jesus appeared to her first. "Still dark" doesn't place it in the fourth watch |
| Peter's charcoal fires | Both are in John, but no text says Jesus chose the fire to remind him. The word-for-love switch is disputed |
| Naaman | "Leprosy" covers various skin diseases. The servant girl's captivity isn't a happy setup |
| Naomi | Her grief is real. Don't say she didn't appreciate Ruth |
| Genesis 3 | They hear God, then hide (3:8). The fruit isn't called an apple. "Where are you?" is also where judgment begins, so don't present it as only tender. The clothes (3:21) come before they're sent out (3:23) |
| Why God made people | Scripture gives more than one purpose: his glory (Isaiah 43:7), "for him" (Colossians 1:16), and ruling and filling the earth (Genesis 1:28). "By your will" is Revelation 4:11. Saying he didn't need us doesn't deny these |
