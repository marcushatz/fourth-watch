# Story discovery and selection

Marcus's direction: "begin with a powerful human or theological question, investigate the biblical narrative, and discover an answer that changes how the audience understands the entire event."

Start with what is at stake for someone. Details come after that, and only if they strengthen it.

## Where compelling stories come from

Look for these in the text. They overlap, and one strong source is enough.

| Source | The question to ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A costly decision | What does this person risk by choosing? | Ruth staying with Naomi |
| Consequence | What can't be undone afterward? | Peter after the rooster |
| Sacrifice | What is given up, and for whom? | A shepherd's lamb at the door |
| Mercy | Who deserved something else? | David and Mephibosheth |
| Injustice | Who was wronged, and does anyone see it? | Hagar in the wilderness |
| Hope against evidence | What reason is there to give up? | Hannah praying year after year |
| Reversal | What flips, and for whom? | Joseph and his brothers |
| Wonder | What is far bigger than expected? | The earth before the first light |
| A meaningful revelation | What new understanding changes the whole event? | Mark's bread in the storm |
| A worry people already carry | What do viewers already fear or doubt? | People who never heard of Jesus |

## Discovery questions

Answer these in prose, for the passage in front of you. The first four are the four parts in `annotated-scripts.md`.

1. **Desire:** who wants something here, and who stands to lose?
2. **Obstacle:** what stands in the way, and how does it grow?
3. **Decision:** what's the moment nobody can take back?
4. **Outcome:** what goes differently from what anyone expected?
5. What would a viewer who has never read this care about?
6. What would a viewer who knows it well not have noticed?
7. Why does this matter to someone's life this week?
8. What does the text leave unexplained?

If nobody stands to lose anything and nothing changes, it may not be a story. It can still be a good visual explainer if the idea itself is wondrous, and the viewer's own question can then carry the desire. Don't force stakes that aren't there.

## Two kinds of extraordinary value

Every video should leave the viewer with at least one, and ideally both.

- **Emotional:** they felt something real. Moved, encouraged, astonished, relieved, joyful, convicted or filled with wonder.
- **Intellectual:** they understand something they didn't before, and it changes how they see the passage.

Neither is forced. A story that moves people deeply doesn't need a hidden discovery. A clear explanation doesn't need a manufactured tear. Either one fails if it's small.

## Test the concept

Marcus requires the idea card (`templates/idea_card.md`, explained in `production/10_creative_decisions.md`) before a script. Answer it, then run these before writing a word.

| Test | A strong sign | A warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Who cares? | A real kind of person, and why | "Bible readers will find this interesting" |
| Size | It changes how you see the event | It's a neat detail |
| Honest resolution | The text delivers an answer or an honest limit | The hook needs a motive Scripture never gives |
| Natural experience | The feeling comes from the events | You'd have to tell people how to feel |
| Middle | Each beat causes the next | The beats could be shuffled |
| Share | "I'd send this to ___ because ___" | "It's cool" |
| Visible | We can show it with the art we have | It lives only in the narration |

## Selecting

- Offer at most three options, one line each. Lead with one recommendation.
- Reject a concept for a flaw in the concept, never because an early draft was weak.
- Don't favor a story because it's familiar, easy, hard to criticize or tied to the channel's name.
- An open question can lead a video. The script must say plainly what Scripture doesn't tell us.
- Search and comment data show interest, not quality. Use them to check that people care.
- For a blind reaction, use question mode in `tools/build_cold_review.py`.

## Discard

Drop it if it's only clever, needs a theology lesson to matter, depends on a word echo or a disputed reading, or interests a researcher more than a viewer.
