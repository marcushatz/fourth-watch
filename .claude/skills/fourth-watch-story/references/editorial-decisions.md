# Marcus's editorial decisions

This is the most valuable material in the skill. It's the record of what he approved, what he rejected and why. His newest words win.

## What he wants

- "Our mission is not to make viewers think, 'That was an interesting Bible fact.' Our mission is to make them think, 'I never realized that. That was incredible. I want to watch another.'"
- "A viewer should leave every Fourth Watch video having learned something meaningful AND experienced something worth remembering."
- "A simple story told exceptionally well can be more powerful than seven obscure biblical connections."
- "Our goal is to identify genuinely extraordinary stories and use those patterns to communicate them exceptionally well."
- "The key is to make the engine a better storyteller, not merely a more disciplined scriptwriter."

## Rejected, and the lesson

| Rejected | His words | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Storm v3, "They." | "The ending feels small because the insight is small." | Size of meaning decides, not elegance |
| Genesis script v1 | "an emotional insight should emerge from the events of the story, not interrupt them." | Experience first, then meaning. Move faster and go further |
| Experimental Serpent script | "decent, but it isn't as intellectually compelling as our earlier concepts" | "Don't mistake dramatic wording for a compelling story" |
| Storm drafts in general | "thinking like a biblical researcher trying to find an overlooked detail, rather than a storyteller" | Begin with stakes |
| Mary Magdalene recommendation | "distinguish a bad concept from a good concept that hasn't yet been executed well" | A weak draft isn't a weak story |
| Mary's hook | "Never promise a revelation Scripture doesn't provide." | The promise must be one the text can keep |
| Mary after "Mary." | "The emotional climax can itself be the ending." | Stop at the peak |
| Rejecting his storm question | "Biblical accuracy doesn't require avoiding interpretation. It requires distinguishing what Scripture says from what we're reasonably concluding." | Don't call avoidance accuracy |
| Serpent with many links | "sharing a Hebrew word doesn't prove an intentional connection between the passages." | A shared word never carries a climax |
| Bronze Serpent v1 | "cinematography cannot compensate for a script that has already finished making its point" | Don't keep going after the point is made |
| Serpent midpoint | "Once viewers understand the connection between the serpent and Jesus, they've received the main value. What reason do they have to stay?" | Every section needs a new reason |
| Fast retelling | "the storytelling is taking too much presence rather than giving value" | Retelling needs to change something |
| Esau and Jacob v6 | "i dont understand what the clear message is" | One clear message. Fix the spine, not the lines |
| Unforgivable sin drafts | "i stilll dont know if it provides enough value" | Answer the viewer's next questions |
| Samson and Jesus opposites | "a random connection" | Connections must be real |
| "Calling you back to Him" | Corny, and it assumes the viewer strayed | Never assume the viewer's spiritual state |
| Leah outlines | "weird way of talking" | Plain modern speech |
| The serpent story overall | "i do not think the serpent story appeals to emotion" | A clever connection isn't an experience |

## Older rules that changed

These conflict across the writer skill, `system/` guides and engine references. Only Marcus retires a rule. Everything not listed here still stands, and `standing-rules.md` lists what's in force.

| Older rule | Ruling here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every script is an investigation | The story chooses its form | Elijah works as a retelling, and Marcus asked for freedom |
| Must start with a question | Many openings work | Elijah and "Who is Jesus?" open on a person |
| Retelling stays under 15% | The rule stands: keep the famous story short. The exact number is dropped, and longer retelling must change something | Round 5, and his Nov 5 note on quotas |
| 190-245 words, or 75-95 seconds | No target. About 2 minutes if justified | Round 39, and his "duration earned" on Nov 5 |
| At least 3, 4 or 5 discoveries | No quota | Round 41 |
| Write 5 angles and score them | Up to three options, judged in words | Round 41 |
| One universal principle at the end | Only if the story earns it | The climax can be the end |
| The strongest discovery comes last (Round 38) | Usually, but the emotional climax can be the ending | Round 41 is newer |
| A hook can't ask an unanswered "why" | It can, if the script states the limit | Round 42 |
| AI judges and panels | Critics at most, never evidence | Round 41 |
| Value score of 3 or more in `fw_check.py` | Ignore for story judgment | It can't see story quality |

Two conflicts are still open for Marcus. They're at the end of `standing-rules.md`.
