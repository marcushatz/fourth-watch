# Preliminary walkthroughs

**UNTESTED.** These show how the draft skill would approach five different stories. No script has been written from them, and no viewer has seen them. Only Genesis 1:2 was checked against esv.org this session. Everything else is a paraphrase that needs checking against the ESV at script stage. They aren't approved topics.

## 1. Joseph reveals himself (Genesis 45)

- **Stakes:** the brothers who sold him stand before a ruler who can destroy them, and they don't know who he is. Joseph holds all the power and is hiding his grief.
- **Form:** dramatic scene. **Opening:** an action already happening: a powerful man clears the room.
- **Emotional path:** dread → confusion → shock → terror → forgiveness → release.
- **Understanding:** the viewer sees that revenge was fully available, which is what gives his mercy its weight.
- **Payoff:** climax as ending. Stop soon after he names himself and they can't answer.
- **Risk:** the story is long and needs one scene, not the whole saga. Don't invent his thoughts.

## 2. Mephibosheth at the king's table (2 Samuel 9)

- **Stakes:** a disabled grandson of the old king is summoned by the new king. In that world, he could expect to be killed.
- **Form:** portrait. **Opening:** a hard moment: a man who can't walk is told the king wants him.
- **Emotional path:** fear → disbelief → relief → belonging.
- **Understanding:** David's kindness comes from a promise to Jonathan, so mercy is grounded in loyalty, not sentiment.
- **Payoff:** a quiet final image with real weight: he eats at the king's table, and his feet are hidden under it. The hidden feet are our image and need labeling.
- **Risk:** don't turn it into an unmarked picture of salvation. "Could expect to be killed" is an inference from the period.

## 3. Hannah (1 Samuel 1)

- **Stakes:** childless, taunted year after year, she prays so intensely that the priest thinks she's drunk.
- **Form:** dramatic scene. **Opening:** a person in trouble: a woman mistaken for a drunk at the worst moment of her life.
- **Emotional path:** humiliation → desperation → being heard → peace → costly joy.
- **Understanding:** she gives back the very son she begged for, which changes what the prayer meant.
- **Payoff:** role change or reframe, ending when she hands him over.
- **Risk:** this is high-risk for anyone facing infertility. The script can't imply that prayer guarantees a child. Needs a pastoral read.

## 4. The earth before the first light (Genesis 1)

- **Stakes:** no human character. The value is wonder and meaning.
- **Form:** visual explainer. **Opening:** an extraordinary moment: before light, before land, the text describes darkness and deep water.
- **Emotional path:** awe → unease at the emptiness → wonder as order and life appear → peace.
- **Understanding:** the text starts with "without form and void," then forms and fills, so creation is shown as making a home. The form-and-fill reading is widely held but is still a reading.
- **Payoff:** answer plus deeper truth: before the first light, "the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters."
- **Risk:** stay out of arguments about the earth's age and how creation worked. Lintel has a Genesis 1 recital, so ours needs its own question and thread.

## 5. Why did God strike Uzzah? (2 Samuel 6)

- **Stakes:** a man reaches out to steady the ark, and he dies. It looks deeply unfair, and David himself is angry and afraid.
- **Form:** investigation. **Opening:** a hard question people may already have.
- **Emotional path:** shock → anger → fear → sober understanding → reverence.
- **Understanding:** the text shows the ark being carried on a cart, against the law's instructions, so the disaster begins earlier than the moment Uzzah reaches out. The connection to the carrying law is an inference.
- **Payoff:** honest open ending. Say what the text shows, and admit what stays hard.
- **Risk:** the easy answer sounds like blaming the victim. Don't explain away David's anger.

## What these show

Five stories produced five different forms, openings and payoffs, and the feeling came out of each passage. Whether they lead to better scripts is unknown.
