# Visual storytelling research

Prepared Nov 6, 2026, for the Fourth Watch style exploration.

**How to read the numbers.** Subscriber and view figures are channel totals from third-party trackers or articles, with the date shown. They measure a whole channel, not a visual style. Nothing here proves a style causes higher retention. Long-form results don't automatically carry over to Reels or Shorts.

**Evidence labels.** *Verified* means the figure came from a source I opened. *Reported* means one article said so and I couldn't check it further. *Judgment* means my own reading. *Not found* means I couldn't confirm a figure.

## What we learned

1. **No style owns this format.** The biggest short-form figure here, Zack D. Films, uses rendered 3D. Large long-form channels use flat stick figures, vector art, 3D and simple ink. Script, pace and consistency sit alongside style in all of them, so style alone can't be credited.
2. **Audiences accept simple art when the idea is strong.** History Matters, After Skool and Alan Becker reach very large audiences with modest drawing. This is a warning against spending all our effort on polish.
3. **Serious biblical emotion has worked in animation at scale.** *The King of Kings* earned an A+ CinemaScore and $83.5M worldwide. It's a feature film, so its result says little about Reels.
4. **BibleProject varies its style from video to video.** Jon Collins says style follows the topic, emotional topics need more characters and acting, and a shared script tone holds the videos together. One film need not bind the whole brand.
5. **Distinctive looks often come from one strict rule.** Obra Dinn uses 1-bit dithering, Cartoon Saloon keeps its pencil and woodcut line, and Arcane paints over 3D. Each decided what to leave out.
6. **Low-effort AI Bible content already fills the feed.** That raises the value of a look that clearly isn't generic, and it also shows that audiences will watch Bible content in imperfect art.

## What this means for the choice

- Audience evidence can't choose the winner. It only rules out assuming that any one look is proven.
- The strongest case for a distinctive look is brand memory, which is a judgment rather than a measurement.
- The real test is still strangers watching our frames, then a short motion test.
